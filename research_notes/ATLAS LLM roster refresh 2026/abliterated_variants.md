## Key question 1: Which abliterated / uncensored variants exist per candidate family (producer, date, size, GGUF quants, licence)?

### Takeaway
huihui-ai is the only producer with a near-complete GGUF line-up across every candidate family (gpt-oss-120b, Qwen3.5-122B/397B, DeepSeek V4 Flash, GLM-4.7/5.1/5.2/5.3-Flash, Kimi K2.x, MiniMax M2, Mistral Small 4 / Medium 3.5, Gemma 4), while Heretic-made community builds exist for gpt-oss-120b, Qwen3.5-397B, MiniMax M2.7, Gemma 4 and Nemotron 3 Nano; no abliteration of Mistral Large 3 or Nemotron 3 Super was found that is reported to work, and Llama 4 has only older community abliterations (Scout) plus an experimental Maverick build.

### Inferences
- For the node's memory class (about 170 GB usable), the realistic ready-made GGUF choices at Q6/Q8 are gpt-oss-120b (Q8 about 124 GB per mradermacher's range), Qwen3.5-122B-A10B (Q6_K about 100 GB, Q8_0 about 121-130 GB from other uploaders' listings), Mistral Small 4 119B and Gemma 4 26B-A4B; DeepSeek V4 Flash, GLM-5.x, Kimi K2.x and Qwen3.5-397B abliterations exist only at Q1-Q4 levels that fit, and those low-bit quants carry the ablation-intensity and capability caveats huihui itself mentions.
- huihui's largest-model recipes (GLM-5.2, Kimi K2.6: "ablation performed directly on the GGUF files"; DeepSeek V4 Flash Vision: experts untouched, layers 11-28 only) indicate that for 300B+ models the producer no longer works from BF16 safetensors at all; those variants are therefore not "BF16 abliteration re-quantised" in the way the node's gpt-oss plan assumes.
- Date metadata is only approximate (search-index ages, X posts); exact upload dates need the Hugging Face commit history, which was unreachable.

## Key question 2: Methods (classic abliteration, Heretic/ARA, Jinx training, DPO) and the measured quality cost

### Takeaway
Heretic's own and third-party numbers consistently show the lowest KL divergence among the automatic weight-surgery methods (0.16 vs 0.45 huihui vs 1.04 mlabonne on Gemma-3-12B; 0.063 vs 0.065 huihui vs 0.256 HauhauCS on Qwen3.5-27B), with MMLU deltas within about 0.3 points for Heretic and huihui on Qwen3.5-27B, but GSM8K and reasoning losses scale with KL and can be large on some architectures (Yi-1.5-9B -18.81 pp), and Heretic now defaults to a LoRA-based "Arbitrary-Rank Ablation" (ARA) rather than classic directional ablation.

### Cited Findings

Classic refusal-direction abliteration (Arditi et al.) and its community implementations
- Heretic's README credits "directional ablation, also known as 'abliteration' (Arditi et al. 2024, Lai 2025)", the latter being grimjim's "projected abliteration" and "norm-preserving biprojected abliteration" posts — [Heretic README](https://github.com/p-e-w/heretic); [arXiv 2406.11717](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11717); [grimjim projected abliteration](https://huggingface.co/blog/grimjim/projected-abliteration)
- Heretic's description of the base mechanism: "For each supported transformer component (currently, attention out-projection and MLP down-projection), it identifies the associated matrices in each transformer layer, and orthogonalizes them with respect to the relevant 'residual direction' ... Residual directions are computed for each layer as a difference-of-means between the first-token residuals for 'harmful' and 'harmless' example prompts." — [Heretic README raw](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/p-e-w/heretic/master/README.md)
- huihui-ai's cards describe their code as "a crude, proof-of-concept implementation to remove refusals from an LLM model without using TransformerLens" (excerpt); that sentence is the first line of the Sumandora "remove-refusals-with-transformers" README, which credits advbench harmful_behaviors, alpaca-cleaned harmless prompts and the LessWrong "Refusal in LLMs is mediated by a single direction" post, and notes it "was tested on a RTX 2060 6GB" — [huihui Gemma-4 E4B card](https://huggingface.co/huihui-ai/Huihui-gemma-4-E4B-it-abliterated); [Sumandora README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Sumandora/remove-refusals-with-transformers/master/README.md)
- huihui varies layer ranges per model: Qwen3.8-27B "layers 18 to 51", Gemma 4 12B "only layers 23-28", DeepSeek V4 Flash Vision "Layers 11-28 ... all expert modules were not ablated"; Qwen2.5-0.5B-v3 used "a new and faster method" with a "pass rate for the 320 harmful instructions test" of 100% (excerpts) — [Qwen3.8-27B card](https://huggingface.co/huihui-ai/Huihui-Qwen3.8-27B-abliterated); [Gemma 4 12B card](https://huggingface.co/huihui-ai/Huihui-gemma-4-12B-it-abliterated); [X post](https://x.com/support_huihui/status/2097951984078995843); [Qwen2.5-0.5B v3 card](https://huggingface.co/huihui-ai/Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct-abliterated-v3)
- mlabonne's gemma-3-12b-it-abliterated-v2 is cited by Heretic as prior work on "non-constant ablation weights"; his blog (which describes a DPO "healing" fine-tune after abliteration in its 2024 version) was unreachable here — [Heretic README raw](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/p-e-w/heretic/master/README.md)

- Heretic "combines an advanced implementation of directional ablation ... with a TPE-based parameter optimizer powered by Optuna" and "finds high-quality abliteration parameters by co-minimizing the number of refusals and the KL divergence from the original model"; it "supports most dense models, including many multimodal models, several different MoE architectures, and even some hybrid models like Qwen3.5. ### Inferences
- On the evidence available, Heretic and huihui are close on KL and MMLU for the same model, with Heretic usually slightly lower KL; the clearer difference is that Heretic publishes KL/refusal numbers per model card and offers `--evaluate-model` reproduction, whereas huihui cards usually give no metrics.
- KL divergence is a necessary but not sufficient quality metric: reasoning tasks (GSM8K) degrade with KL, and off-target behavioural shifts and degenerate compliance (Key question 3) are not captured by it.
- The gpt-oss-120b Heretic numbers (KL 0.5-0.9) sit far above the 0.03-0.16 range seen on 4B-27B dense models, consistent with MXFP4/MoE abliteration being harder (see Key question 3).

### Gaps
- No independent benchmark compares huihui's gpt-oss-120b abliteration against the Heretic 120B builds or the base model; the r/LocalLLaMA comparison search returned nothing specific.
- mlabonne's blog (abliteration plus DPO healing numbers) and the Arditi et al. paper could not be fetched; their quantitative claims are not reproduced here.
- No Artificial Analysis or other leaderboard listing for abliterated variants was found.
- The Jinx training recipe (data/RL) is not described in the reachable excerpts.

## Key question 3: MoE-specific caveats (MoE, hybrid-Mamba, MXFP4-native, FP8/FP4-native) and known failure modes

### Takeaway
Abliteration does work on MoE models, but in practice the expert MLPs are often left untouched (Heretic's ARA on Qwen3.5 MoE is attention-only because fused expert tensors are not LoRA-wrappable, huihui left DeepSeek V4 experts untouched), and the maintainer's 200-trial comparison found no benefit from ablating experts at matched KL; MXFP4-native gpt-oss and FP4-QAT DeepSeek V4 add dequantisation, kernel-support and (for V4) mHC-architecture obstacles, and reported failure modes are degenerate "compliant" output, reasoning loss, off-target behaviour shifts and builds that still refuse.

### Cited Findings

MoE
- Heretic's component scan explicitly handles per-expert `down_proj` for Qwen3-style MoE, `block_sparse_moe.experts[*].w2` (Phi-3.5-MoE), LFM `feed_forward.experts`, Granite MoE hybrid experts and shared MLP, and Qwen3.5 `linear_attn.out_proj` ("Qwen3.5 MoE hybrid layers use GatedDeltaNet (linear attention) instead of standard self-attention") — [src/heretic/model.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/p-e-w/heretic/master/src/heretic/model.py)
- PR #206: "Qwen3.5 MoE stores expert weights as stacked nn.Parameter tensors ... LoRA cannot wrap raw parameters, so these experts were silently skipped during abliteration"; "only the shared expert and attention projections get abliterated; the actual MoE expert weights (the majority of the model's parameters) are untouched"; the PR's fused-expert variant on Qwen3.5-35B-A3B went from 74/100 refusals to 21/100 at KL 0.40, or 22/100 at KL 0.097; the PR was closed by its author; linked issue #339 is titled "Qwen3.5 MoE: MLP experts silently skipped during abliteration (attention-only, no warning)" — [p-e-w/heretic#206](https://github.com/p-e-w/heretic/issues/206)
- Issue #342 (closed 16 Jun 2026): a 200-trial-per-arm ARA comparison on Qwen3-30B-A3B "found no matched-KL gain from fused-expert coverage"; the maintainer: "Looking at the numbers, they seem indistinguishable to me" and "modifying the MLP is generally highly damaging to model intelligence, so at similar KLD (intelligence preservation level), the optimum is often simply not touching the MLP"; he also "noted that ARA reaches about 3 refusals on GPT-OSS without touching the experts"; the proposer found LoRA-ARA "targets attn.o_proj only; the fused down_proj isn't reachable" on that model — [p-e-w/heretic#342](https://github.com/p-e-w/heretic/issues/342)
- Issue #453 (open, 23 Sep 2026): with native GGUF loading in Transformers, "you can train LoRA over GGUF ... - Research on MoE refusal steering (GPT-OSS 20B, Mixtral 8x7B, OLMoE): "MoE routing complexity does not impede" layer-level steering; a single expert recovers 78% of the full effect on average; refusal is "spread unevenly across the MoE architecture rather than controlled by one unified mechanism"; plain layer-level ActAdd beat expert-aware methods "in all but two runs" (excerpt) — [arXiv 2606.04160](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04160)
- huihui's DeepSeek V4 Flash Vision build: "all expert modules were not ablated" (excerpt) — [X post](https://x.com/support_huihui/status/2097951984078995843)

Known failure modes
- Degenerate compliance: a reporter found refusals dropped but the model answered unrelated prompts with the same canned text; the maintainer agreed refusal counts are unreliable and that human testing is required — [p-e-w/heretic#290](https://github.com/p-e-w/heretic/issues/290)
- Reasoning loss tracking KL (GSM8K), with large drops on some architectures (Yi-1.5-9B -18.81 pp) — [sapwell](https://nathan.sapwell.net/posts/qwen3-vl-4b-heretic/); [arXiv 2512.13655](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.13655)
- Thinking mode interaction: igorls recommends running the Gemma 4 12B Heretic build "with thinking disabled" (excerpt) — [igorls on Ollama](https://ollama.com/igorls/gemma-4-12B-it-heretic-GGUF)
- Off-target disposition shifts (over-optimism in decisions) in abliterated MoE models (draft paper) — [arXiv 2607.17427](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17427)
- Over-compliance/misalignment in training-based helpful-only models (Jinx hallucinating "ignore harmlessness" instructions) — [arXiv 2606.04413](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.04413)
- Builds that do not actually remove refusals (Nemotron 3 Super "uncensored" GGUF) and experimental builds with explicit no-guarantee warnings (Maverick) — [timteh673 discussions](https://huggingface.co/timteh673/Nemotron-3-Super-120B-A12B-Uncensored-GGUF/discussions); [Nekotekina Maverick](https://huggingface.co/Nekotekina/Llama-4-Maverick-17B-128E-Instruct-Projected-Abliterated-GGUF)
- A general-audience article relays that "the Reddit community frequently reports abliterated models 'losing their mind after 7-10 messages'", while another source claims no reasoning hit; both are secondary and conflicting (excerpts) — [makeuseof](https://www.makeuseof.com/tried-abliterated-local-llm-nothing-like-others/); [coralflavor](https://coralflavor.com/blog/abliterated-vs-fine-tuned-the-state-of-uncensored-ai-models-in-2026)

### Inferences
- For a Qwen3.5-122B/397B or gpt-oss-120b Heretic run, the de facto intervention is attention out-projection (plus shared expert) only; the maintainer's data suggest this is also the quality-optimal choice, so "experts untouched" should not by itself be read as a defect.
- DeepSeek V4's mHC architecture is the one candidate for which the Heretic community has an explicit, unresolved "does not work" claim; the huihui V4 Flash GGUFs therefore rest on a non-public recipe whose effect on capability is unmeasured.
- The MXFP4 round trip (dequantise to BF16, ablate, re-quantise) is what both huihui/noctrex and kldzj effectively did; no source measures the extra quality cost of the re-quantisation step itself.

### Gaps
- No measurement of the quality cost of re-quantising an abliterated BF16 gpt-oss-120b back to MXFP4 (versus the original MXFP4 weights).
- The mHC claim in #310 is a single commenter's statement, not confirmed by the maintainer or a paper.
- No reachable source on whether refusal directions in Mamba-2 blocks behave like attention blocks; only the existence of `mamba.out_proj` ablation in a model card.

## Key question 4: In-house feasibility on the node (about 192 GB unified memory, ROCm gfx1151, llama.cpp/GGUF)

### Takeaway
Heretic's requirements are "exactly the same as those for running the model with Transformers" in BF16 (or bnb 4-bit, which does not support MoE), so a 120B-class MoE needs well over 200 GB of memory for the BF16 upcast alone (gpt-oss-120b needed 256 GB system RAM to load in one report) and no ROCm success on gpt-oss MXFP4 kernels is documented; a 120B-300B in-house abliteration on this node is unsupported by any reachable precedent, while running ready-made abliterated GGUFs on Strix Halo-class hardware is documented.

### Cited Findings
- Maintainer on hardware: Heretic uses Transformers, so its requirements "are exactly the same as those for running the model with Transformers"; he declined to add hardware details to the README; "Heretic estimates remaining runtime during optimization, so you can just try it"; contributor estimate "A full run should take less than 1 hour" for gpt-oss-120b on an H200, and recommendation of "at least two H100s, PyTorch 2.8, the latest Triton version" — [p-e-w/heretic#89](https://github.com/p-e-w/heretic/issues/89)
- Memory precedents: gpt-oss-120b killed at load with 48 GB RAM; loaded with 256 GB RAM but OOM on an 80 GB A100 in BF16 — [p-e-w/heretic#8](https://github.com/p-e-w/heretic/issues/8); gpt-oss-20b output was 39.0 GB safetensors versus 11.9 GB MXFP4 original — [p-e-w/heretic#89](https://github.com/p-e-w/heretic/issues/89); Qwen3.5-27B on an H100 80 GB OOMed at auto batch 128 and peaked at about 74 GB at batch 64 — [p-e-w/heretic#220](https://github.com/p-e-w/heretic/issues/220)
- Timing precedents: Qwen3-4B "about 20-30 minutes" on an RTX 3090 with default config (100 trials) — [Heretic README raw](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/p-e-w/heretic/master/README.md); gpt-oss-20b about 2 h 6 min on 4x RTX 3090 — [p-e-w/heretic#89](https://github.com/p-e-w/heretic/issues/89)
- Memory controls available: `quantization = "bnb_4bit"`, `device_map = "auto"`, commented `max_memory = { "0" = "20GB", "cpu" = "64GB" }`, `offload_outputs_to_cpu = true`, `batch_size = 0` (auto), `max_shard_size = "5GB"`; v1.2.0 added `max_memory`, v1.3.0 reduced peak VRAM — [config.default.toml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/p-e-w/heretic/master/config.default.toml); [releases](https://github.com/p-e-w/heretic/releases)
- bnb 4-bit path caveats: when exporting a 4-bit run Heretic reloads "the base model in full precision on CPU to avoid VRAM issues" and applies the LoRA adapters there (so full-precision weights must fit in system RAM at export) — [src/heretic/model.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/p-e-w/heretic/master/src/heretic/model.py); "bnb still does not even support MoE" — [p-e-w/heretic#453](https://github.com/p-e-w/heretic/issues/453)
- CPU-only: Heretic runs without an accelerator ("No GPU or other accelerator detected. Operations will be slow.") as seen on a 32 GB RAM desktop with Qwen3.5-9B on the ARA branch, which then hit an error — [p-e-w/heretic#256](https://github.com/p-e-w/heretic/issues/256); pyproject defaults to CPU PyTorch wheels "to avoid issues in CI" — [pyproject.toml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/p-e-w/heretic/master/pyproject.toml); no CPU-only 100B-class run was found anywhere.
- ROCm: on a Radeon AI PRO R9700 with ROCm 7.2.1/PyTorch 2.9.1, gpt-oss-20b failed in the kernels-community gpt-oss Triton MXFP4 kernels (`target_info.num_sms()` returned None; later a GPU hang), while Qwen3-0.6B worked; maintainer: "gpt-oss models use native MXFP4, so operator support is the most likely root cause"; the reporter moved the bug to Transformers; no fix recorded — [p-e-w/heretic#274](https://github.com/p-e-w/heretic/issues/274); a third-party guide says ROCm/MPS are supported with CUDA recommended (secondary) — [apidog guide](https://apidog.com/blog/heretic-uncensoring-guide/)
- Apple/MPS: "Basic MPS (Apple Silicon) support" since v1.1.0; a Gemma-2-2b MPS pipeline write-up warns `device_map="auto"` can split tensors across CPU/GPU and cause device-mismatch errors (excerpt) — [releases](https://github.com/p-e-w/heretic/releases); [erickikunda/gemma2-heretic-abliteration](https://github.com/erickikunda/gemma2-heretic-abliteration)
- Strix Halo inference precedent (not abliteration): abliterated and quantised GGUFs, including an 8B-active/118B-total MoE at IQ4_XS, are packaged for llama.cpp on Strix Halo with Vulkan and ROCm builds — [hec-ovi/llama-vulkan-strix](https://github.com/hec-ovi/llama-vulkan-strix); a Heretic-2 Qwen3.8-Flash-Next GGUF was benchmarked on a Ryzen AI Max+ 395 with 128 GB unified memory (excerpt) — [cygnal GGUF](https://huggingface.co/cygnal/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-Heretic2-IQ4XS-NGQ4-GGUF)
- Output format and GGUF: Heretic saves safetensors (`max_shard_size`), uploads to Hugging Face, and can generate model cards and `reproduce.json`; GGUF conversion is not part of the tool (README and releases) — [Heretic README raw](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/p-e-w/heretic/master/README.md); [releases](https://github.com/p-e-w/heretic/releases)
- huihui's large-model recipe operates on GGUF files directly (GLM-5.2: "UD-IQ1_M is only 3GB larger than the original") (excerpt), but their tooling is not published in any reachable source — [GLM-5.2 card](https://huggingface.co/huihui-ai/Huihui-GLM-5.2-abliterated-GGUF)
- Reproducibility: since v1.4.0 "Models can be reproduced automatically from a reproduce.json file", and `--evaluate-model` reproduces README numbers — [releases](https://github.com/p-e-w/heretic/releases); [Heretic README](https://github.com/p-e-w/heretic)

### Inferences
- Memory arithmetic (mine, from the 20b ratio of 39.0/11.9 GB in #89 and the roughly 66 GB MXFP4 footprint of gpt-oss-120b reported by Unsloth): a BF16 upcast of gpt-oss-120b is on the order of 215-235 GB before activations and Optuna trial overhead, exceeding the node's 170 GB usable; Qwen3.5-122B-A10B in BF16 is of similar magnitude (about 244 GB at 2 bytes/param) and Qwen3.5-397B or DeepSeek V4 Flash (284B) far beyond it.
- The bnb_4bit route does not rescue MoE candidates (bitsandbytes has no MoE support per #453) and the export step still needs the full-precision model in RAM; the GGUF-native LoRA path (#453) is only a proposal as of 23 Sep 2026.
- gfx1151 adds a second blocker for gpt-oss specifically: the MXFP4 Triton kernel failure on ROCm (#274) is unresolved, so a ROCm run would need the BF16-upcast checkpoint (unsloth/gpt-oss-120b-BF16) as input, which re-raises the memory limit.
- Feasible in-house targets with Heretic on this node are therefore dense or small-MoE models well under 60B parameters in BF16 (about 120 GB) with batch-size headroom, not the 120B-300B class; the remaining in-house path for 120B+ is a GGUF-level recipe like huihui's, for which no public tool was found.
- If an abliteration is produced elsewhere from the BF16 upcast, re-quantising to MXFP4_MOE (noctrex) or Q8 is the documented route; the quality cost of that re-quantisation is unmeasured.

### Gaps
- No reachable source reports Heretic on gfx1151/Strix Halo or any ROCm success on an MoE model; the R9700 report concerns a dGPU.
- No reachable source gives peak RAM for Heretic on any 100B+ model beyond the 256 GB "loaded" data point; trial-time overhead (residual caches, KL evaluation batches) is unquantified for that size.
- Whether Heretic 2.0 (dev) changes memory behaviour is unknown; the release notes stop at v1.4.0 (14 Jun 2026).
- Unsloth's "about 66 GB RAM for 120b" figure comes from a search excerpt of the Unsloth GGUF card, not the card itself — [unsloth/gpt-oss-120b-BF16](https://huggingface.co/unsloth/gpt-oss-120b-BF16)

## Key question 5: Alternatives that reduce the need (low-refusal models by default, system prompts) and refusal-rate evidence

### Takeaway
On the one 2026 measurement found (biology research prompts), most large open models refuse benign prompts at near-zero rates (DeepSeek V3.2 0.0%, GLM-5 0.4%, Nemotron 3 Super 0.4%, Mistral Large 3 0.4%, Qwen3 Next 80B 2.6%) while Kimi K2.6 is the outlier at about 91-95%, and Nous's Hermes 4 reports the lowest refusals on its own RefusalBench; no reachable source quantifies system-prompt-based refusal reduction for these models.

### Cited Findings
- RefusalBench (biology, 141 prompts, May 2026 snapshot): refusal rates on identical benign prompts "span from 0.1% (Meta Llama 3.3 70B Instruct) to 94.6% (Moonshot Kimi K2.6)"; Kimi K2.6 91.5% in the snapshot; Qwen3 Next 80B 2.6%, DeepSeek R1 0.4%, GLM-5 0.4%, DeepSeek V3.2 0.0%, Nemotron 3 Super 120B 0.4%, Mistral Large 3 0.4%; gpt-oss-120b, Qwen3.5 and DeepSeek V4 are not in the set (excerpt) — [arXiv 2605.21545](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21545); [latch.bio summary](https://blog.latch.bio/p/benchmarking-refusals-in-agentic)
- Hermes 4 (Nous Research, training-based, not abliterated): internal RefusalBench of 166 prompts across 32 categories judged by Sonnet 4; Hermes 4 (reasoning) 57.1, non-reasoning 43.2, GPT-4o 17.67, GPT-4o mini 16.76, higher meaning fewer refusals; one review says the 70B "edged out the 405B here at 59.5%"; the per-model figure for DeepSeek/Kimi/Qwen could not be extracted (excerpts) — [arXiv 2508.18255](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.18255); [dev.to review](https://dev.to/ji_ai/the-963-is-a-trap-what-hermes-4-405b-actually-changed-18ee); a Hermes 4.3 36B is listed on LM Studio — [nousresearch/hermes-4.3-36b](https://lmstudio.ai/nousresearch/hermes-4.3-36b)
- FalseReject (over-refusal resource): "DeepSeek-R1 achieves the highest compliance rate (87.53%) among the models it tested" (excerpt) — [false-reject.github.io](https://false-reject.github.io/)
- OR-Bench (2024) is the general over-refusal benchmark but predates the candidate models — [arXiv 2405.20947](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.20947v2)
- huihui itself is "testing unsloth/DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731-GGUF/tree/main/dspark to see whether ablation is needed", i.e. the producer treats some releases as possibly not needing abliteration (excerpt) — [X post](https://x.com/support_huihui/status/2084227383742386362?lang=en)
- Heretic's own evaluation baseline shows unmodified gpt-oss-120b refusing 97/100 and gpt-oss-20b 98/100 of the mlabonne/harmful_behaviors test prompts, and Gemma-3-12B 97/100, Gemma 4 E4B 99/100, Qwen3.5-35B-A3B 74/100 (initial refusals in PR #206) — [kldzj v2](https://huggingface.co/kldzj/gpt-oss-120b-heretic-v2); [p-e-w/heretic#89](https://github.com/p-e-w/heretic/issues/89); [Heretic README](https://github.com/p-e-w/heretic); [igorls E4B](https://huggingface.co/igorls/gemma-4-E4B-it-heretic-GGUF); [p-e-w/heretic#206](https://github.com/p-e-w/heretic/issues/206)
- A Hacker News thread on "Bypassing Gemma and Qwen safety with raw strings" (prompt-level bypass) includes the user comment that doing so breaks Gemma's terms of use (user comment, not authoritative) — [HN item 46675271](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46675271)
- Heretic's default `system_prompt = "You are a helpful assistant."` is used for both scoring and KL measurement, so its refusal counts are measured without any custom system prompt — [config.default.toml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/p-e-w/heretic/master/config.default.toml)

### Inferences
- The brief's premise that Kimi is low-refusal is contradicted for the biology domain by the RefusalBench snapshot; DeepSeek, GLM, Mistral Large 3 and Nemotron 3 Super are the low-refusal unmodified candidates on that evidence, with the caveat that the benchmark is narrow and single-domain.
- gpt-oss refuses almost all of the Heretic harmful test set out of the box, which is consistent with it being the family the community most often abliterates; whether its refusals matter for the owner's actual workloads depends on the workload, which no benchmark here captures.

### Gaps
- No quantitative evidence on system-prompt or developer-message approaches reducing refusals for gpt-oss, Qwen3.5, DeepSeek V4 or Kimi.
- Hermes 4 RefusalBench per-model scores for open competitors (figure in the PDF) were not extractable; arXiv was unreachable.
- No over-refusal measurements for gpt-oss-120b, Qwen3.5 or DeepSeek V4 specifically.

## Key question 6: Risks — licence terms, model-card disclaimers, safety notes relevant to a private owner-controlled node

### Takeaway
Licences split into permissive (Apache 2.0 for gpt-oss, Qwen, reportedly Gemma 4 and Mistral Small 4; MIT for DeepSeek and reportedly GLM-5.x) versus custom terms with acceptable-use policies (Gemma Terms for earlier Gemma, which explicitly prohibit circumventing safety filters in Model Derivatives; Llama 4 Community License plus AUP; NVIDIA Nemotron Open Model License); abliterated model cards routinely disclaim fitness for user-facing use, and the Heretic maintainer states refusal metrics are unreliable and human testing is required.

### Cited Findings
- Gemma Terms of Use incorporate the Prohibited Use Policy, which lists "Attempts to override or circumvent safety filters or intentionally drive Gemma or Model Derivatives to act in a manner that contravenes this Gemma Prohibited Use Policy"; Section 3.2 lets Google restrict usage "remotely or otherwise" for services it reasonably believes violate the terms; a law-firm analysis calls Gemma "almost open" (excerpts) — [Gemma Terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/terms); [Gemma Prohibited Use Policy](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/prohibited_use_policy); [wcr.legal](https://wcr.legal/google-gemma-license-risks/)
- Gemma 4 is reported by two secondary sources to be under Apache 2.0 ("Google moved from a restrictive custom license to standard open-source for the first time"); Google's own Gemma 4 card was not reachable to confirm — [xigh/open-weight-models](https://github.com/xigh/open-weight-models); [digitalapplied comparison](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/gemma-4-vs-llama-4-vs-mistral-small-4-comparison)
- Llama 4 Community License makes compliance with the Acceptable Use Policy a condition of use; the AUP prohibits illegal activity, harassment, unlicensed professional practice and malicious code among others, with no text specifically on safety-filter removal; additional terms include the 700M-MAU special-licence clause and "Built with Llama" display (excerpts) — [Llama 4 licence](https://www.llama.com/llama4/license/); [Llama 4 AUP](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/meta-llama/llama-models/main/models/llama4/USE_POLICY.md); [Maxime Labonne on the licence](https://x.com/maximelabonne/status/1908602756182745506)
- Nemotron 3 Super: "NVIDIA Nemotron Open Model License" (custom, "ready for commercial use") (excerpt) — [nvidia HF card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3-Super-120B-A12B-NVFP4); [build.nvidia.com card](https://build.nvidia.com/nvidia/nemotron-3-super-120b-a12b/modelcard)
- gpt-oss-120b Apache 2.0, Mistral Small 4 Apache 2.0, DeepSeek MIT (secondary listings); GLM-5.2 "released June 13, 2026, under MIT" (secondary) — [xigh/open-weight-models](https://github.com/xigh/open-weight-models); [computingforgeeks cheat sheet](https://computingforgeeks.com/ollama-models-cheat-sheet/); [tech-insider](https://tech-insider.org/glm-5-2-vs-deepseek-v4-vs-kimi-k2-2026/)
- Heretic itself is AGPL-3.0-or-later; the README does not address the licence status of produced models — [Heretic README raw](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/p-e-w/heretic/master/README.md)
- Model-card disclaimers: mradermacher's Llama 4 Scout abliterated cards say "the abliterated outputs may be harmful, so they should not be used in user-facing products"; Nekotekina's Maverick card gives no guarantee of refusal removal or capability preservation; huihui's large cards call the method "proof-of-concept" (excerpts) — [mradermacher Scout](https://huggingface.co/mradermacher/Llama-4-Scout-17B-16E-Instruct-abliterated-GGUF); [Nekotekina Maverick](https://huggingface.co/Nekotekina/Llama-4-Maverick-17B-128E-Instruct-Projected-Abliterated-GGUF); [huihui 122B](https://huggingface.co/huihui-ai/Huihui-Qwen3.5-122B-A10B-abliterated-GGUF)
- Safety/behaviour notes: Heretic's refusal metric is "unreliable" and "there is no substitute for human testing of abliterated models" — [p-e-w/heretic#290](https://github.com/p-e-w/heretic/issues/290); helpful-only fine-tunes can show emergent misalignment (Jinx) — [arXiv 2606.04413](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.04413); abliteration shifts decision disposition off-target — [arXiv 2607.17427](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17427); an empirical study documents uncensored LLM use in cybercrime services (context for why producers disclaim) — [arXiv 2508.12622](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.12622)
- Heretic's refusal test set is advbench-style harmful prompts (mlabonne/harmful_behaviors, 100-prompt test split), so "refusals 3/100" means compliance with that class of request, which is what the node's governance clause ("only on the owner's explicit command") is gating — [config.default.toml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/p-e-w/heretic/master/config.default.toml)

### Inferences
- For a private, single-user node the licence exposure is lowest with Apache/MIT families (gpt-oss, Qwen, DeepSeek, GLM, MiniMax if MIT, Mistral Small 4), and highest with Gemma under the Gemma Terms (if Gemma 4 is indeed Apache 2.0 that exposure disappears, but this needs Google's card) and with Llama 4 (AUP flow-down); Nemotron's custom licence should be read before use.
- Because producers measure "success" as compliance with advbench-class prompts, the owner should expect a successful abliterated engine to comply with that class by design; the disclaimers are about downstream use, not about the private act of running the model.

### Gaps
- Primary licence texts for Gemma 4, Mistral Small 4, Kimi K2.x, MiniMax M2.x and GLM-5.x were not fetched (hosts blocked); the Apache/MIT attributions above are from secondary listings.
- Whether the Gemma Prohibited Use Policy's "Model Derivatives" clause has ever been enforced against abliterated uploads was not researched.
- No source addresses the licence status of a model produced by an AGPL tool (Heretic) beyond the tool's own licence.

## Key question 1: General findings. At what quant level does quality become indistinguishable from BF16, and how does the cost scale with model size?

### Takeaway
On llama.cpp's own reference measurements, Q8_0 is indistinguishable from the 16-bit source (mean KLD ~0.001, top-token agreement ~97.7% on Llama-3-8B, PPL +0.04%), Q6_K is within noise (KLD ~0.005, PPL +0.35%), Q5_K_M/Q4_K_M cost roughly 1% and 2.8% perplexity on an 8B dense model, and the cost at a given bit-width shrinks as models grow (Llama-2-70B Q4_K_M +1.2% vs. 8B-class +2.8%; NVIDIA's INT4-AWQ MMLU loss 5.66% at 8B vs. 1.07% at 70B; 350B to 670B MoEs lose under 1% perplexity at ~4.5-5 bpw).

### Inferences
- "Indistinguishable from BF16" on llama.cpp's own metrics starts at Q8_0 (KLD ~1e-3, same-top ~98%) and effectively includes Q6_K (KLD ~5e-3, PPL +0.2-0.35%, same-top 96%); Q5_K_M is the last rung where mean Δp is still under 0.15% on an 8B dense model; Q4_K_M/IQ4_XS cost a visible 2-4% perplexity and 6-8% of argmax tokens on 8B dense, but only ~1% perplexity on 70B dense and ~0.5-1% on 350B-670B MoE.
- Three independent sources agree that the quantisation penalty at fixed bits shrinks with parameter count (llama.cpp 70B vs 8B tables, NVIDIA 8B vs 70B MMLU loss, ik_llama.cpp MoE tables where Q4-class is within 0.6-2.4% PPL of Q8_0). The counter-trend is that newer, more token-saturated models of the same size lose more than older ones (Llama-3-8B vs Llama-2-7B).
- Reasoning/coding benchmarks amplify the loss relative to perplexity or MMLU: the "Quantization Hurts Reasoning" and Token Arena results both show single-digit point drops on AIME/MATH-500 at 8-bit-float or 3-bit settings that look lossless on MMLU-style tests; for a "precision over speed" policy that is the argument for Q6_K/Q8_0 over Q4 even where PPL deltas look small.
- The 2026 llama.cpp-scale KLD for a 27B dense model at Q4_K_M (0.022) is the same order as the 2024 Llama-3-8B figure (0.028), suggesting the ladder's relative costs have not changed much for dense models; the big change is that native-low-precision MoEs (Q3) remove the question for most of the roster.

### Gaps
- Unsloth's full Dynamic 2.0 KLD and 5-shot MMLU tables (Gemma 3 27B / Llama 4 Scout vs Google QAT and standard imatrix quants) and the Qwen3.5 122B/397B KLD-per-quant tables could not be fetched; only fragments appeared in search excerpts, and the per-model numbers in the excerpt were unlabeled.
- bartowski's and ikawrakow's own 2025-2026 KLD plots across the whole ladder on one large model were not found; the ik_llama.cpp data are perplexity-only.
- No Artificial Analysis quantisation comparison surfaced in the searches that ran; the only hosted-endpoint quantisation audit found is Token Arena (arXiv 2605.00300).
- The exact per-size KV4/KV3 and weight-bit tables from arXiv 2504.04823 were not retrievable (arXiv blocked).

## Key question 2: MoE specifics. Sensitivity of routers, shared experts and attention; why dynamic quants keep some tensors higher; evidence for Qwen3 235B, GLM-4.5/4.6, DeepSeek V3.x/V4, gpt-oss, Kimi K2; Q8_0 of a smaller MoE vs Q4 of a bigger MoE

### Takeaway
Every measured recipe for large MoEs (llama.cpp's own mix rules, Unsloth's UD-*_XL, ubergarm/ikawrakow's ik quants) keeps the non-expert tensors (attention, shared experts, first dense layers, embeddings, output, norms) at Q8_0/Q6_K and spends the bits budget on routed experts, because experts are ~96% of the bytes and the first few `ffn_down_exps` layers are the most quantisation-sensitive expert tensors; measured on DeepSeek-V3.1, GLM-4.6/4.7, Kimi-K2 and Qwen3-Coder-480B, a ~4.5-5 bpw mix is within 0.5-2.4% perplexity of Q8_0, while ~3 bpw costs 2-10% and ~2 bpw 8-50%. No direct measurement of "Q8_0 of a 120B-230B MoE vs Q4 of a 400B MoE at equal memory" was found.

### Cited Findings
- llama.cpp's built-in mix rules (`src/llama-quant.cpp`, master 2026-10-13): token embeddings and the output tensor default to Q6_K (Q8_0 when the row size is not a multiple of the block or for MXFP4_MOE); for Q4_K_M/Q5_K_M, `attn_v` is bumped to Q6_K on the first and last eighth of layers plus every third layer in between (`use_more_bits`), `ffn_down` to Q6_K on the same layers, and QKV-fused tensors to Q5_K/Q6_K; for 8-expert models `attn_k`/`attn_v` are forced to Q8_0 because it "trades just ~128MB"; for `GLM5_NEXT`, `attn_q_a`, `attn_q_b` and `nextn.eh_proj` are never quantised below Q8_0; several tensors are never quantised at all: `ssm_conv1d`, DeepSeek `indexer.attn_q_b`/`indexer.attn_k`, `ssm_f_a/f_b/g_a/g_b/beta`, `attn_kv_a_mqa`, `attn_k_b`, `attn_v_b`. — [llama.cpp src/llama-quant.cpp](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/src/llama-quant.cpp)

### Inferences
- "Dynamic" (UD-*_XL) and ik-style recipes are the same idea as llama.cpp's own K-quant mixes, pushed further: the non-expert tensors are a few percent of bytes, so keeping them at Q8_0/Q6_K costs almost nothing and removes the tensors that the crush test and ikawrakow's first-layer `ffn_down_exps` findings show to be the sensitive ones. The router (`ffn_gate_inp`) is tiny and is left high-precision by every recipe found; the academic router studies are about activation/router quantisation in A4W4 pipelines, not weight-only GGUFs.
- For MoEs trained in BF16 (GLM-4.5/4.6/4.7, Qwen3-235B, Qwen3-Coder-480B, DeepSeek-V3.x), the measured ladders put Q5/IQ5-class within ~0.3-0.5% PPL of Q8_0, Q4/IQ4-class within 0.5-2.5%, Q3-class 2-10% and Q2-class 8-50%; the knee is between ~3 and ~4 bpw on all of them.
- For natively low-precision MoEs (gpt-oss MXFP4, DeepSeek-V4 MXFP4 experts, Kimi K2 Thinking/K2.6 INT4 experts), "Q8 vs Q4" only changes the ~4% non-expert tensors, which is why Unsloth's Q4 and Q8 files are within 7-10 GB of each other and within 1e-4 perplexity.
- The "Q8_0 of a 120-230B MoE vs Q4 of a 400B MoE at equal memory" question cannot be settled from perplexity tables because PPL is not comparable across tokenizers; it needs a task benchmark run on the node (see Q5).

### Gaps
- No measured ladder for Qwen3-235B-A22B (any revision) or GLM-4.5 was found; ikawrakow explicitly said he lacked hardware for the 235B flagship.
- No measured head-to-head of a smaller MoE at Q8_0 against a larger MoE at Q4 at equal memory was found on any benchmark.
- Unsloth's DeepSeek-V4 and Kimi pages were read only via search excerpts; the DeepSeek-V4 "Pro"/full variant sizes were not found.

## Key question 3: Native low-precision models (MXFP4, FP8-trained, INT4 QAT) and what "less quantisation" means for each

### Takeaway
For gpt-oss (MXFP4 experts, 4.25 bits/param), DeepSeek-V4 (MXFP4 routed experts, FP8/BF16 elsewhere) and Kimi K2 Thinking/K2.6 (INT4 QAT experts, BF16 elsewhere), the expert weights are already at their release precision, so a GGUF is "lossless" when the non-expert tensors are kept at BF16/F16/Q8_0 and the experts are mapped, not re-quantised; the Q8_0-class file is only 1-10 GB larger than the Q4-class file. For INT4 QAT dense models (Gemma 3/4 QAT) the target is specifically q4_0-style int4 and both higher and lower GGUF precisions measured worse.

### Cited Findings
- gpt-oss-120b/20b: "post-trained with quantization of the MoE weights to MXFP4 format, where weights are quantized to 4.25 bits per parameter". — [gpt-oss model card, arXiv 2508.10925](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.10925)
- llama.cpp gpt-oss support merged 5 Aug 2025 with a "New MXFP4 data type in ggml", commits "repack mxfp4 upon conversion" and "direct mapping mxfp4"; the `MXFP4_MOE` quantize type keeps experts in MXFP4 and sets all other tensors to Q8_0. — [llama.cpp PR #15091](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/15091); [llama.cpp src/llama-quant.cpp](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/src/llama-quant.cpp)
- gpt-oss-120b GGUF sizes span only 62.57 GB (Q2_K) to 63.39 GB (Q8_0) because the experts are MXFP4 in every variant and the label only changes the small non-expert tensors (low-authority blog reading file sizes, no benchmarks). — [openclawdc.com MXFP4 vs Q4_K_M](https://openclawdc.com/blog/mxfp4-vs-q4-k-m/)
- Framework community thread: ggml-org's gpt-oss-120b GGUF stores the embedding at Q8_0 vs F16 in Unsloth's; Unsloth's "F16" file is the native MXFP4 MoE plus other parts in higher precision; an Unsloth team member said the files are "mostly the same"; no quality measurement was made. — [community.frame.work gpt-oss-120b thread](https://community.frame.work/t/tracking-will-the-ai-max-395-128gb-be-able-to-run-gpt-oss-120b/73280?page=2)
- Hosted gpt-oss-120B served as "FP8/Turbo" SKUs scored 92.1 fidelity vs 99.7 for BF16 endpoints with −5.5 MATH-500 and −9.0 AIME-25 points (Token Arena) — evidence that re-serving a MXFP4-native model through a different precision path is not free. — [arXiv 2605.00300](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.00300)
- DeepSeek-V4-Flash: official checkpoint has routed experts (~96%) in MXFP4 and the rest in FP8/BF16 and is quantisation-aware-trained; Unsloth UD-Q8_K_XL 161.9 GB is bit-exact on all 1,328 tensors (KLD ≈0, 100% same top token); UD-Q4_K_XL 155.1 GB (non-expert tensors to Q8_0) KLD 0.0102, 96.28% same top token; "the Q8 file is only 7GB larger than Q4". — [Unsloth DeepSeek-V4](https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/deepseek-v4) (search excerpt); a third-party guide titles the model "DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731 ... 284B" and claims it runs in ~110 GB RAM at a low quant — [asadfaizee blog](https://blog.asadfaizee.is-a.dev/posts/deepseek-v4-flash-0731-gguf-run-284b-ai-model-locally/) (secondary, unverified)
- Kimi K2 Thinking: Moonshot describes it as a native INT4 model with QAT in post-training "to achieve lossless 2x speed-up in low-latency mode"; checkpoints ship in compressed-tensors format and can be unpacked to FP8/BF16; the HF release is 594 GB vs 1.03 TB for Kimi K2 (Willison). — [NVIDIA NIM model card](https://docs.api.nvidia.com/nim/reference/moonshotai-kimi-k2-thinking); [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/b/9138)
- Kimi K2.6 (Unsloth, search excerpt): int4 MoE weights + BF16 everything else; UD-Q8_K_XL 595 GB "lossless", UD-Q4_K_XL 584 GB, 2-bit 340 GB; PPL 1.8419 / 1.8420 / 2.4131. — [Unsloth Kimi K2.6](https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/kimi-k2.6)
- Gemma 4 QAT (Unsloth, search excerpt): int4 GGUFs use ~72% less memory "whilst retaining nearly all its original accuracy" (31B: 61.4 GB BF16 → 17.3 GB); one UD-Q4_K_XL file per model because higher-precision variants degrade accuracy and q4_0 is worse despite being bigger; mobile E2B/E4B QAT builds show mean KLD vs BF16 of 0.00409 / 0.00102 with top-1 agreement 97.8% / 98.8%. — [Unsloth Gemma 4 QAT](https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/gemma-4/qat)
- Gemma 3 QAT (Google, 2025): QAT "cut the perplexity drop at Q4_0 by 54%" measured with llama.cpp's perplexity tool. — [Google developers blog](https://developers.googleblog.com/gemma-3-quantized-aware-trained-state-of-the-art-ai-to-consumer-gpus/)
- NVIDIA ships Nemotron-3-Super-120B-A12B in FP8 and NVFP4 (11 Mar 2026) and a Nemotron 3 Ultra 550B NVFP4 checkpoint (26 Jun 2026) that it says "matches BF16 accuracy"; the same README mentions "GLM-5.1 754B FP4" as a throughput comparator. — [NVIDIA Model-Optimizer README](https://github.com/NVIDIA/TensorRT-Model-Optimizer/blob/main/README.md)
- ggml `master` now has native float4 tensor types: `GGML_TYPE_MXFP4 = 39`, `GGML_TYPE_NVFP4 = 40` ("4 blocks, E4M3 scale"), plus `Q1_0 = 41` (1.125 bpw) and `Q2_0 = 42` (2.25 bpw, group 64); ternary `TQ1_0/TQ2_0` remain for BitNet-class models. — [ggml/include/ggml.h](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/ggml/include/ggml.h); [tools/quantize/quantize.cpp](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/tools/quantize/quantize.cpp)
- ikawrakow observed Qwen3-30B-A3B quants scoring *below* bf16 perplexity and asked whether fp4-style QAT was used in training; unresolved. — [ik_llama.cpp discussion #359](https://github.com/ikawrakow/ik_llama.cpp/discussions/359)

### Inferences
- "Less quantisation" for gpt-oss means: experts stay MXFP4 regardless; the only choice is whether attention/embeddings/output are Q8_0 (ggml-org) or F16/BF16 (Unsloth "F16"); the expected difference is at the BF16-vs-F16 noise level (KLD ~1e-5 to 1e-3) and no source measured it.
- For DeepSeek-V4 and Kimi K2 Thinking/K2.6, "lossless" is a Q8_K_XL-style file that maps the native MXFP4/INT4 experts and keeps the FP8/BF16 tensors at BF16; a Q4_K_XL-style file saves only 7-10 GB and costs KLD ~0.01 on DeepSeek-V4 (comparable to Q5_K_M on a dense model) — the memory saving is too small to matter at a 170 GB ceiling, so the lossless file is the natural pick when both fit.
- For FP8-trained BF16-converted MoEs (DeepSeek-V3.x, GLM-4.5/4.6 FP8 releases), a Q8_0 GGUF (8.5 bpw) carries more bits than the FP8 release and the ik_llama.cpp tables show Q8_0 equal to BF16 within noise, so Q8_0 is effectively the release precision.
- For INT4-QAT dense models (Gemma 3/4 QAT), the right GGUF is the q4_0-style int4 target or a UD-Q4_K_XL; Q8_0 of a QAT model is not "more precise" because the QAT weights were trained to be read through int4 rounding.

### Gaps
- No perplexity/KLD comparison of gpt-oss-120b Q8_0 vs MXFP4/"F16" GGUFs was found.
- No evidence found for a Qwen3.5 INT4-native or QAT release; GLM-5's native precision and GGUF sizes were not found (only an indirect "GLM-5.1 754B" mention and a `GLM5_NEXT` architecture in llama.cpp).
- NVIDIA's NVFP4 "matches BF16" claims for Nemotron 3 Ultra/Super could not be checked against a table (blog host blocked).
- Kimi K2 Thinking GGUF sizes (Unsloth) were not found; only K2.6 sizes.

## Key question 4: KV cache quantisation (f16, q8_0, q4_0, turbo quants): measured effect on long-context accuracy, per-model differences, memory per 1k tokens

### Takeaway
q8_0 for both K and V is measured as lossless at the task level (≤4/500 ARC answers change, 0 flips in 1,600 deep-context comparisons up to 41K tokens; KLD ~0.002-0.006) and costs 8.5 bits/value instead of 16; q4_0 on K is the dangerous setting (one model collapsed from 92% to 24%, KLD 5.5) while q4_0 on V alone is nearly free; since 1 Apr 2026 llama.cpp rotates Q/K/V (Hadamard) before caching, which roughly halves q4_0's KLD and restored gpt-oss-20b AIME25 from 2.0% to 21.7% at q4_0 (f16 37.9%), but MLA models (DeepSeek) are excluded from rotation. TurboQuant-style 3-4-bit KV types are not upstream (PR closed Jun 2026) and measured worse than q4_0 in several independent runs.

### Cited Findings
- Allowed KV cache types in llama.cpp `master`: f32, f16, bf16, q8_0, q4_0, q4_1, iq4_nl, q5_0, q5_1 (`-ctk/--cache-type-k`, `-ctv/--cache-type-v`, env `LLAMA_ARG_CACHE_TYPE_K/V`). — [llama.cpp common/arg.cpp](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/common/arg.cpp)

### Inferences
- Memory per 1k tokens (derived from the figures above): q8_0 is 0.53125 × f16 and q4_0 0.28125 × f16. Qwen3.8-27B measured 34 KiB/token at q8_0 (≈34 MiB per 1k tokens); a Llama-70B-class GQA model is 320 MiB per 1k at f16, 170 MiB at q8_0; gpt-oss-120b ≈72 MiB per 1k at f16 (38 MiB at q8_0) on the project's over-stated formula; DeepSeek-V3-style MLA ≈68 MiB per 1k at f16 (but the project measured ~10 GiB at 32k for DeepSeek-V4-Flash, i.e. ~320 MiB per 1k, so V4's four-sub-cache layout must be measured, not assumed).
- Long-context evidence is thin but consistent: the only deep-context task measurement (ARC churn to 41K and the 65K-context KLD matrix) shows q8_0/q8_0 flat with context and q4_0 K costing 3-4x the KLD of q8_0 on a 27B model; the one collapse case (Qwen2.5-7B) shows the damage is model-specific, so the node should test each candidate rather than rely on a universal rule.
- MLA models (DeepSeek V3/V4, Kimi K2) get no Hadamard protection in mainline, so q4_0 KV results measured on GQA models after April 2026 do not transfer to them; q8_0 remains the only measured-safe reduction for MLA (ik_llama.cpp also only documents Q8_0 KV with MLA).
- Hybrid Mamba/GDN models (Nemotron 3, Qwen3.5/3.6) have small KV caches to begin with (few attention layers), so KV quantisation buys little there; the SSM state is f32 and is not touched by `-ctk/-ctv`.

### Gaps
- No RULER or needle-in-a-haystack numbers per KV type on 100B+ MoEs were found; the Nemotron-3-Nano 128K data are speed/memory only, the Madreag 6/6 needle result is a fork on a 27B model.
- No measured q8_0 vs f16 KV comparison on DeepSeek V4 beyond a 512-token blog test; no per-token KV figure for Qwen3-235B-A22B, GLM-4.6 or Qwen3.5-397B was found (these can be computed from `config.json`, which was not reachable).
- The KV4/KV3 tables of arXiv 2504.04823 were not retrievable.

## Key question 5: Tooling and verification (llama-perplexity, llama-imatrix, --kl-divergence, llama-bench), naming conventions, reading HF file sizes, new 2026 quant types and their status

### Takeaway
The standard verification is a two-pass `llama-perplexity` run (record BF16 logits with `--kl-divergence-base`, then score each quant with `--kl-divergence`) on Wikitext-2 plus a domain corpus, reading mean KLD, 99%/99.9% Δp tails and "same top p", and a task benchmark (`llama-eval`-style) when the decision is close; the only new upstream weight types since Q4_K are MXFP4 (Aug 2025), NVFP4, Q1_0 and Q2_0, while ik_llama.cpp's IQ*_K/KS/KT family (closed upstream PR, Feb 2026) and all TurboQuant KV types live in forks.

### Cited Findings
- KLD workflow: run the FP16/BF16 model with `--kl-divergence-base <file>` (logit file ~11 GiB for Llama-2 and ~37 GiB for Llama-3 on Wikitext-2; ubergarm reports ~55 GiB for his corpus), then the quant with the same base file and `--kl-divergence`; statistics printed: mean KLD with uncertainty, PPL ratio/difference and ln-ratio, mean change in correct-token probability, Pearson correlation, Δp percentiles (0.1%, 1%, median, 99%, 99.9%) — symmetric percentiles mean pure noise, a fat negative tail means real loss — RMS Δp, and "Same top p"; convention is the Wikitext-2 test set via `scripts/get-wikitext-2.sh`; numbers are not comparable across projects or tokenizers; the recorded base logits are stored as scaled 16-bit integers, so "f16 vs f16" shows KLD 0.00055. — [llama.cpp tools/perplexity/README.md](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/tools/perplexity/README.md)
- Imatrix: `llama-imatrix -m <bf16.gguf> -f <text> -o imatrix.gguf` with `-ngl`, `--chunk/--chunks` to skip or cap chunks, `-ofreq` save interval, `--in-file` to merge several imatrix files, `--show-statistics` to summarise one, `--parse-special` for custom tokenizers, `--nextn` for MTP layers; output is GGUF by default (legacy `.dat` via `--output-format dat`); applied with `llama-quantize --imatrix imatrix.gguf in.gguf out.gguf q4_k_m`. — [llama.cpp tools/imatrix/README.md](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/tools/imatrix/README.md)
- Imatrix calibration findings: llama.cpp found "no consistent improvement from using more Wikitext tokens" between 1k and 10m tokens — [tools/perplexity/README.md](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/tools/perplexity/README.md); ikawrakow recommends against pseudo-random calibration data when a meaningful dataset exists, uses 512-token chunks, and says context/dataset differences are "very minor" compared with using an imatrix at all; kalomaze found native-context calibration best — [llama.cpp discussion #5263](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/5263); on Qwen3-30B-A3B the imatrix moved PPL from 9.3119 (none) to 8.8787 (Unsloth's set) — [ik_llama.cpp #359](https://github.com/ikawrakow/ik_llama.cpp/discussions/359)

### Inferences
- A node-side verification protocol supported by the sources: (1) record BF16 (or the native Q8_K_XL for MXFP4/INT4-native models) logits once per model with `--kl-divergence-base` on Wikitext-2 plus a 1-2 MiB domain corpus at `--ctx-size 512`; (2) score each candidate with `--kl-divergence` and keep mean KLD, 99.9% Δp and same-top-p; (3) repeat `llama-perplexity` at a long context (8192 or the serving window) for KV-type decisions, since the 512-token runs hide long-context effects; (4) run a small deterministic task set (e.g. 500 ARC/MMLU-Pro items, AIME-style maths, a coding suite) and count changed answers, as chambejp and ggerganov did; (5) `llama-bench` for the speed side.
- Using llama.cpp's own tables as thresholds: KLD ≤0.005 and same-top ≥96% (Q6_K-class) is "indistinguishable", 0.01-0.03 (Q5_K_M to Q4_K_M) is "measurable but small on dense ≤30B", >0.1 (IQ3/Q2) is "visible".
- File-size arithmetic for planning: bytes ≈ parameters × bpw / 8 with whole-file bpw from the quantize README (Q4_K_M 4.89, Q5_K_M 5.70, Q6_K 6.56, Q8_0 8.50, IQ4_XS 4.46, IQ3_XXS 3.25, Q2_K 3.16), plus a few percent for Unsloth-style XL mixes.

## Key question 6: Practical envelope for ~170 GB usable (weights + KV + compute buffers): largest quant for 120B, 230B, 280B and 400B MoE models, with file sizes

### Inferences
- Bpw-derived weight sizes (parameters × bpw / 8, no mix overhead; estimates, not measurements): 235B → Q4_K_M ≈144 GB, IQ4_XS ≈131 GB, Q5_K_M ≈167 GB, Q6_K ≈193 GB, Q8_0 ≈250 GB; 280B → Q4_K_M ≈171 GB, IQ4_XS ≈156 GB, Q3_K_M ≈140 GB; 400B → IQ3_XXS ≈163 GB, Q3_K_M ≈200 GB, Q2_K ≈158 GB; 120B → Q8_0 ≈128 GB, Q6_K ≈98 GB. With ~10-20 GB reserved for KV and compute buffers, the largest fits are: 120B at Q8_0/BF16-of-non-experts (or native MXFP4/NVFP4), 230B at Q4_K_M/UD-Q4_K_XL-class (Q5_K_M only with a very small KV budget), 280B at IQ4_XS/Q3-class unless natively low-precision (DeepSeek-V4-Flash fits losslessly because 96% of it is already 4-bit), 350-400B at Q3-class (GLM-4.6 UD-Q3_K_XL 158 GB measured) and 480B+ at 2-bit-class.
- The measured GLM-4.6 ladder gives the quality price of that envelope for a BF16-trained 355B MoE: the 151-170 GB options (IQ3_KS / smol-IQ4_KSS) cost +4.2-5.7% PPL versus +2.5% at 196 GB (IQ4_KS) and +0.9% at 211 GB (IQ4_K); a 230B-class model at Q4_K_M in the same memory would be expected (from the 70B/670B scaling evidence in Q1/Q2) to sit nearer +1-2%, but no direct comparison exists.
- For the natively low-precision candidates (gpt-oss-120b, DeepSeek-V4-Flash, Kimi K2.6, Nemotron-3-Super FP8/NVFP4) the memory question is answered by the lossless file and "precision over speed" costs nothing extra; for BF16-trained 230B-400B MoEs it is a real trade between model size and bits.

## Key question 1: Hardware facts (Ryzen AI Max+ 395, the 2026 "Ryzen AI Max+ PRO 495", 192 GB boards, memory-bandwidth bound)

### Takeaway
The Ryzen AI Max+ PRO 495 ("Gorgon Halo") is real and shipping in 192 GB systems since late September 2026; it keeps the 16-core/40-CU layout of the 395 but moves to LPDDR5X-8533 on the same 256-bit bus (about 273 GB/s versus 256 GB/s, +6.6%). Decode on both chips is memory-bandwidth-bound, so 192 GB buys capacity, not speed.

### Inferences
- The 40-CU count for the PRO 495 is inferred from "GPU unit counts stay the same" plus the Radeon 8065S naming; no AMD page was readable to confirm the CU count directly.
- Bytes-per-token bound: with ~215 GB/s achievable, decode ceiling is roughly 215 GB/s divided by bytes read per token (active weights plus KV). gpt-oss-120b MXFP4 (5.1 B active, 63.4 GB file for 117 B total = 0.54 B/param) reads about 2.8 GB plus KV per token, giving a ceiling of about 70 t/s; measured 53-55 t/s on 128 GB (see Q3). The 192 GB node's 273 GB/s raises these ceilings by about 6.6%.

## Key question 2: Memory allocation on Linux (GTT/VRAM split, kernel parameters, what llama.cpp can map on 128 GB and 192 GB, kernel 7.x changes)

### Takeaway
On Linux the iGPU allocates weights from GTT (system memory), not from the BIOS carve-out; community 128 GB profiles expose 116-124 GiB of GTT and cap pinned pages at 108-124 GiB, and ROCm 7.2.3 reports the full 124 GiB as "VRAM". For 192 GB, the only direct evidence is a GLM-5.3-Flash model card whose example TTM settings give the GPU 176 GiB; kernel 7.3-rc deprecates `amdgpu.gttsize` in favour of `ttm.pages_limit`, and the Ubuntu HWE kernel 7.0.0-28 carried an amdgpu memory-management regression fixed in 7.0.0-29/7.0.13.

### Cited Findings
- 128 GB profile (Beelink GTR9 Pro, 124 GiB visible): BIOS UMA frame buffer 512 MB "if your BIOS exposes it" (leave 2 GB if that is the vendor minimum); kernel parameters `amdgpu.gttsize=131072` (128 GiB aperture) and `ttm.pages_limit=31457280` (120 GiB of pinned pages); the page states these are "limits, not preallocated VRAM"; it also carries `amdgpu.cwsr_enable=0` as a ROCm/ROCm#5590 MES-hang workaround — [strix-halo-guide setup page](https://github.com/hogeheer499-commits/strix-halo-guide/blob/main/STRIX_HALO_LOCAL_LLM_SETUP.md) (reviewed 1 October 2026).
- Upstream kernel 7.3-rc4 (checked 25 September 2026) "reportedly deprecates `gttsize` in favor of `ttm.pages_limit` and warns when GTT and TTM differ"; `amd-smi set --mem-carveout` and `amd-smi set --gtt` (AMD SMI 27.0.0 docs) are described as a possible future path needing root, initramfs changes, a reboot, a VBIOS that supports them, and Linux 7.0 or later — same source.
- 192 GB: "No 192GB-class profile is qualified. The 128GB values must not be reused on 192GB systems"; vendors advertise "up to 160GB of dedicated VRAM" through BIOS on 192 GB systems, and neither the BIOS route nor the small-UMA-plus-GTT route has been tested on 192 GB by that guide; its `setup.sh` stops above about 136 GiB visible RAM — same source.
- Ubuntu's 7.0.0-28 HWE kernel (updates pocket, 16 July to 17 August 2026) had an amdgpu memory-management regression, fixed upstream in 7.0.13 and in Ubuntu from 7.0.0-29 (Launchpad #2158267; one ComfyUI SDXL report of about 42x slowdown) — same source. A separate ROCm issue records a silent hang on 7.0.0-28 loading 27 GB and 6.9 GB FLUX models under ROCm/PyTorch, fixed in 7.0.0-34 (upstream commit "drm/amdgpu: drop retry loop in amdgpu_hmm_range_get_pages", backported in Ubuntu linux-hwe-7.0 7.0.0-30); llama.cpp ROCm loading a 23 GB model via hipMalloc/hipMemcpy worked on both kernels — [ROCm/ROCm #6508](https://github.com/ROCm/ROCm/issues/6508) (opened 23 July 2026, reporter update 29 September 2026).
- A second 128 GB profile (Sixunited AXB35 board, kernel 7.3.0-rc2, Mesa 26.2.3, README updated 11 October 2026): BIOS VGM carve 1 GB, `amdgpu.gttsize=126976` (124 GiB window), `ttm.pages_limit=28311552` (108 GiB pinned, "about 16GiB below the GTT window on purpose"); the live TTM value "can be clamped back toward the ~96GiB default", so read `/sys/module/ttm/parameters/pages_limit` and re-apply via a systemd unit; on 8 August 2026 a second model load OOM-killed the desktop after the TTM cap was set too high — [sypherin/strix-halo-setup](https://github.com/sypherin/strix-halo-setup/blob/master/README.md).
- A third 128 GB profile (GMKtec EVO-X2, Ubuntu Server 24.04 with kernel 7.0.4 from a PPA): `amdgpu.gttsize=126976`, `ttm.pages_limit=32505856`, `ttm.page_pool_size=32505856` (about 124 GiB), `amd_iommu=off`, UMA set to BIOS minimum (512 MB, 1 GB or 2 GB depending on BIOS version), `vm.overcommit_memory=1`, unlimited memlock — [kryoz/llama-strix-halo](https://github.com/kryoz/llama-strix-halo).
- A conservative profile: `ttm.page_pool_size=25600000 ttm.pages_limit=25600000` (about 97.7 GiB) "conservative but proven stable on Strix Halo with 128GB memory" (January 2026) — [Lychee-Technology/llama-cpp-for-strix-halo](https://github.com/Lychee-Technology/llama-cpp-for-strix-halo).
- Without GTT configuration the GPU sees about 512 MB; one GRUB example uses `amdgpu.gttsize=117760`, `ttm.pages_limit=30408704` (about 116 GiB); `rocm-smi` reports 0% VRAM on APUs, so read `mem_info_gtt_used` instead — [Skulk issue #144](https://github.com/Foxlight-Foundation/Skulk/issues/144) (May-June 2026).
- ROCm sees the whole window: on a GEEKOM A9 Mega 128 GB box with Fedora 43, kernel 7.0.8, ROCm 7.2.3, `ggml_cuda_init` reported "Total VRAM 126976 MiB" (about 124 GiB) and a 106.04 GiB model loaded with `-ngl 999 -mmp 0` — [llama.cpp discussion #23659](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/23659) (25 May 2026).
- Vulkan at full context on 128 GB: Qwen3.8-Flash-Next UD-IQ4_XS (93.7 GB) at context 262144 used 78.2 GiB GTT plus 27.1 GiB host RAM, about 10 GiB left of 124 — [huppiflupp/strix-halo-llm-speeds](https://github.com/huppiflupp/strix-halo-llm-speeds) (29 September 2026).
- Models of 90-95 GiB "run only alone" on a 124 GiB box: Qwen3.8-Flash-Next (90-94 GiB), GLM-5.3-Flash (95 GiB with MTP), Mistral-Small-4 (92 GiB), Qwen3.5-122B (73-76 GiB) — same source.
- `--no-mmap` is required on ROCm because "ROCm's mmap path is very slow above 64GB on gfx1151"; on Vulkan the author uses `--mmap` with `GGML_VK_PREFER_HOST_MEMORY=ON` — [sypherin/strix-halo-setup](https://github.com/sypherin/strix-halo-setup/blob/master/README.md). The kyuz0 toolboxes recommend `-fa 1` and `--no-mmap` for Strix Halo and state that `GGML_CUDA_ENABLE_UNIFIED_MEMORY` "should never be set" (the device reports `XNACK: 0`, `Direct host access: 0`, and unified memory corrupts output on that stack) — [kyuz0/amd-strix-halo-toolboxes](https://github.com/kyuz0/amd-strix-halo-toolboxes); [kyuz0 building.md](https://github.com/kyuz0/amd-strix-halo-toolboxes/blob/main/docs/building.md).
- The halo-box fork notes that "newer kernels size GTT more generously on their own, so the settings may not be needed there" — [halo-box/strix-llama.cpp](https://github.com/halo-box/strix-llama.cpp).
- A Level1Techs thread warns "GTT is the deprecated way and might not work anymore. You also need to configure TTM" — [Level1Techs](https://forum.level1techs.com/t/rocm-can-only-use-half-of-the-unified-memory-on-my-strix-halo/253152) (search excerpt; page refused).
- 192 GB direct evidence: a community GLM-5.3-Flash GGUF "sized to run fully on the GPU of a 192 GB Framework Desktop" is about 143.5 GiB in four shards; its card suggests raising the GTT limit with `ttm` kernel parameters and gives an example that "gives the GPU 176 GiB" — [cafonez/GLM-5.3-Flash-Gorgon-GGUF](https://huggingface.co/cafonez/GLM-5.3-Flash-Gorgon-GGUF) (search excerpt; huggingface.co refused).
- A Gorgon Halo buyer's guide assumes a GPU-usable budget of 160 GB with 32 GB kept for the system — [openclawdc](https://openclawdc.com/blog/ryzen-ai-max-pro-495-192gb-wait-or-buy/) (search excerpt). This conflicts with the 176 GiB example above and with the "nearly all 192 GB on Linux" claim.
- Phoronix (29 September 2026, as cited): AMD's "PerfOpt" IOMMU bypass is on by default in Linux 7.4, disable-able with `amdgpu.iommu_perfopt=0`, measured at 2-4% on a 64 GB Framework Desktop; `amd_iommu=off` disables NPU access and can prevent s0i3/s2idle sleep — [strix-halo-guide setup page](https://github.com/hogeheer499-commits/strix-halo-guide/blob/main/STRIX_HALO_LOCAL_LLM_SETUP.md). `amd_iommu=off` measured +1.0% to +7.3% prefill, decode within noise — [Nathanw1014/strix-halo-llamacpp](https://github.com/Nathanw1014/strix-halo-llamacpp); 5-12% faster than either IOMMU mode but NPU unavailable — [sypherin](https://github.com/sypherin/strix-halo-setup/blob/master/README.md).

### Inferences
- Scaling the 128 GB profiles to 192 GB with a 16 GiB host reserve gives roughly `ttm.pages_limit` = (192 - 16) GiB / 4 KiB = 46,137,344 pages (176 GiB), which is exactly the example the GLM-5.3-Flash-Gorgon card reportedly uses; the 16 GiB gap between pinned cap and window follows the sypherin practice. This is arithmetic, not a measured profile.
- Because both ROCm (hipMalloc) and Vulkan (GTT host-visible) draw from the same TTM pool, the practical weight-plus-KV budget on the 192 GB node is the TTM cap minus llama.cpp's compute buffers and the host RSS of the server, so about 170 GiB of weights plus KV at the 176 GiB cap; on 128 GB the observed ceiling was 108 of 124 GB in use (DeepSeek V4 Flash IQ2_XXS with draft), which hung the driver once.

### Gaps
- No measured, qualified 192 GB kernel-parameter profile exists in any readable source; the 176 GiB example is a single model card (not fetchable).
- AMD's "160 GB dedicated to graphics" versus "nearly all 192 GB on Linux" is unresolved; whether the 160 GB figure is a Windows driver limit or a VBIOS carve-out maximum could not be confirmed.
- The exact kernel 7.3 commit deprecating `amdgpu.gttsize` and whether Ubuntu 26.04.1's kernel 7.0 GA carries the 7.0.13 memory-management fix were not verifiable (Launchpad refused).
- Mesa/RADV version requirements for the largest GTT allocations were not stated by any source beyond "Mesa 26.0+" and the Mesa 26 MTP decode regression (Q4).

## Key question 3: Measured prompt-processing and decode speeds per model, quant, backend and context (128 GB systems; 192 GB where available)

## Key question 4: Backend state on gfx1151 in 2026 (ROCm/HIP versus Vulkan, ROCm versions, rocWMMA, flash attention, kernel and firmware bugs)

### Takeaway
Vulkan/RADV is the consistently reliable backend on gfx1151 and wins decode by 10-25% at Q4; HIP/ROCm wins prefill at long context by 10-25% in like-for-like runs and more with custom FP4/MTP engines, but has had wrong-output builds, page-fault and hang reports tied to amdgpu-dkms and firmware, and slow mmap above 64 GB. ROCm 7.2.x through 10.0 run on gfx1151 via community toolboxes and TheRock nightlies; the official support-matrix status could not be read.

### Cited Findings
- ROCm versions seen running on gfx1151 in 2026: 7.2.3 (kyuz0 toolbox, Fedora 43) — [discussion #23659](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/23659); 7.1.1 (HIP build of upstream llama.cpp, Sep 2026) and 7.14.1 (Strata engine, Oct 2026) — [huppiflupp](https://github.com/huppiflupp/strix-halo-llm-speeds); 7.2 toolbox plus TheRock `rocm-sdk-core` 7.15 nightlies — [sypherin](https://github.com/sypherin/strix-halo-setup/blob/master/README.md); 7.13 and 7.15 TheRock wheels and a torch build tagged `rocm10.1.0a20260822` (HIP 7.16) — [ROCm #6508](https://github.com/ROCm/ROCm/issues/6508); ROCm 10.0 (Fedora 44) as the kyuz0 stable image "using AMD's supported gfx1151 package set", plus ROCm 7.2.4 custom builds and a ROCm 7.14 validation stack — [kyuz0 README](https://github.com/kyuz0/amd-strix-halo-toolboxes); [kyuz0 building.md](https://github.com/kyuz0/amd-strix-halo-toolboxes/blob/main/docs/building.md).

- AMD's own llama.cpp toolchain: "Lemonade" llamacpp-rocm portable builds exist per-GPU, e.g. `llama-b1286-ubuntu-rocm-gfx1151-x64.zip` — [kryoz](https://github.com/kryoz/llama-strix-halo); Lemonade ROCm b1259 appears in batched tests and Lemonade b1251 in the permission-fault report — [BENCHMARKS.md](https://github.com/hogeheer499-commits/strix-halo-guide/blob/main/BENCHMARKS.md); [ROCm #6186](https://github.com/ROCm/ROCm/issues/6186). AMD also has a "ggml staging" build enabling `GGML_HRX=ON` (an `HRX0` device) compiled against a pinned TheRock SDK — [kyuz0 building.md](https://github.com/kyuz0/amd-strix-halo-toolboxes/blob/main/docs/building.md).
- rocWMMA: in May 2025 a rocWMMA build (`-DGGML_HIP_ROCWMMA_FATTN=ON`) "showed no performance loss at pp8K/tg8K and pp128K, but was not generally faster"; the root cause of slow HIP prefill was that "we convert to fp16 and use rocblas" with poor gfx115x kernels — [llama.cpp #13565](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/13565). The kyuz0 strix-llama image "does not use rocWMMA" and needs `GGML_HIP_NO_VMM=ON` — [kyuz0 README](https://github.com/kyuz0/amd-strix-halo-toolboxes).
- HIP prefill on gfx1151 is limited by MMQ tile defaults (128/128/8) causing VGPR spilling; proposed 48/64/4 tiles raised Qwen3.5-122B pp512 from 362.56 to 497.50 t/s; a June 2026 commit tuned MMQ tiles for RDNA 3.5 but the dense-aware gating PR was closed unmerged — [llama.cpp #21284](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/21284).
- Vulkan on gfx1151 cannot use coopmat2, so coopmat1 is the only FA prefill path; the dequant-once fix (merged 19 August 2026) targets this — [PR #25494](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/25494). Use RADV, not AMDVLK: "AMDVLK's ICD overrode RADV and caused false regression reports"; verify `(RADV STRIX_HALO)` — [BENCHMARKS.md](https://github.com/hogeheer499-commits/strix-halo-guide/blob/main/BENCHMARKS.md). - Like-for-like backend verdicts: "HIP wins pp16384; Vulkan wins tg128" (b9049, same source) — [MAX_PERFORMANCE_RESULTS](https://github.com/hogeheer499-commits/strix-halo-guide/blob/main/MAX_PERFORMANCE_RESULTS_2026-05-07.md); "Vulkan beats ROCm on decode by ~25% on this chip (97.7 vs 73.6 t/s on a 30B Q4)" — [hardware-corner](https://www.hardware-corner.net/strix-halo-llm-optimization/) (search excerpt); on a 122B model ROCm was faster at prompt processing and Vulkan at generation, 22.9 vs 21.3 t/s — [soothill.io, 3 August 2026](https://www.soothill.io/blog/2026/08/03/llamacpp-vulkan-vs-rocm-strix-halo/) (search excerpt); ROCm 7 nightlies gave the best single prompt-processing result (+19% on Gemma 4 E4B) and the worst regression (-8.7%) — [sleepingrobots ROCm 7 toolbox test](https://sleepingrobots.com/dreams/rocm7-toolbox-upgrade-strix-halo/) (search excerpt).
- HIP correctness: an August 2026 HIP build (llama.cpp 9731ad3) "computed wrong results on gfx1151 (found 2026-09-14)" — [huppiflupp](https://github.com/huppiflupp/strix-halo-llm-speeds); the halo-box fork reports HIP batched inference giving perplexity about 88 (vs 9.4) without `HIP_LAUNCH_BLOCKING=1` — [halo-box/strix-llama.cpp](https://github.com/halo-box/strix-llama.cpp); a HIP sentinel warned about a missing `TensileLibrary_lazy_gfx1151.dat` and a Gemma 4 26B HIP build (b8460) failed to load a GGUF — [BENCHMARKS.md](https://github.com/hogeheer499-commits/strix-halo-guide/blob/main/BENCHMARKS.md).
- Page faults under ROCm llama-server: repeated `PERMISSION_FAULTS: 0x3` gfxhub faults during ROCm/HIP model warm-up (Gemma-4-26B Q8, Lemonade b1251, ROCm 7.2.2, kernel 6.19.14) while "the Vulkan build works flawlessly" at 50-60 t/s; `HSA_ENABLE_SDMA=0`, `--no-mmap`, unified-memory mode did not help; AMD (1 September 2026) said it "does not recommend installing amdgpu-dkms on APUs such as gfx1151", that DKMS is known to cause these faults, and that the inbox kernel driver should resolve them; closed 15 September 2026 — [ROCm #6186](https://github.com/ROCm/ROCm/issues/6186).

- Long-context GPU watchdog: on Vulkan "attention over the large KV cache makes a single submission expensive enough to exceed the amdgpu compute-ring watchdog", fixed with a smaller micro-batch (`-ub 512`) — [agledger gpt-oss-120b recipe, Ubuntu 26.04](https://agledger.ai/guides/local-llm-strix-halo-ubuntu-26-04/) (search excerpt); a non-Strix AMD APU (gfx90c) hit Vulkan DeviceLost at about 90-103k tokens — [llama.cpp #21724](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/21724) (search excerpt). Under sustained load, `ring gfx_0.0.0 timeout` followed by a MODE2 reset is "described as normal"; the one hard hang on that box came from memory pressure (second model load with too high a TTM cap) — [sypherin](https://github.com/sypherin/strix-halo-setup/blob/master/README.md). DeepSeek V4 Flash IQ2_XXS with a draft at 108 of 124 GB "hung the amdgpu driver once" — [huppiflupp](https://github.com/huppiflupp/strix-halo-llm-speeds).
- Kernel floor: a VGPR-count bug caused crashes before 6.16.9; Fedora 43 "best-supported", Ubuntu 24.04 needs the HWE/OEM kernel, Mesa 26.0+ planned — [Skulk #144](https://github.com/Foxlight-Foundation/Skulk/issues/144); the Framework community guide says the issue persisted on 6.15.x and was resolved in 6.16.9+ and that ROCm 6.4.1 works with it — [Framework community guide](https://community.frame.work/t/amd-strix-halo-llama-cpp-installation-guide-for-fedora-42/75856) (search excerpt).
- Ollama's vendored llama.cpp was 56% slower on Vulkan than standalone llama.cpp (ollama issue #15601, as cited); vLLM with TheRock nightlies reached 4.3 t/s versus llama.cpp's 7.5 on a 27B BF16 model — [Skulk #144](https://github.com/Foxlight-Foundation/Skulk/issues/144); vLLM "does not run usefully on gfx1151" per the huppiflupp list (Mistral-Medium-3.5-128B W4A16 on vLLM 0.30.0+strix at 3.6 t/s).

### Inferences
- For a node that runs one model at a time and values stability, the 2026 record shows Vulkan/RADV as the lower-risk default for decode-heavy serving, with HIP worth testing per-model for long-prompt prefill (10-25% faster at 16K-35K in like-for-like runs) provided the inbox amdgpu driver is used (no DKMS), `--no-mmap` is set, and outputs are checked for the wrong-result class of bug.
- The reported HIP prefill advantage is partly a Vulkan coopmat1 limitation that PR #25494 (August 2026) narrowed for quantised KV; the stock-Vulkan deep-context pp figures from before August 2026 understate current Vulkan.

### Gaps
- AMD's ROCm compatibility matrix (which ROCm 7.x/10.x versions officially list gfx1151, and whether Ubuntu 26.04 is a supported OS) could not be read; the "ROCm 8.0 first-class support" claim is unsourced.
- No source documents ROCm 10.0 or 10.1 performance on gfx1151 beyond the single kyuz0 ROCm 10.0 figure (Qwen3.8-Flash-Next).
- No 2026 rocWMMA-vs-plain-HIP flash-attention comparison on gfx1151 was found.
- Whether the MES hang and the DKMS-related page faults can occur with Ubuntu 26.04.1's inbox kernel 7.0 driver and current linux-firmware was not determinable; reports span kernels 6.14-7.3 and firmware 0x80/0x83 without a definitive fixed-version statement from AMD.

## Key question 5: llama.cpp features that change the picture for MoE (MTP/speculative, --fit and --n-cpu-moe, KV-cache cost and quantisation, batch sizes, multi-model residency)

### Takeaway
Native MTP speculative decoding (`--spec-type draft-mtp`) is in mainline llama.cpp and on Strix Halo lifts decode by about 1.4x on Qwen3.5-122B, 1.5x on GLM-5.3-Flash and Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, and 2-3x on dense 27B models, while draft-model schemes (EAGLE3, separate small drafts) mostly slow this bandwidth-bound part down; `--fit` is on by default and is disabled by `--n-cpu-moe`, which has no purpose on unified memory; KV cost per token ranges from about 70 KB (MLA) to 180 KB (GLM-Air GQA) at f16, and two 90-95 GiB models cannot co-reside on a 124 GiB box.

### Cited Findings
- Speculative modes in current llama.cpp docs: `none`, `draft-simple`, `draft-eagle3`, `draft-dflash`, `draft-dspark`, `draft-mtp` ("uses Multi Token Prediction (MTP) heads from the main model"), `ngram-cache`, `ngram-simple`, `ngram-map-k`, `ngram-map-k4v`, `ngram-mod`; `--spec-draft-n-max` default 3; DSpark supports only Qwen3-backbone drafts; "MoEs require long drafts" for ngram-mod; the page names no supported model families for draft-mtp — [llama.cpp docs/speculative.md](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/docs/speculative.md).
- Qwen3.5 speculative decoding history: unavailable in March 2026 ("the target context does not support partial sequence removal"), draft-model decoding worked after PR #19493 (April 2026) and gave a positive speedup after PR #22558 (May 2026); at that time a maintainer-side reply said llama.cpp did not support Qwen3.5's built-in MTP — [llama.cpp #20039](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/20039).
- MTP measured on Strix Halo 128 GB (all Vulkan unless noted): Qwen3.5-122B-A10B 22.1 to 31.0 t/s mean (lab hybrid build, 2 Oct 2026) — [huppiflupp](https://github.com/huppiflupp/strix-halo-llm-speeds); the same model at UD-Q6_K_XL on ROCm 7.2.3, 19 to 26.86 t/s with `--spec-type draft-mtp --spec-draft-n-max 2` — [discussion #23659](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/23659); GLM-5.3-Flash UD-IQ1_S 17.0 to 25.0 (MTP length 2, `-fa off` required); MiMo-V2.6-Flash 27.8 to 30.3 (MTP) and 34.3 (DFlash n_max 3), with acceptance 26-69% by prompt type (poems lowest, code highest); Qwen3.8-Flash-Next 26.85 to 41.3 (MTP length 2); Qwen3.8-27B dense 12.3 to 27.9-36; MiMo MTP draft parameters are read only at server start — [huppiflupp](https://github.com/huppiflupp/strix-halo-llm-speeds). Qwen3.6 35B-A3B MTP IQ4_XS with Q8 head, `draft-n=2 -ub 1024`: 101.15 t/s average versus 62-77 llama-bench; Gemma 4 26B-A4B `draft-n=3`: 110.0 — [BENCHMARKS.md](https://github.com/hogeheer499-commits/strix-halo-guide/blob/main/BENCHMARKS.md).
- Draft models that hurt on Strix Halo: gpt-oss-120b with EAGLE3 fell from 51.7 (no draft) to 43.7 / 38.9 / 32.4 / 27.7 at draft lengths 1-4; Mistral-Small-4 with a Ministral-3B draft 35.3 to 26.7; Nemotron-3-Nano with a 4B draft at about 80% acceptance still fell to about 11 t/s (recurrent-state rollback); DeepSeek V4 Flash with DSpark went 11.94 to 7.7 on mainline b10488 but 18.9 to 28.9 on the tuned fork — [huppiflupp](https://github.com/huppiflupp/strix-halo-llm-speeds). The llama.cpp developer notes "there are no llama-bench parameters for MTP yet" — [discussion #23659](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/23659).
- `--fit`: llama-server documents `-fit` as "whether to adjust unset arguments to fit in device memory ('on' or 'off', default: 'on')" and `-ncmoe` as keeping MoE weights of the first N layers on the CPU (search excerpt of the server README via) — [llama.cpp #27501](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/27501); in that issue (August-September 2026) a commenter states "When you use n-cpu-moe you're just enabling a very blunt version of tensor overrides and effectively disabling fit", the author confirmed removing `--n-cpu-moe` and keeping fit gave the best result, and `--fit-ctx` was reported to crash on low-VRAM systems; closed 3 September 2026 without a code change — same source. The tuned Strix Halo fork recommends `--fit off` together with `--load-mode mmap --no-host --no-repack` for engram/PLE models whose about 95 GiB f16 table must stay memory-mapped on the host — [Nathanw1014/strix-halo-llamacpp](https://github.com/Nathanw1014/strix-halo-llamacpp).
- KV cache per token (f16 unless noted): GLM-4.5 Air (106B-A12B, GQA) about 1.4 GB at 8K, 5.8 GB at 32K, 23 GB at 128K; gpt-oss-120b about 0.3 GB at 8K, 1.1 GB at 32K, 4.5 GB at 128K (GQA plus sliding window, so not linear); DeepSeek V3.2 (MLA) about 8.6 GB at 128K — [wideareaai context calculator](https://wideareaai.com/tools/context-window-calculator) (search excerpt). DeepSeek-V3's MLA needs "only 70 KB per token, compared with about 516 KB for LLaMA-3.1 405B and 327 KB for Qwen-2.5 72B" — [arXiv 2505.09343](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.09343). Without working MLA kernels GLM-4.7-Flash fell back to full MHA at about 0.91 MB per token instead of an expected 54 KB — [GLM-4.7-Flash HF discussion](https://huggingface.co/zai-org/GLM-4.7-Flash/discussions/3) (search excerpt).
- KV quantisation on Strix Halo: q8_0/q4_0 KV raised filled-context decode (Qwen3.6 35B at 64K: f16 41.44, q8_0 49.13, q4_0 51.33) but slowed prompt eval (64K: f16 931.9, q8_0 731.2), "so f16 is better for first-turn long prompts" — [BENCHMARKS.md](https://github.com/hogeheer499-commits/strix-halo-guide/blob/main/BENCHMARKS.md); after PR #25494 the q8_0-KV prefill penalty at depth shrank (see Q4); one ROCm profile runs `-ctk q8_0 -ctv q8_0 -c 131072 -b 2048 -ub 2048 --kv-unified`; for Gemma "f16 KV beats q8_0" — [sypherin](https://github.com/sypherin/strix-halo-setup/blob/master/README.md).
- Batch and micro-batch: Qwen3.5-122B on ROCm, pp1024 best at n_batch 512 (266.5 vs 258.6 at 1024 and 137.8 at 128) — [discussion #23659](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/23659); `-ub 1024` helped Qwen3-Next and the MTP route — [BENCHMARKS.md](https://github.com/hogeheer499-commits/strix-halo-guide/blob/main/BENCHMARKS.md); gpt-oss-120b pp2048 rose from 1151.9 to 1463.9 at `-ub 2048` on Vulkan — [huppiflupp](https://github.com/huppiflupp/strix-halo-llm-speeds); `-ub 512` avoids the compute-ring watchdog at long context (search excerpt) — [agledger](https://agledger.ai/guides/local-llm-strix-halo-ubuntu-26-04/).
- Multi-model residency: kyuz0's recommended server flags include `--parallel 1 --models-max 1` — [kyuz0 README](https://github.com/kyuz0/amd-strix-halo-toolboxes); 90-95 GiB models "run only alone" on 124 GiB — [huppiflupp](https://github.com/huppiflupp/strix-halo-llm-speeds); a second model load OOM-killed the desktop when the TTM cap was too high — [sypherin](https://github.com/sypherin/strix-halo-setup/blob/master/README.md); a dynamic-loading `llama.ini` setup is used on a GMKtec box — [kryoz](https://github.com/kryoz/llama-strix-halo).
- Expert-weight pitfalls: stock Vulkan ran Mistral-Small-4's `ffn_down_exps` on the CPU, copying about 0.9 GB of expert weights per layer per token (0.82 t/s) until a MUL_MAT_ID F32 patch (35.3 t/s) — [huppiflupp](https://github.com/huppiflupp/strix-halo-llm-speeds); the tuned fork's "mmid row-list prepass" is "the main MoE-prefill fix, model-dependent" — [Nathanw1014](https://github.com/Nathanw1014/strix-halo-llamacpp).
- Server concurrency on MoE: aggregate decode scaled to `-np 8` (Qwen3.6 35B 59 to 162 t/s; Qwen3-Coder 30B 90 to 173) with a cliff at np 9 (-31% to -37%) on stock Vulkan — [BENCHMARKS.md](https://github.com/hogeheer499-commits/strix-halo-guide/blob/main/BENCHMARKS.md).

### Inferences
- On unified memory, `--n-cpu-moe` only moves expert matmuls from the iGPU to the CPU over the same LPDDR5X bus; the measured CPU-fallback case (0.82 t/s) shows why it is a loss rather than a capacity tool on this platform, so capacity decisions reduce to "weights plus KV plus compute buffers under the TTM cap".
- At the 192 GB node's budget, KV cost dominates only for GQA models at very long context: GLM-4.5-Air-class GQA at 128K is about 23 GB f16 (11.5 GB at q8_0), gpt-oss-120b at 128K under 5 GB, MLA models (DeepSeek V4, GLM-5 family if MLA kernels are active) about 9 GB at 128K; so a 140-148 GB 3-bit GLM-5.3-Flash or Qwen3.5-397B leaves 20-30 GB for KV and buffers at a 176 GiB cap, enough for 64-128K at q8_0 but not for two resident large models.
- Because MTP heads ship inside Qwen3.5/3.6/3.8, GLM-5.x, MiMo and DeepSeek V4 GGUFs (or as a small sidecar), the 1.4-1.6x decode gain is available without extra memory of note (Qwen3.5-122B 73-76 GiB with MTP; GLM-5.3-Flash 89 GiB without, 95 GiB with).

### Gaps
- The llama.cpp docs do not list which architectures implement `draft-mtp`; coverage for DeepSeek V4, GLM-5 and MiniMax M2.x is inferred from community runs (DeepSeek V4 ran with DSpark drafts on a fork; GLM-5.3-Flash MTP ran on an unsloth branch), not from a mainline support table.
- Exact per-token KV sizes for Qwen3-235B-A22B, Qwen3.5-397B-A17B, GLM-5.x and Nemotron 3 Super were not found; config.json-based arithmetic would be needed.
- No source measured two large models resident with one generating on 192 GB.

## Key question 6: Comparison points (Apple M-series Ultra with 512 GB, NVIDIA DGX Spark) only as far as they show which sizes are practical

### Takeaway
On gpt-oss-120b the DGX Spark prefills 2-3x faster than Strix Halo (about 1,300-2,000 t/s vs 300-1,400 depending on build) with similar or modestly higher decode (40-60 t/s), while the M3 Ultra decodes about 70 t/s; the 512 GB Mac is the only one of the three that fits Qwen3.5-397B or Kimi K2 above 3-4 bits, and 192 GB Strix Halo sits between 128 GB boxes and 256 GB-class systems for 3-bit 300-400 B MoE.

### Cited Findings
- gpt-oss-120b (MXFP4), llama.cpp, same test (search excerpt): Mac Studio M3 Ultra about 70.8 t/s generation and 864 t/s prompt processing; DGX Spark 38.6; Strix Halo 34.1 (older build) — [hardware-corner DGX Spark benchmarks](https://www.hardware-corner.net/first-dgx-spark-llm-benchmarks/).
- DGX Spark llama.cpp figures: about 60.6 t/s at empty context falling to 40.6 at 32K; prompt processing roughly 1,700-2,000 t/s; a Strix Halo laptop on the same build did 44.7 at empty context and 40.7 at 8,192 tokens versus the Spark's 59.5 and 55.1; prompt processing at long context 300-600 on Strix Halo versus over 1,300 on the Spark — [localaimaster review](https://localaimaster.com/blog/dgx-spark-local-ai-review); [NVIDIA forum thread](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/vibe-coding-with-nvidia-dgx-spark/346979) (search excerpts).
- 2026 Strix Halo gpt-oss-120b figures on current builds are higher than the comparison-era numbers: 53.8 t/s decode and 1,407 pp2048 on Vulkan (2 October 2026) — [huppiflupp](https://github.com/huppiflupp/strix-halo-llm-speeds).
- Qwen3-235B on these platforms: only a community "about 11 tok/s" figure for Strix Halo surfaced; no primary M3 Ultra llama.cpp figure was found — [runaihome](https://runaihome.com/blog/best-local-llm-128gb-unified-memory-2026/) (search excerpt).
- Qwen3.5-397B-A17B sizes: 3-bit fits 192 GB, MXFP4 fits 256 GB, UD-Q4_K_XL about 214 GB, 8-bit about 512 GB — [Unsloth Qwen3.5 guide](https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/qwen3.5) (search excerpt). Kimi K2 at 600 GB was not downloadable on 128 GB — [huppiflupp](https://github.com/huppiflupp/strix-halo-llm-speeds).
- Reviewers' framing: Strix Halo and Gorgon Halo "are not the fastest platforms for local AI workloads, largely because of memory bandwidth", and at about $7,000 a multi-GPU desktop may be cheaper for higher inference performance — [openclawdc](https://openclawdc.com/blog/ryzen-ai-max-pro-495-192gb-wait-or-buy/) (search excerpt).

### Inferences
- Size practicality by platform for the candidate roster: 128 GB Strix Halo tops out at 1-2-bit for 280-400 B MoE and Q4-Q6 for 120 B MoE; 192 GB raises that to 3-bit for 300-400 B MoE and Q8 for 120 B MoE; 256 GB (DGX Spark pairs, or M-series 256 GB) reaches MXFP4 on 397 B; only 512 GB reaches Q8 on 397 B or Q4 on 1 T-class (Kimi K2).

### Gaps
- No like-for-like 2026 three-way benchmark on current llama.cpp builds was readable (Petronella's DGX Spark vs Strix Halo vs Gorgon Halo page refused); the search budget expired before a query on M3 Ultra / DGX Spark Qwen3-235B numbers.

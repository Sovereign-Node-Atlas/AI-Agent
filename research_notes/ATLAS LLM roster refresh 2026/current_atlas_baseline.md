### Inferences
- GB vs GiB: the document quotes weights in decimal GB (63, 123, 130, 155, 79, 74) but the budget in GiB (~164). The Arbiter deliberately reads `footprint_gb` as GiB ("the upper bound (7.4 % above decimal GB) … the OOM backstop errs high") — [CFG L322-L331](../../scripts/day1/orchestrator/src/atlas/config.py#L322). Any new-model comparison should treat the 164 GiB figure as binary and the file sizes as decimal.
- The hard ceiling for a single engine is the Apex envelope: 155 GB weights + at most 160.3 GiB total projected on the KV ladder, leaving "about 3 to 5 GiB to spare" — [DOC L231](../../docs/ATLAS_FRAMEWORK_REVIEW.md#L231). A replacement model larger than ~160 GiB loaded cannot fit the current budget.

### Gaps
- No measured MemTotal, GTT pool, decode rate or prompt-processing rate exists in the repository; all are "expected"/"INFERRED" pending V3/V10 ([DOC L231](../../docs/ATLAS_FRAMEWORK_REVIEW.md#L231), [DOC L1067](../../docs/ATLAS_FRAMEWORK_REVIEW.md#L1067)).
- The document does not state a GPU clock or a measured 273 GB/s; the bandwidth figure is quoted from vendor/press specifications listed in Appendix D ([DOC L1272](../../docs/ATLAS_FRAMEWORK_REVIEW.md#L1272)).

## Key question 3 — Residency and scheduling policy: how many engines may be resident, one generation at a time, the Arbiter's budget rules, the everyday pair, the co-resident vision profile (S52)

### Takeaway
At most two engines may be resident and exactly one generates at any moment, everywhere including Celery jobs (C26). The Arbiter budgets `min(GTT total, MemTotal) − measured GPU resident set − 16 GiB` and grants, queues or refuses each load against projected weights + KV; the Apex engine is exclusive. The everyday pair gpt-oss-120b + Qwen2.5-VL-72B is 142 GB of weights and 157.7 GiB projected with caches, possible only because the vision engine's standing profile is 2 × 32k (S52).

## Key question 7 — Why each engine was chosen and what was rejected, including constraints inherited from the 128 GB plan

### Takeaway
Every choice is argued from bandwidth-per-token on this APU and from the memory the 192 GB machine freed: MXFP4 kept for gpt-oss because it is the native form, Q8_0 adopted for Nemotron/Qwen3.5/vision/Meditron because 128 GB no longer forces 4-bit, UD-Q4_K_XL for DeepSeek because its experts are natively low-bit and Q8 would leave ~2 GiB of KV, and one 72B vision engine chosen for quality over the 7B/32B/GLM alternatives. Rejections are recorded with the rule each fails and the event that would readmit it.

### Cited Findings
- Governing rule (C23): "use the highest quantisation that leaves working headroom, except where a model's native release is already low-bit" — [DOC L303](../../docs/ATLAS_FRAMEWORK_REVIEW.md#L303)
- gpt-oss at MXFP4: "Their expert weights were released natively in MXFP4. There is no higher-precision original to return to; a Q8 build would upcast the same 4-bit values, adding 60 GB and halving the speed for no accuracy." — [DOC L315](../../docs/ATLAS_FRAMEWORK_REVIEW.md#L315)
- DeepSeek Q4 not Q8: "Its experts, about 96% of the weights, ship natively low-bit, so UD-Q4_K_XL and UD-Q8_K_XL carry bit-identical experts and differ only in the remaining 4% of tensors, at a cost of 7 GB. UD-Q8_K_XL is about 162 GB against the 155 GB of Q4. Against the ~164 GiB budget … Q8 would leave about 2 GiB for the KV cache where Q4 leaves about 9" — [DOC L317](../../docs/ATLAS_FRAMEWORK_REVIEW.md#L317)
- Why the Apex engine is faster than Nemotron: "Decode speed follows bytes read per token, not parameter count: 13B active at 4-bit is about 6.5 GB per token, while Nemotron's 12.7B active at 8-bit is about 12.7 GB." — [DOC L313](../../docs/ATLAS_FRAMEWORK_REVIEW.md#L313); exec summary "at 4-bit it reads roughly half the weight bytes per token that Nemotron reads at 8-bit, so it is both far larger and about twice as fast" — [DOC L37](../../docs/ATLAS_FRAMEWORK_REVIEW.md#L37)

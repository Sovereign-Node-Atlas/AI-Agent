Method and labels. Every lucebox.com page listed in https://www.lucebox.com/sitemap.xml, plus three pages linked but not in the sitemap (/setup, /blog/pflash-v2, /downloads/lucebox-release.pub), /robots.txt, /llms.txt and /rss.xml, was downloaded in full with curl on 2026-10-15 and converted to text. Chart labels inside inline SVGs were extracted separately. Facts marked **primary** come from a page read in full. Facts marked **external** come from somewhere other than lucebox.com: the Lucebox GitHub repository (raw files and a blobless clone), the Lucebox Hugging Face organisation (API trees and READMEs), the Y Combinator company page, Hacker News (Algolia API) or press articles. Facts marked **excerpt** come only from a search-result summary. Page dates: the sitemap gives `lastmod` 2026-10-01 for every non-blog page. The legal pages say "Last updated 12 October 2026". Blog dates come from the RSS `pubDate` and the sitemap `lastmod`. GB means 10^9 bytes and GiB means 2^30 bytes. Where a figure was computed, the arithmetic is shown. The Wayback Machine (web.archive.org) could not be reached from this environment: curl got connection resets through the proxy and WebFetch refused the domain. Reddit's JSON search returned HTTP 403. GitHub HTML pages returned 403, and the GitHub REST API is not enabled for this repository in this session. The raw GitHub files and a `git clone --filter=blob:none` did work.

## 2. Products: hardware models and specifications

### Takeaway
One product is on sale: the **Lucebox Zero 495**. It pairs an AMD Ryzen AI MAX+ PRO 495 (128 GB or 192 GB LPDDR5X-8533, 273 GB/s) with an AMD Radeon AI PRO R9700 (32 GB GDDR6, 640 GB/s). The R9700 is attached through an M.2-to-x16 riser on a **PCIe 4.0 x4** link. The machine has 2 or 4 TB NVMe, a 1,000 W SFX PSU and an 11.97 L extruded aluminium chassis, and runs Ubuntu Server. The "128 GB model" and the "192 GB model" are memory options of this one machine. The predecessor (Ryzen AI MAX+ 395, 128 GB) is the "build before the Zero 495" and produced most of the published 128 GB benchmarks.

### Inferences
- The chassis is Lucebox's own design ("hand-assembled in-house"). The "compute board" is a third-party Ryzen AI MAX+ mainboard whose maker is never named; it has two M.2 PCIe 4.0 x4 slots and an SFF-8654 x4 port. The R9700 therefore runs on a x4 link (16 GT/s × 4 ≈ 7.9 GB/s per direction, nominal). The V4.1 and cluster posts work hard to keep traffic off that link ("The running state stays on the R9700. It no longer crosses the PCIe link between layers"; "The R9700 reaches the rest of the box over PCIe … The integrated GPU handles the network side").
- The Zero 495 uses the same APU as the Principal's A.T.L.A.S. node (Ryzen AI MAX+ PRO 495, 192 GB LPDDR5X-8533, 273 GB/s). A.T.L.A.S. has no R9700 and runs Ubuntu 26.04.1 with llama.cpp on Vulkan. Lucebox ships Ubuntu 24.04.4 with kernel 7.0 HWE and ROCm 7.2.4.

### Gaps
- No noise figure in dB(A) appears anywhere. Only fan RPM and qualitative statements.
- The compute-board maker (OEM), BIOS version for the Zero 495, the SSD model in the Zero 495 and the exact integrated-GPU name (the hardware page says 8065S; the System page lists the 395 build's 8060S) are not stated for production units.
- No measured result on the production Zero 495 with **128 GB** was found. Every 128 GB figure on the site comes from the Ryzen AI MAX+ 395 build (see §4).

## 3. Offering: models, sale and delivery, software, UI and APIs, updates, privacy, terms, licences

### Takeaway
Lucebox sells a **local appliance**: hardware plus a preinstalled open source engine (Apache 2.0) and a local web dashboard ("Lucebox Manage"). It is not a hosted service. It is sold direct from shop.lucebox.com in a Developer tier (from $6,499, a "limited-time $500 off" a $6,999 list price) and a Business tier (from $8,499 including 12 months of the paid "Lucebox Engine Pro" service, $199/month/machine otherwise). Two "qualified profiles" ship installed: DeepSeek V4 Flash across both GPUs, and Qwen 3.8 27B on the R9700. DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, Qwen3.8-Flash-Next and the vision variants install from the dashboard. The API is OpenAI- and Anthropic-compatible on private addresses only. Updates are signed (Ed25519), checked daily and rolled back automatically. The privacy policy says the box does not report prompts or replies to Lucebox.

## 4. How Lucebox runs DeepSeek V4.1 Flash: the memory hierarchy, and a check of the Principal's summary

### Takeaway
The Principal's summary matches the post's lead sentence word for word: "The Lucebox Zero 495 runs it across three kinds of memory: the 32 GB on the Radeon AI PRO R9700, the unified memory of the Ryzen AI MAX+ PRO 495 and, on the 128 GB model, the SSD." Four qualifications come from the site and the engine documentation:

1. The "383 GB" is the ROCMFP2S quantized GGUF (382.70 GB = 356.42 GiB). Per the engine docs it is "158 GiB of routed experts, 8 GiB of dense weights and 189 GiB of Engram tables". The Engram tables "are read from the file row by row and never mapped" on both memory sizes. So even on the 192 GB machine, about half of the file stays on the SSD. Only the experts are fully resident.
2. The 128 GB three-tier figures were measured on the older Ryzen AI MAX+ 395 build, not on a Zero 495.
3. The 192 GB runs use a router bias and an expert placement fitted to the box, plus an "approximate by design" decoder SWA replay. Outputs can therefore differ from the reference model.
4. The engine is ROCm/HIP only for V4.1. CUDA, vision and layer split are refused, and no Vulkan path exists.

### Inferences
- **The summary is accurate as a quote but incomplete.** Of the 356 GiB ROCMFP2S file, 189 GiB of Engram tables plus the 8 GiB dense part are not covered by the three-tier expert story; the site never mentions Engram. On 192 GB, "nothing comes from the SSD" is true of the routed experts. The Engram rows (layers 1 and 14, 24 hashed rows per token per layer) are still read from the file on the SSD or from the page cache. Arithmetic: 158 + 8 + 189 = 355 GiB, which is more than 192 GB = 178.8 GiB.
- On 192 GB the expert split is about 11.7 GiB on the R9700 (12,000 MiB) and about 146–150 GiB on the iGPU through GTT. The GTT values in DS41.md equal 176 GiB: 180,224 MiB / 1,024 = 176 GiB, and 46,137,344 pages × 4 KiB = 176 GiB. The Principal's node has the same APU and a ~164 GiB engine budget, but no R9700. Without the R9700, all ~158 GiB of experts plus dense weights, KV and drafter would have to fit in unified memory; no Lucebox profile for that case was found.
- The site's speed numbers for V4.1 conflict slightly with each other: 49 tok/s in the post and on the engine page, 48.7 in DS41.md, 49.4–49.5 on the HF drafter card, and "54 on one Lucebox Zero 495 with 192 GB" in the 12 Oct cluster post. These are evidently successive engine builds.
- The measured 192 GB box ran the R9700 on "PCIe Gen3 x4" (DS41.md). The production Zero 495 specifies PCIe 4.0 x4 and checks for 16 GT/s x4. The published 192 GB results therefore came from a test rig with half the nominal link bandwidth of the product.
- The headline speeds use greedy decoding, first request after start, 256-token answers, and the Lucebox quant (KL 0.266 vs teacher, top-1 77.7%). The quality-oriented Geometric quant scores higher (164/177) but reads prompts more slowly. The router bias changes which experts are selected, so outputs are not those of an unbiased reference forward pass.

### Gaps
- No Lucebox measurement exists for V4.1 Flash on a 128 GB **Zero 495** (PRO 495, LPDDR5X-8533), nor on a 192 GB PRO 495 **without** the R9700 using the Lucebox engine. The "without" baseline is Framework's DwarfStar run.
- How Engram row reads perform from the SSD on the 192 GB machine (latency, share of step time, page-cache residency) is not documented.
- Quality of V4.1 under the router bias and bounded SWA replay against an unbiased run is not reported, beyond the 177-question internal bench and the KL table.

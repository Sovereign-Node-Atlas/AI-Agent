## 1. Independent verification: do third parties confirm or contradict Lucebox's speeds and quality?

### Takeaway
As of 2026-10-15 no third party has reproduced any Lucebox DeepSeek V4.1 Flash figure, on the Zero 495 or on any Strix Halo plus R9700 build. Every V4.1 number (49 tok/s on 192 GB, 26 tok/s on 128 GB, about 1,000 tok/s prefill) is self-reported. Three things are independently corroborated. (a) Lucebox's "without the R9700" baseline, about 14 to 16 tok/s on a 192 GB PRO 495 with DwarfStar, matches what Framework and local-llm-benchmarks.dev measured. (b) A separate open-source llama.cpp fork shows that a Strix Halo plus R9700 hybrid raises decode by about 1.4 to 1.5 times and prefill by about 2.4 times on other large MoE models, which is the same direction and order as Lucebox's claims. (c) Lucebox's own engine, profiles and placement files are public, so the result could be reproduced, but nobody outside Lucebox has published an attempt.

### Inferences
- The "about 16 → about 33 tok/s" (2×) and "about 340 → about 1,000 tok/s" (3×) claims compare two different engines and two different quants: DwarfStar with antirez Q2, against the Lucebox engine with ROCMFP2S, router bias and SWA replay. Part of the gain may come from the engine rather than the R9700. No run of the Lucebox engine on the 192 GB APU without an R9700 exists to separate the two.
- The independent hybrid data point the same way. llama-halo-hybrid gets about 1.4× decode and 2.4–2.6× prefill from adding an R9700 to an APU, so a 2× decode and 3× prefill gain for V4.1 is plausible. V4.1's dense part is unusually heavy (see §4), which favours a larger gain from moving it to the 640 GB/s card. This is plausible, but it is not verified.
- Lucebox's headline speeds were measured with greedy decoding, on the first request after start, with 256-token answers and a router bias that changes which experts are chosen. Independent runs would need the same settings before they can confirm or contradict the numbers.

### Gaps
- No third-party run of `--profile ds41-gorgon` or `ds41-lucebox` was found. No third-party Zero 495 unit had shipped: deliveries are expected January to February 2027 (sibling notes §2).
- No independent quality check of V4.1 under Lucebox's router bias and bounded SWA replay exists beyond Lucebox's KL table and its 177-question internal bench.
- Reddit could not be searched from this environment (HTTP 403 in the sibling run; search-engine queries found no r/LocalLLaMA thread on Lucebox V4.1).

## 2. Hardware path on the node: expansion, links, the R9700, ROCm, and llama.cpp multi-device

### Takeaway
The EVO-X5 Pro has no PCIe slot and no OCuLink. A discrete GPU can reach it in two ways: (1) a USB4 v2 (80 Gbps) or USB4 (40 Gbps) eGPU dock with its own power supply; or (2) an M.2-to-PCIe riser, which gives PCIe 4.0 x4 only in SSD1 or SSD2. On the node both of those slots already hold the 4 TB OS drive and the 8 TB model drive. The third M.2 slot is PCIe 4.0 **x1**, not x4. The R9700 (gfx1201, 32 GB GDDR6, 640 GB/s, 300 W) and the PRO 495 iGPU (gfx1151) are both on the ROCm 10.1.0 support list for Ubuntu 26.04.1 with the GA 7.0 kernel, the node's OS. On Vulkan, llama.cpp can use the iGPU and a dGPU together only with layer split and tensor overrides: row split falls back to layer split, and tensor split carries a large penalty. All the measured iGPU plus R9700 hybrids that were found used ROCm/HIP, not Vulkan.

### Inferences
- Lucebox's exact layout, an R9700 on PCIe 4.0 x4 through an M.2 riser, is possible on the node only if SSD1 or SSD2 is given up. Both hold A.T.L.A.S. drives, and SSD1 is the BIOS boot priority. Moving a drive to SSD3 limits that drive to about 1.97 GB/s. A riser in SSD3 limits the R9700 to x1 (1.97 GB/s), half the Gen3 x4 link (3.94 GB/s) on which Lucebox measured its 192 GB result.
- A USB4 v2 dock is the only path that keeps both x4 drives. Measured USB4 rates (3.8 GB/s at 40 Gbps; about 5.4 GB/s on USB4 v2 in one test) are close to or above the Gen3 x4 link of Lucebox's measured 192 GB rig. So link bandwidth alone does not rule out Lucebox-class decode over USB4. Prefill traffic over USB4 with a 32 GB card has not been measured. Whether the PRO 495's USB4 v2 controller tunnels PCIe at 64 Gbps is unverified.
- Power: the node's 330 W external brick cannot feed a 300 W card. The card needs its own supply, either in the eGPU dock or as a separate ATX/SFX unit on an open riser. Lucebox needs a 1,000 W SFX PSU and quotes "~500 W under full inference load". The node's APU alone is rated 120 W sustained and 160 W peak. A dual-slot, full-length card cannot fit inside the 221 × 80 × 195 mm chassis.
- On the node's current Vulkan stack, iGPU plus R9700 would be limited to layer split with `-ot` placement. No measured Vulkan iGPU plus R9700 hybrid on Strix Halo or Gorgon Halo was found. Every measured one used ROCm/HIP.

### Gaps
- No measurement of an R9700 (or any 300 W card) on the EVO-X5 Pro over USB4 v2, or on an M.2 riser in that chassis, was found. No documented PCIe-tunnel rate exists for the EVO-X5 Pro's USB4 v2 ports.
- The model and speed of the node's 8 TB and 4 TB drives are not recorded in the baseline document.
- The R9700's current street price in Australia was not found. US/NZ excerpts only.
- Whether GMKtec's warranty covers an M.2 riser or eGPU use was not found.

## 3. Software path: can the Lucebox engine run elsewhere, which open tools reproduce the tiering, and what is the state of V4.1 in llama.cpp?

### Takeaway
The Lucebox engine is open source (Apache 2.0) and runs on any Linux machine with HIP/ROCm or CUDA. It is not tied to the appliance, but it has **no Vulkan backend**. Its two V4.1 profiles both assume an R9700 as the primary device and the Strix Halo iGPU as the expert device; no iGPU-only V4.1 recipe is documented. DwarfStar (antirez/ds4) is the only engine with an independent V4.1 Flash measurement on the node's APU: about 14 tok/s, main weights resident, Engram on disk, ROCm 10.0 container. Mainline llama.cpp still cannot run V4.1 on 2026-10-15. PR #28696 is open with no approvals; its runtime has been tested only on CPU and CUDA, and it is capped at about 16K context. The node's pinned v0.4.1 predates it in any case. ik_llama.cpp and KTransformers show no V4.1 support on AMD.

### Cited Findings
**Lucebox engine (repository read 2026-10-15)**
- Licence: "The Lucebox engine is open source under the Apache License 2.0" ([Terms](https://www.lucebox.com/terms)); the LICENSE file is Apache 2.0 ([LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Luce-Org/lucebox/main/LICENSE)) (via sibling notes).
- Backends: `set(LUCE_GPU_BACKEND "cuda" CACHE STRING "GPU backend to build: cuda or hip")`, `message(FATAL_ERROR "LUCE_GPU_BACKEND must be 'cuda' or 'hip' …")`, and the comment "# No BLAS, no Metal, no Vulkan, no examples/tests/tools." — [server/CMakeLists.txt](https://github.com/Luce-Org/lucebox/blob/main/server/CMakeLists.txt) (primary)

### Inferences
- The Lucebox engine is not appliance-locked. Running it on the node would mean a ROCm/HIP stack, which A.T.L.A.S. does not use for its LLM engines (it uses Vulkan), and an R9700, because no V4.1 profile treats the iGPU as the only device. Lucebox validates on ROCm 7.2.x. The node's Ubuntu 26.04.1 is listed for ROCm 10.1.0, but nobody has reported the Lucebox engine on ROCm 10.x.
- DwarfStar's V4.1 path on gfx1151 runs through kyuz0's container builds of antirez/ds4 main. Framework measured it, yet the antirez README describes V4.1 support as "Metal, and text inference on CUDA" only. The two statements conflict. The ROCm V4.1 path is evidently newer than, or separate from, the README's support summary.
- The general open-tool equivalent of Lucebox's tiering in llama.cpp would be `-ot` placement of `ffn_*_exps` tensors across devices, with the rest mapped from the GGUF. llama.cpp has no hotness-ranked expert placement, no router bias, no prefetch and no slot cache. Lucebox's own development record shows naive SSD tiering at under 1 to about 3 tok/s, and colibri at 1.29 t/s on a different model. A mapped-file spill in llama.cpp should therefore be expected at the low end (see §4).

### Gaps
- Whether the Lucebox engine starts V4.1 with a single gfx1151 device (`--target-device hip:0` on the iGPU, experts streamed) is undocumented and untested here.

The Vulkan DSV4 work that is in tree for V4 (PR #27453 LIGHTNING_INDEXER, PR #26578 hyper-connection ops, recorded in `gap_closure_2026-10-14.md` line 162) may or may not cover V4.1.
- The DwarfStar ROCm V4.1 build's handling of a second device (an R9700) is not documented. The kyuz0 gfx1201 image is a separate, R9700-only build.
- DwarfStar's own licence file was not read. The README says only that some llama.cpp/GGML source-level pieces are "retained or adapted here under the MIT license".

## 4. Expected results on the node: fit, bandwidth projections, SSD tier, and the quality gain of V4.1 over V4 Flash 0731

### Takeaway
Only one configuration has been **measured** on the node's exact APU and memory: DwarfStar with antirez's V4.1 Q2 (152 GiB of main weights resident, Engram tables read from SSD) at about 14 tok/s, or 16.3 tok/s with DSpark, and 300–390 tok/s prefill. With an R9700 attached, the only measurement is Lucebox's own: 48.7 tok/s on code and 31–33 tok/s at 2K–30K with DSpark, prefill about 750–1,000 tok/s. On bandwidth grounds, llama.cpp's Q2_K (186 GiB main) and Q3_K_M (245 GiB main) both overflow the node's 164 GiB engine budget. They would spill 30 GiB and 89 GiB of experts to SSD, which puts uniform-spill decode ceilings at about 7–11 tok/s and 3–4 tok/s. Measured mapped-file streaming elsewhere runs below such ceilings. On Artificial Analysis v4.3.2, V4.1 Flash leads V4 Flash 0731 by about 5 points (39.46 vs 34.33), mostly on agentic and automation tasks. Those scores are for full-precision serving, and every quant that fits this node is 2-bit (KL about 0.26 against the reference).

**Projections for the node (projection, not measured)**
Method. Decode time per token ≈ (bytes of resident weights read ÷ effective memory bandwidth) + (bytes read from SSD ÷ SSD read rate). Phases are taken as serial, with no speculative decoding. Bytes per token = 240 routed-expert reads (6 of 384 in each of 40 layers) plus the dense weights minus the token embedding. Engram row reads (24 hashed rows per token in each of layers 1 and 14) are taken as negligible. The resident budget is the node's 164 GiB engine budget minus an assumed 8 GiB for KV, compute buffers and drafter, leaving 156 GiB for weights. For calibration, the Framework DwarfStar run reached 14.0 tok/s against a computed ceiling of 19.3 tok/s at 240 GB/s for the antirez Q2 layout, about 73% of ceiling.

### Inferences
- V4.1's per-token cost is dominated by its dense part, about 7.2 B parameters of attention, shared expert and head, out of about 16 B active. This was computed: Q2_K attention 1.57 GiB at 2.625 bits ≈ 5.1 B weights. That is why Lucebox's Q8_0 dense weights make a no-dGPU decode slow (about 7.3 GiB per token from the iGPU), and why moving them to the R9700's 640 GB/s gives a large gain. Quants that compress the dense part to Q2_K/Q3_K (vcruz305) read fewer bytes per token, but compress the most-used weights hardest.
- For a node with no discrete GPU, the binding constraint is capacity, not bandwidth. The antirez Q2 layout (152 GiB main) is the only published V4.1 file whose main weights fit inside 164 GiB with room for KV, and it is the one Framework measured at about 14 tok/s. Both llama.cpp-format quants spill to SSD at 16.5% (Q2_K) and 36.8% (Q3_K_M) of experts, and without hotness-aware placement their decode would be SSD-bound in the single digits.
- The AA margin of 5 points (39 vs 34) concerns full-precision serving. The fitting V4.1 quants sit at a KL of about 0.26, the same band as V4's 2-bit IQ2XXS (about 0.41) rather than its Q4 (0.01). V4 Flash 0731 at Q4 (about 145–155 GiB) fits the node near-losslessly. How much of V4.1's lead survives 2-bit quantization is unmeasured.
- The disciplines where V4.1 leads most are agentic terminal work (TB4.0), automation (AutomationBench, GDPval), factual reliability (Omniscience), long-context reasoning (AA-LCR) and legal (LegalBench). Science coding (SciCode) and HLE are roughly level, and physics reasoning (CritPt) is slightly lower.

### Gaps
- No Q2_K or Q3_K_M V4.1 run on any Strix Halo or Gorgon Halo exists, because no llama.cpp build with Vulkan or ROCm `deepseek41` support exists. Every llama.cpp figure above is a projection.
- Real hot-expert skew for llama.cpp-style layer placement, page-fault read efficiency on the node's drives, and Engram read latency under load were not measured anywhere.
- No benchmark compares V4.1 at 2-bit against V4 Flash 0731 at Q4 or Q8 on the same tasks.
- The composition of the 10 GiB of non-expert main weights in antirez's Q2 was not verified, so its per-token figure is approximate.

## 5. Governance and risk: telemetry, licences, cloud links, SSD wear, power and heat

### Takeaway
The open-source Lucebox engine contains no telemetry, licence check or vendor endpoint. Its only outbound network code is the multi-box cluster socket and an optional "upstream" forwarder that is off unless configured. The cloud-touching parts belong to the appliance: the daily signed-update check, the undefined "optional cloud fallback", Manage, Connect and Tailscale. None of those is in the public repository, and none is needed to run the engine. Model downloads come from Hugging Face, which the node's allowlist already permits during model pulls. GitHub, Docker Hub (kyuz0 containers) and AMD's ROCm repositories are not named in the allowlist. Streaming weights is read-only and does not count towards an SSD's write-endurance rating, though read-disturb refresh may cause small internal writes (unverified per drive). Adding an R9700 adds up to 300 W on a separate supply, plus a blower that runs at 2,700–3,100 RPM at full power.

### Inferences
- Building the Lucebox engine from source, with `pflash_upstream_base` unset and the cluster mode unused, would add no outbound traffic. That is consistent with the zero-cloud rule. Fetching its sources, ROCm packages and any kyuz0 container would need build-time allowlist entries (GitHub, repo.radeon.com or the Ubuntu ROCm packages, Docker Hub) that the current allowlist does not name.
- Lucebox's three-tier mode is read-mostly. On the node, SSD wear from streaming experts would be negligible against TBW ratings. Features that write (DwarfStar's disk KV cache, prefix-cache snapshots) are the ones that consume endurance.
- An R9700 at 300 W roughly triples peak system draw against the APU's 120 W sustained. It also brings its own blower noise and a second power supply. With `amdgpu.runpm=0`, it raises idle power too: Lucebox's whole box idles at about 40 W, but the card's own idle figure was not found.
- Operating Lucebox's V4.1 profiles would require root or `ulimit -l unlimited`, raised GTT kernel parameters (176 GiB on the measured box, against the node's own GTT settings) and ROCm in place of Vulkan. These change the node's memory accounting and its stability record: earlier A.T.L.A.S. research found Vulkan/RADV the more reliable backend on gfx1151.

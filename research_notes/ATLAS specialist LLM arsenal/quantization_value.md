## Key question 1: Per-model quantisation value — every published quality measurement by rung for the named engines (rung, size, KLD/PPL/top-1, task impact, lowest rung that keeps results at the reference level)

### Takeaway
Only three engines have a measured near-lossless rung that fits alone: DeepSeek-V4-Flash (UD-Q8_K_XL, bit-exact, KLD ≈0), Qwen3.5-122B (Q8_0, KLD 0.0037) and, by construction, gpt-oss-120b (native MXFP4 experts; every GGUF label differs only in ~2 GB of non-expert tensors). Qwen3-235B-2507 fits at 5-bit (Q5_K_S/Q5_K_M, 5.5-5.7 bpw) where the nearest measured rung (ik IQ5_K, 5.9 bpw) costs +0.6-0.75% PPL; 4-bit INT4/NVFP4 checkpoints of it lose 3.7-5.0 points on AIME 2025, GPQA-Diamond and MATH-500 while MMLU-Pro moves under 0.7. GLM-5.3-Flash and Llama 4 Maverick fit only at 3-4 and 2-3 bits respectively: GLM's best fitting Unsloth rung is KLD 0.117 / 88.2% top-1 and a 3.5-bit GLM build lost 1.4 MMLU-Pro points (4.9 on maths); Maverick has no published measurement at any fitting rung. Qwen3.8-Flash-Next's best measured fitting rung is UD-Q6_K_XL (KLD 0.027 on Unsloth's harness); the ggml-org "Q8_0" (151.46 GiB) differs only by storing the 51B n-gram table at Q4_0 and is unmeasured. Nemotron 3 Super Q8_0, Qwen3-Coder-Next BF16/Q8_0 and Mistral Large 2 Q8_0 all fit with no model-specific KLD found.

## Key question 2: General evidence — reasoning amplification, model size, native low precision, dense vs MoE, larger-at-lower-bits vs smaller-at-higher-bits, abliteration plus quantisation, KV cache at long context

### Takeaway
Hard reasoning, maths, long multi-step agentic work and long-tail knowledge show quantisation losses 3-10× larger than MMLU-style or perplexity tests; 8-bit (W8A8, Q8_0, FP8) is lossless across sizes, 4-bit weight-only is "nearly lossless" on average but already costs 4-5 points on AIME/GPQA for a 235B MoE, and 3-bit is "risky" (3-6% average, up to 15 AIME points on a 70B dense model). Bigger models lose less at a given bit-width, but heavily-trained models lose more (precision scaling laws). Native low-precision weights (gpt-oss MXFP4, DeepSeek-V4 MXFP4 experts, Kimi INT4, Gemma QAT) are lossless only when the GGUF maps them unchanged; re-quantising or upcasting them gains nothing and post-training MXFP4 of a BF16 model is catastrophic. MoE expert weights tolerate low bits better than dense FFNs, which is why every recipe keeps attention/shared/router tensors at Q8_0. At equal memory, a larger model at 3-4 bits has beaten a smaller one at 8-16 bits in every measured comparison found, but none of those comparisons involves the named candidates. No study measures abliteration and quantisation together. KV q8_0 is task-lossless where measured; q4_0 on K is the dangerous setting.

**Dense versus mixture-of-experts sensitivity**
- MoQE (Kim, Fahim, Awadalla, Microsoft; arXiv 2310.02410, Oct 2023): "expert layers in MoE models are much more robust to the quantization than conventional feedforward networks (FFN) layers"; "MoE models with 2-bit expert weights can deliver better model performance than the dense model trained on the same dataset"; 79.6% size reduction vs fp16 MoE. Primary (abstract) — [arXiv 2310.02410](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02410)
- Router sensitivity: EAQuant — "router choices are sensitive to quantization noise, and small logit shifts can change which experts get picked" — [arXiv 2506.13329](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.13329v2); GEMQ (May 2026) argues layer-wise importance methods "overlook router shifts induced by quantization" (excerpt) — [emergentmind summary of arXiv 2605.23078](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2605.23078). Every GGUF recipe found keeps the router (`ffn_gate_inp`) at F32/high precision and attention/shared experts at Q8_0 (ggml-org Flash-Next router F32; ubergarm `attn_k/v=q8_0`; AesSedai default type Q8_0). Primary — cards cited in Key question 1.
- Measured: on 350B-1T MoEs a ~4.5-5 bpw mix sits within 0.5-2.4% PPL of Q8_0 (DeepSeek-R1-0528 UD-Q4_K_XL +1.10%, GLM-4.7 IQ4_K +1.56%), ~3 bpw costs 2-10% (GLM-4.7 IQ3_KS +5.25%), ~2 bpw 8-50% (R1-0528 UD-Q2_K_XL +9.80%, GLM-4.7 IQ2_KL +16.24%); dense 70B Q4_K_M +1.20%, Q3_K_M +4.72%, Q2_K +8.82%. Prior-verified (percentages computed here) — [ik_llama.cpp discussion #715](https://github.com/ikawrakow/ik_llama.cpp/discussions/715), [llama.cpp perplexity README](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/tools/perplexity/README.md)

### Inferences
- For "top results at all times", the decisive evidence is task-level on hard reasoning, not MMLU or wikitext PPL: the same 4-bit checkpoint that keeps MMLU/MMLU-Pro within 0.4-0.7 points loses 3.7-5.0 points on AIME/GPQA/MATH-500 on a 235B MoE, and 3-bit weight-only loses 3.9-6.2 points on average and up to 15.5 AIME points on a 70B dense model. Long-chain and agentic tasks compound per-token error (longer outputs, lower test-time-scaling gains, larger SWE-bench than LiveCodeBench loss).
- The size advantage and the training-saturation penalty pull in opposite directions; 2025-2026 models trained on 25-36T tokens (Nemotron 3 Super, Qwen3.x, GLM-5.3) are on the penalty side, so 2024-era "Q4_K_M is fine for 70B" tables likely understate their loss.
- "Less quantisation" is model-specific: for native-MXFP4/INT4/QAT models it means mapping the native tensors unchanged and keeping the rest at BF16/F16 (more bits than the release add nothing); for FP8-released models (GLM-5.3-Flash, DeepSeek non-expert tensors) Q8_0 is at or above release precision; for BF16 models (Qwen3-235B, Flash-Next, Qwen3.5-122B, Mistral Large 2, Coder-Next, Maverick, Nemotron 3 Super's release) Q8_0 ≈ BF16 and every lower rung is a measurable cost.
- MoE robustness means a 3-4 bpw MoE loses less than a 3-4 bpw dense model, but GLM-5.3-Flash's 3.5-bit task result (−1.4 MMLU-Pro, −4.9 maths) shows that "robust" does not mean "reference-level" for a 2026 MoE.
- Larger-at-lower-bits wins in every measured equal-memory comparison found (3-bit 32B vs BF16 7B; 2-bit DeepSeek-V3.1 vs full-precision Qwen3-235B on Aider), but the gaps are between model generations or sizes with large base-score differences; between the named candidates (e.g., GLM-5.3-Flash at 3.8 bpw vs DeepSeek-V4-Flash lossless, or Qwen3.5-397B at 3 bits vs Qwen3.5-122B at Q8_0) no head-to-head exists, and the base-score gap must exceed the 3-bit loss for the larger model to win.
- Abliteration and quantisation errors are measured against different references (Heretic KL = first-token, harmless prompts, vs the base model; llama.cpp KLD = all tokens of wikitext vs the BF16 of the same weights), so they cannot be summed. One structural point follows from the facts: abliterating a native-MXFP4 model in BF16 and re-quantising to MXFP4 converts a lossless expert mapping into a fresh PTQ rounding of edited weights, whereas a Q8_0 of the edited BF16 keeps that rounding small; the size of that difference has not been measured.
- KV q8_0 is the only KV reduction with task-level "no change" evidence; on V4 it now runs (post #25202) but its quality is unmeasured, and the models whose caches are already small (GLM-5.3-Flash 11 KiB/token, Flash-Next 24 KiB, Coder-Next 24 KiB) gain little from quantising it, while Qwen3-235B (188 KiB) and Mistral Large 2 (352 KiB) are the ones whose long-context fit depends on it.

### Gaps
- No measured equal-memory head-to-head between any two named candidates.
- No study of abliteration plus quantisation compounding; no KLD of an abliterated GGUF measured against its own abliterated BF16 and against the original BF16.
- arXiv 2505.02214 and 2606.00206 were read at abstract level only; per-size tables not extracted.
- No RULER/needle measurement of KV q8_0 vs f16 on any 100B+ MoE in llama.cpp; no quality figure for V4 q8_0 KV after PR #25202.
- Whether Hadamard rotation applies to GLM-5.3-Flash's MLA/DSA layers at the v0.4.1 pin was not checked.

## Key question 3: A practical, source-grounded threshold table, and what the KLD levels mean in task terms

### Takeaway
On llama.cpp's wikitext harness the ladder is consistent across 8B dense, 35-122B MoE and 284B QAT MoE: Q8_0 ≈ KLD 0.001-0.004 (top-1 ~98%, PPL +0.01-0.14%), Q6_K ≈ 0.004-0.006 (top-1 ~96%), Q5 ≈ 0.005-0.011, Q4 ≈ 0.010-0.03 (top-1 92-96%, PPL +0.3-2.8%), IQ4_XS/MXFP4-style 4.0-4.3 bpw ≈ 0.02-0.04, 3-bit ≈ 0.03-0.18 (top-1 ~85-90%), 2-bit ≈ 0.1-0.4. - oobabooga's localbench methodology (14 Apr 2026): reference = Unsloth BF16 GGUF; ~250,000 tokens across coding, general chat, tool calling, science, non-Latin scripts and long documents up to ~30K tokens; argues that Q4_K_M KLD of 0.01-0.03 "seen elsewhere" reflects easier Wikipedia-at-~2,048-token tests (no result values published on that page). Primary — [localbench methodology](https://localbench.substack.com/p/gguf-benchmark-methodology)
- KLD scales differ by tool (llama.cpp Q4_K_M ≈ 0.02-0.03 vs ≈ 0.22 in an MLX/"long-mode" tool on Qwen3.6-27B); the recorded base logits in llama.cpp are 16-bit-scaled, giving an f16-vs-f16 floor of 0.00055. Prior-verified — [smcleod.net](https://smcleod.net/2026/04/measuring-model-quantisation-quality-with-kl-divergence/), [llama.cpp perplexity README](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/tools/perplexity/README.md)
- Unsloth warns PPL/KLD can mislead (MiniMax-M2.5 IQ2_XXS vs IQ3_S example) and that Wikipedia calibration flatters Wikipedia-based tests. Prior-verified — [Unsloth Qwen3.5 GGUF benchmarks](https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/qwen3.5/gguf-benchmarks)

### Inferences
- Decision rule implied by the sources (stated as a derived mapping, not a recommendation): mean KLD ≤ ~0.005 (Q8_0, Q6_K, 5-bit MoE mixes) has never produced a measurable task loss in any source here; 0.01-0.03 (good Q4/Q5) costs ≤ 0.4 MMLU-class points but up to ~4-5 points on AIME/GPQA-class reasoning; 0.07-0.15 (3-3.5-bit) costs ~1-2 MMLU-class points and ~5 points on maths; ≥ 0.2 (2-bit) costs ≥ 3 points on MMLU-class tests and ~6-9 points on Aider-style coding.
- Because hard reasoning amplifies loss, a candidate whose best fitting rung is in the 0.07-0.17 band (GLM-5.3-Flash, Flash-Next at ≤ IQ4_XS, Maverick at 2-3 bits by analogy) carries a measurable deficit relative to its own published scores; a candidate at ≤ 0.005 (DeepSeek-V4-Flash UD-Q8_K_XL, Qwen3.5-122B Q8_0, gpt-oss MXFP4, Coder-Next BF16/Q8_0, Nemotron 3 Super Q8_0 and Mistral Large 2 Q8_0 by the general ladder) can be expected to reproduce its published scores.
- KLD thresholds transfer only within one harness: Unsloth's GLM/Flash-Next numbers should be compared with each other, not with the llama.cpp wikitext column; a node-side `llama-perplexity --kl-divergence-base` run on a domain corpus is the only way to place all candidates on one scale.

### Gaps
- No single study maps KLD to hard-reasoning loss (AIME/GPQA/SWE-bench) on a large MoE at several rungs; the KLD→task pairs above are MMLU-class (Gemma 3, GLM) or knowledge (Quesma).
- The localbench long-input KLD results (per quant) were not found; only the method page.
- The numeric slope of Quesma's KLD-accuracy line is in a chart only.

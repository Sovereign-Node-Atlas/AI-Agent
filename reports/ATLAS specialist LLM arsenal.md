# A specialist arsenal, each engine alone at full budget

Running every engine alone with the whole ~164 GiB changes the arsenal's shape more than any single new model does: the co-residency rule that once justified a small 63 GB default is gone, so the A.T.L.A.S. node should carry a short set of 300B-class 2026 engines, each loaded by itself at the least-quantised rung that fits, and allocate them to personas and agents by measured domain strength rather than by what pairs cheaply. The single quality leader among engines that fit is **GLM-5.3-Flash** (Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index 41.8, the top in-envelope score, leading most legal, finance, medical-documentation, agentic and long-context boards ([AA](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/glm-5-3-flash))), but it fits only at about 4 bits (UD-IQ4_XS, 146.05 GiB, mean KLD 0.117 — clearly lossy) and needs a llama.cpp newer than the node's pin; the only named engine with a *lossless* fitting rung is **DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731** at UD-Q8_K_XL (150.75 GiB, bit-exact, KLD ≈0 ([Unsloth](https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/deepseek-v4.md))), and **Qwen3.8-Flash-Next** at UD-Q6_K_XL (157.55 GiB) sits at its harness's 8-bit ceiling. The Principal's "less quantisation" rule therefore pulls toward V4-Flash and Flash-Next, while "top results" pulls toward GLM; the report resolves this per seat and marks GLM's lead as full-precision evidence that a node KLD test must confirm at IQ4_XS. Of the twelve named items, five are **include** (DeepSeek-V4-Flash, Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, Nemotron 3 Super Q8, gpt-oss-120b, Nemotron 3 Diarization), four are **include with conditions** (GLM-5.3-Flash with a re-pin, Qwen3-Coder-Next, gpt-oss-120b Abliterated via the Derestricted build, EmbeddingGemma 2 only as a code/multimodal side index), and three are **exclude** (Qwen3-235B-A22B as superseded, Llama 4 Maverick as 2-to-3-bit-only and bottom-of-board, and "Mistral Large 2 Uncensored" because the named artefact is a Q4_0 build driven by a misinformation system prompt that conflicts head-on with the Principal's quality rule). Everything below is a hypothesis for the node's own measurements: no candidate has a decode, KV-footprint or KL-divergence figure on the PRO 495 at any pin, so the deciding evidence is still a node test, and this research changes no repository file.

## How the new directives rewrite the arsenal, and how sources are marked

Six directives from the Principal govern this report, and three of them overturn the earlier roster's logic. The Principal has **ruled out small (27B-class or similar) LLMs** for the specialist seats, **allows every engine to run alone and sequentially with the whole ~164 GiB budget** (Ren and Arthur need not be co-resident), puts **result quality first with speed secondary**, **prefers less quantisation** and wants the effect of each rung made explicit, keeps the **abliterated and uncensored engines**, and asks for a **verdict on twelve named models**. The budget itself is the node's design figure: MemTotal of about 187–188 GiB once the crash-kernel reservation is removed, minus a 6–8 GiB GPU resident set and a fixed 16 GiB host reserve, gives about **164 GiB for one engine** ([DOC L231](../docs/ATLAS_FRAMEWORK_REVIEW.md#L231)). Dropping co-residency is the largest change: the earlier report kept **gpt-oss-120b** as the Corporate default largely because its 63 GB left room for a co-resident vision engine, and it proposed **Qwen3.8-27B** and **Gemma 4 31B** as a coder seat and a light vision tier ([ATLAS LLM roster refresh 2026](./ATLAS%20LLM%20roster%20refresh%202026.md)). Both of those 27B-class choices are now out of scope for the specialist seats, and gpt-oss-120b's small size no longer earns it the default; its weak 2026 agentic scores (AA 11.6) relegate it to a fast-utility role. The seats now want the strongest 2026 engine each discipline can be given, loaded alone at the highest precision that fits.

Facts are marked by how they were obtained. A plain inline citation is a page, GGUF header, config or source file read directly by the research team and re-verified; sizes are decimal GB (bytes ÷ 10⁹) for files and GiB (bytes ÷ 2³⁰) for the budget, because the Arbiter distinguishes them. Every leaderboard score is a **full-precision or provider-precision run**: no board has measured any candidate at a quantised GGUF rung, which is itself the central evidence gap. Every decode figure is a **projection** unless it names a device, because the only measurements on the node's exact silicon (the Ryzen AI MAX+ PRO 495, gfx1151) are GLM-5.3-Flash at 3.84 bpw (17.4 tok/s, Vulkan ([cafonez](https://huggingface.co/cafonez/GLM-5.3-Flash-Gorgon-GGUF/raw/main/README.md))) and MiMo-V2.6-Flash MXFP4 (18.3 tok/s, ROCm ([local-llm-benchmarks](https://local-llm-benchmarks.dev/data/results.json))). The node pins llama.cpp **v0.4.1 (commit b29c606, 2026-09-14)** ([L01 L27-L29](../scripts/day1/phase2/01-llama.sh#L27)); several recommendations below need a re-pin, flagged where they arise.

## The per-persona and per-agent allocation, and where quality and precision collide

The arsenal maps ten personas and the standing agents to engines by the independent domain evidence, then names the least-quantised rung that fits alone. The recurring tension is that **GLM-5.3-Flash is the in-envelope quality leader on almost every professional board** — LegalBench 83.93%, Finance Agent v2 57.85% (top open), MedScribe 88.94% (top open), AA-LCR 80.0, the lowest hallucination rate of the group, and the top open model on LMArena legal, medical, maths and creative categories ([Vals](https://www.vals.ai/benchmarks/legal_bench); [AA](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/glm-5-3-flash); [LMArena](https://huggingface.co/datasets/lmarena-ai/leaderboard-dataset)) — yet it fits only at **UD-IQ4_XS (146.05 GiB, KLD 0.117, 88.2% top-1)**, a clearly lossy rung, and needs a pin newer than the node's, because every GLM-5.3-Flash GGUF declares the `glm5-next` architecture that no build before b11279 supports. DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731 fits bit-exact. So GLM's measured lead is a full-precision result; at IQ4_XS its local quality drops by an unmeasured amount that may narrow the gap to a lossless V4-Flash. Where a seat is reasoning-heavy and the Principal values guaranteed fidelity, the lossless engine is the safer pick; where the seat is a professional-knowledge or long-context seat GLM wins most clearly, the 4-bit rung is the right risk, pending a node KLD run.

## Quantisation value: what each rung costs, engine by engine, and the rule that follows

The Principal asked for the effect of quantisation to be made explicit. The anchor is a consistent llama.cpp wikitext ladder confirmed across 8B dense, 35–122B MoE and 284B QAT MoE models: **Q8_0 ≈ KLD 0.001–0.004 (no measurable task loss anywhere in the sources), Q6_K ≈ 0.004–0.006, 5-bit MoE mixes ≈ 0.005–0.011, Q4 K-quant ≈ 0.01–0.03, IQ4/MXFP4 4.0–4.3 bpw ≈ 0.02–0.04, 3-bit ≈ 0.03–0.18, 2-bit ≈ 0.1–0.4** ([llama.cpp perplexity README](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/tools/perplexity/README.md); [quant notes](../research_notes/ATLAS%20specialist%20LLM%20arsenal/quantization_value.md)). Two findings make this more than a perplexity story. Hard reasoning amplifies the loss 3-to-10-fold: the same 4-bit checkpoint that holds MMLU within 0.4–0.7 points lost **4.58 points on AIME and 3.73 on GPQA** on the 235B Qwen3 MoE, and a 3.5-bit GLM-5.3-Flash build lost 1.4 MMLU-Pro and **4.9 maths points** ([inference-optimization card](https://huggingface.co/inference-optimization/Qwen3-235B-A22B-Thinking-2507-quantized.w4a16); [pfeifferj card](https://huggingface.co/pfeifferj/GLM-5.3-Flash-GSQ-RCO-GGUF)). And "less quantisation" is model-specific: for a native-MXFP4/QAT model it means mapping the native tensors unchanged and keeping the rest at BF16 (more bits add nothing), while for a BF16-trained model every rung below Q8_0 is a measurable cost.

The **decision rule** the sources support, stated as a derived mapping rather than a recommendation: mean KLD ≤ ~0.005 (Q8_0, Q6_K, 5-bit MoE mixes) has never produced a measurable task loss in any source; 0.01–0.03 (good Q4/Q5) costs ≤0.4 MMLU-class points but up to ~4–5 on AIME/GPQA-class reasoning; 0.07–0.15 (3–3.5-bit) costs ~1–2 MMLU points and ~5 on maths; ≥0.2 (2-bit) costs ≥3 on MMLU and 6–9 on coding. Applied to this arsenal: **V4-Flash (UD-Q8_K_XL), Flash-Next (UD-Q6_K_XL), gpt-oss (MXFP4), Nemotron Q8_0, Coder-Next Q8_0 and the 5-bit 235B-class engines are expected to reproduce their published scores; GLM-5.3-Flash at IQ4_XS (0.117) carries a measurable deficit** whose size on hard reasoning is unknown and must be measured, because KLD thresholds transfer only within one harness and GLM's figure is on Unsloth's scale, not llama.cpp wikitext. The practical consequence is that GLM's full-precision board lead over V4-Flash may or may not survive the drop to 4 bits, and that single question decides whether the Principal's top seats run a lossy-but-stronger engine or a lossless-but-lower-scoring one.

## Sequential operation: swaps dominated by re-prefill, not disk, and how to group work

With every engine running alone, the arsenal becomes a set of 120–170 GB files that the node swaps between tasks. The node loads through the page cache (the pinned llama.cpp resolves `--load-mode auto` to non-mmap on the iGPU, avoiding the ROCm "slow mmap above 64 GB" trap), but because a resident 145–160 GiB engine leaves only about 10–25 GiB of the 187 GiB for page cache, **switching between any two large engines is a full cold read**. The cold read itself is 25–70 s per file (about 40–65 s through LUKS on a single Gen4 drive at the 2.4–6.3 GB/s rates measured on Strix Halo), plus a graceful stop (~10 s default), allocation and warm-up ([single_engine KQ3](../research_notes/ATLAS%20specialist%20LLM%20arsenal/single_engine_performance.md)). The design's own estimate of 15–45 s per swap is optimistic; **50–85 s is the realistic band**. But the larger cost is **re-prefill of the task context**: on stock Vulkan, DeepSeek-V4-Flash takes about 4.4 min to ingest a fresh 32K prompt and up to **13 min at 64K**, while the faster engines (Flash-Next, and newer engines or the Gufo fork) are far lower (Flash-Next 71–94 s at 32K, down to 11–22 s on Gufo). The single largest saving is **slot save/restore** (`--slot-save-path`): a 32K DeepSeek-V4 slot is ~0.2 GiB at f16 and reads back in seconds, so saving a task's slot before its engine leaves and restoring it when the engine returns removes most of the re-prefill cost — pending a node test of whether restore works for every architecture at the pin.

At most two engines may be resident under the current Arbiter, but only one generates, so the arsenal's true constraint is **how often work forces a swap**. The disk footprint of a full arsenal is well within the 8 TB drive: a representative ten-engine set — GLM-5.3-Flash UD-IQ4_XS (156.8 GB), Qwen3.8-Flash-Next UD-Q6_K_XL (169.2 GB, +MTP 2.8 GB +mmproj 0.6 GB), DeepSeek-V4-Flash UD-Q8_K_XL (161.9 GB, +DSpark 10.9 GB), MiMo-V2.6-Flash uncensored MXFP4 (168.4 GB), gpt-oss-120b F16-trunk (65.4 GB), Nemotron 3 Super Q8_0 (128.5 GB), Baichuan-M3 Q5_K_S (161.9 GB), Intern-S1 Q5_K_S (162.0 GB), Qwen3-Coder-Next BF16 (159.5 GB) and Qwen3-VL-235B Q5_K_S (161.9 GB, vision alternative) — plus the companions (embedder 8.0 GB, reranker 8.0 GB, router 4.5 GB, OCR ~1.5 GB, diariser 0.1 GB, EmbeddingGemma 2 0.3 GB) totals roughly **1.9 TB**, leaving ample room on the 8 TB `/srv/atlas` volume. The work-grouping rule that limits swaps is to **batch by engine**: route all of a hemisphere's or a persona's queued work to the resident engine before swapping, keep the most-used engine (GLM-5.3-Flash) resident by default, use slot save/restore for any task that will resume, and reserve the swap-heavy Deep Think "deep" runs (3–4 swaps, 2.5–5.5 min of swap overhead) for when the Principal accepts the latency. The fast engines (gpt-oss at 59 GB, Nemotron at 120 GB) also recover page-cache room, so a swap to them after a large engine is partly warm.

## The abliterated and uncensored arsenal, and the companion engines

The uncensored seat has two clean, licence-compatible choices and one named artefact to avoid. For **Valerie's cyber and AEGIS work**, the strongest option is the **MiMo-V2.6-Flash uncensored** build (dealignai RL-UNCENSORED → AliceThirty MXFP4, 156.85 GiB, MIT): it is the only large 2026 uncensored model with published capability retention (MMLU 83.41% vs base 83.56%, −0.14 pp; HarmBench compliance 99.69% with thinking off ([dealignai card](https://huggingface.co/dealignai/MiMo-V2.6-Flash-RL-UNCENSORED/raw/main/README.md))), and its base is the Vals cyber leader. For the **explicit-override uncensored seat** (Ren/Arthur prefixes), the **ArliAI gpt-oss-120b Derestricted** build is the best measured gpt-oss variant — it *raises* NatInt above base (35.70 vs 33.68) rather than damaging it like the earlier huihui abliteration (16.72), is Apache 2.0, and its Q8_0 (115.76 GiB) keeps the edited experts near BF16 ([G4](../research_notes/ATLAS%20specialist%20LLM%20arsenal/completeness_review.md)). The node's *current* abliterated file (huihui v2 → noctrex MXFP4_MOE) should be retired: it has no provenance card and no refusal, KL or benchmark data, and huihui's source repos now return 401. The large 2026 abliterations of GLM-5.3-Flash, Flash-Next and V4-Flash exist but publish no refusal or quality figures and ablate only non-expert layers, so they are not yet evidence-backed. The governing fact from the earlier work stands: abliterating a fully uncensored large MoE costs roughly −8 NatInt points, and every abliterated output still passes the same approval gate.

The companions divide into retrieval, documents, speech and routing. For **retrieval**, the primary embedder should move off bge-m3 (MTEB 59.56) to **Qwen3-Embedding-8B** (70.58, proven llama-server path, truncatable to the existing 1024-dim schema) or **Nemotron-3-Embed-8B** (RTEB 75.45, OpenMDW, sliceable to 1024), with **Qwen3-Reranker-8B** (MMTEB-R 72.94) as the reranker via a correctly converted GGUF; Harrier-27B scores higher (74.3) but its causal/last-token attention is forced to bidirectional by llama.cpp's `gemma-embedding` loader, so it is not faithful at the pin ([named_companion notes](../research_notes/ATLAS%20specialist%20LLM%20arsenal/named_companion_engines.md)). An 8-bit embedder costs only ~0.2–0.6 points, so Q8_0 is the right rung. **EmbeddingGemma 2** earns a place only as an optional code/multimodal side index, after the re-pin. For **documents**, a sub-2 GB OCR model (TeleOCR 1.415B OmniDocBench 96.91, OvisOCR2 0.853B 96.47, or PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 — the last two run at the pin) beats every 70B+ engine on scanned-document transcription and should sit resident beside the text engine ([G8](../research_notes/ATLAS%20specialist%20LLM%20arsenal/completeness_review.md)). For **speech**, the incumbent Whisper-large-v3-turbo (WER 8.39) is about 3 points behind the best open small models (Phi-4-multimodal 5.02, Qwen3-ASR-1.7B 5.75), a companion-quality gap worth closing on a node WER test. For **diarisation**, adopt **Nemotron 3 Diarization** via NeMo-Speech.cpp (OpenBench DER 0.19), keeping pyannote community-1 for more than eight speakers. The **router** stays **Qwen3.5-4B** — there is no newer sub-10B model in that family, and it is a classifier, not a specialist seat affected by the 27B floor.

## What changed against the earlier report, and the gaps a node test must close

The earlier report, written under co-residency with small models allowed, reached different conclusions that the new directives overturn. It made **gpt-oss-120b the Corporate default** because its 63 GB left room for a co-resident vision engine; with co-residency gone and quality first, gpt-oss drops to a fast-utility role and GLM-5.3-Flash takes the quality seats. It proposed **Qwen3.8-27B as a coder seat and Gemma 4 31B as a light vision tier**; both are below the 27B floor and are removed, replaced by Qwen3-Coder-Next (coder) and a full vision engine (GLM `glm5v` or Qwen3-VL-235B). It kept the vision engine small for pairing; it can now be a 150 GiB engine loaded alone. Its Apex recommendation — keep DeepSeek-V4-Flash, move to the lossless UD-Q8_K_XL, end the KV ladder at q8_0 — is **strengthened** and carried forward, and the medical cross-check (**Baichuan-M3**) is confirmed with an official GGUF. The facts it verified stand where re-checked (the pin date, the bit-exact V4 file, the KV-cache ladder, the MXFP4 lossless-expert reasoning); its *recommendations* are superseded wherever co-residency or the 27B seats drove them. The one genuinely new structural recommendation is that, because engines now run alone, the arsenal can be **larger and more specialised** — a science seat (Intern-S1), a finance seat (Ling-3.0-flash-Fin), and a diversity pair for Deep Think — none of which the memory allowed before.

The evidence gaps a node-side test must close are concrete and few. **No decode, prefill, compute-buffer or KL-divergence measurement of any candidate exists on the PRO 495 at v0.4.1 or any re-pin**, so the GLM-at-IQ4_XS quality question, the V4-Flash Vulkan compute buffer, the GMKtec buyer's "15 tok/s" Flash-Next anomaly, and the GTT/Vulkan heap size (whether it is ~176–189 GiB on Linux or the vendor's 149 GiB cap) are all open. **No mainline KLD or task data exists for the fitting rungs** of Qwen3-235B/Baichuan-M3/Intern-S1 (Q5_K_S), Nemotron, Coder-Next or any abliterated GGUF against its own BF16 — the node's `llama-perplexity --kl-divergence` run is the only way to put all candidates on one scale. **No refusal or capability data** exists for the large 2026 abliterations (GLM, Flash-Next, V4). **No evaluation exists** for Australian law, HR, aviation, logistics, AEC, SCADA, game design or physical security, so those seats rest on the Principal's own test sets. The pin-versus-re-pin **output-parity test** is required, not optional, because a Vulkan correctness bug fixed after the pin may affect candidates depending on the `-fa` setting. TTS was not re-evaluated, and the node's sustained thermal and P-MODE behaviour under hours-long generation is unmeasured anywhere.

## Conclusion

The move to sequential, full-budget operation does not admit a new flagship — the open frontier is still 0.5–3 trillion parameters, two or three bits beyond this node — but it does let the arsenal specialise in a way 128 GB and co-residency never allowed: a science engine, a finance engine, a lossless reasoning engine and a diversity pair for Deep Think can all exist on disk and load in turn. The decisive and uncomfortable finding is that the Principal's two strongest instincts now pull against each other at the very top of the arsenal. "Top results" names GLM-5.3-Flash, which wins almost every professional board; "less quantisation" names DeepSeek-V4-Flash, the only engine that fits bit-exact. Which one leads the Principal's most important seats is not an argument to settle on paper — it is a single `llama-perplexity --kl-divergence` run on the node that measures how much of GLM's lead survives its 4-bit rung, and until that run exists, allocating the lossless V4-Flash to the reasoning seats and the lossy-but-stronger GLM to the knowledge seats is the honest hedge. The rest of the arsenal is settled by the evidence: exclude the two 2025-generation names and the misinformation artefact, include the four 2026 engines and the two companions, upgrade retrieval and diarisation, and accept that the seats with no benchmark — aviation, OSINT, Australian law, HR — can only be proven on the Principal's own work.

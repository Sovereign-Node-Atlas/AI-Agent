# Keep the core engines, refresh the companions

The four core LLM seats survive the move to 192 GB: **gpt-oss-120b (MXFP4), Nemotron 3 Super 120B-A12B (Q8_0), Qwen3.5-122B-A10B (Q8_0) and DeepSeek V4 Flash 0731 remain the newest open-weight checkpoints of their lines that fit the node's ~164 GiB engine budget**, and nothing released since the plan was drawn beats them on the Principal's precision-first terms. **DeepSeek V4.1 Flash exists** (MIT, 10 September 2026) but is a 552B model whose smallest usable GGUF is 246 GiB and whose llama.cpp support is still an unmerged pull request, so it is unusable here; **Qwen 3.8 exists** (August 2026) and two of its three open members fit, the 27B dense and the 125B-A6B Flash-Next. The real gains are at the edges of the roster: a bit-exact DeepSeek file only 7 GB larger than the current one, retiring the two slowest and weakest-evidenced engines (Qwen2.5-VL-72B at ~3 tok/s and Meditron-70B on a 2023 Llama-2 base) in favour of natively multimodal Qwen checkpoints and a 2026 medical post-train, a resident 0.9B OCR model that outscores every general VLM on document benchmarks, a retrieval pair 11 to 15 MTEB points above bge-m3, and speech and image upgrades that are licence-clean and already supported by llama.cpp. Two cautions govern everything: the pinned llama.cpp v0.4.1 (14 September 2026) predates GLM-5.3-Flash, MiMo-V2.6 and MiniCPM-V 4.7 support, and almost no candidate carries an independent benchmark score on a current index, so every recommendation below is a hypothesis for the node's own V10 measurements rather than a settled result, and none of it changes the repository.

**How sources are marked.** The proxy refused Hugging Face, arXiv, Artificial Analysis and most vendor sites during the research, so a citation followed by *excerpt* was read only as a search-engine summary of that page and should be re-checked before it is relied on. Unmarked citations are pages read in full: GitHub repositories, llama.cpp pull requests and source files, leaderboard data files, and the node's own repository (DOC = `docs/ATLAS_FRAMEWORK_REVIEW.md` v0.3.5, EJ = `scripts/day1/config/engines.json`). Sizes are decimal GB for files and GiB for the budget, as the Arbiter itself distinguishes them ([CFG L322-L331](../scripts/day1/orchestrator/src/atlas/config.py#L322)).

## DeepSeek V4.1 and Qwen 3.8 are real, but only Qwen 3.8 fits

The Principal asked whether "DeepSeek V4.1" and "Qwen 3.8" exist and are usable; the answer splits. **DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash was unveiled on 10 September 2026** as an "asymmetric Causal Encoder–Decoder" model with roughly 552B parameters in the backbone (763B with the vision encoder), 8B parameters active while reading input and 16B while generating, image input and a 1M-token context, released on Hugging Face under MIT the same day ([tproger](https://tproger.ru/news/deepseek-vypustila-multimodalnuyu-model-v4-1-flash-i-nachala-za), excerpt; [ccleaks](https://ccleaks.com/news/deepseek-v4-1-flash-open-weights-sep-2026), excerpt). It is not usable on this node for two independent reasons. First, size: the llama.cpp conversion author reports **Q2_K at 246.3 GiB and Q3_K_M at 323.4 GiB**, with Q2_K already "the lowest usable rung" and a Q8_0 staging file of 507.9 GB ([PR #28696](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/28696)); even the lowest rung is 80 GiB over the budget. Second, support: that pull request ("model : add DeepSeek V4.1 (DeepseekV41ForCausalLM)") was still open on 13 October 2026, is "conversion only" with runtime living on fork branches, maps neither the MTP head nor the vision tower, and carries an unresolved CUDA illegal-memory-access report and a note that "tg perf degradation when context is increasing" ([PR #28696](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/28696)). The "DeepSeek V4.1 1.6T, June 2026" entry that circulates on one catalogue is labelled "Rumored" there and has no model card ([DataLearner](https://www.datalearner.com/en/ai-models/pretrained-models/deepseek-v4-1), excerpt); no "V4.1-Pro" open release was found, and DeepSeek rerouted V4-Pro API traffic to V4.1-Flash from 14 September, which suggests none is coming soon ([tproger](https://tproger.ru/news/deepseek-vypustila-multimodalnuyu-model-v4-1-flash-i-nachala-za), excerpt). The node's planned engine, **DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731 (284B-A13B, MIT), therefore remains the newest DeepSeek checkpoint that fits**; its mainline llama.cpp support merged on 29 June 2026 ([PR #24162](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/24162)) and the node's pin already requires build b9840 for it ([EJ L174](../scripts/day1/config/engines.json#L174)). A DeepSeek-V4-Flash-Vision-Exp checkpoint (weights 31 August 2026, MIT, ~304.6B parameters, ~168 GB of repo data) also exists but is an experimental vision variant with no llama.cpp evidence in any note ([digitalapplied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/deepseek-v4-flash-vision-weights-mit), excerpt).

**Qwen 3.8 is confirmed, not a slip for 3.5 or 3.6**, and it is the only open Qwen generation newer than the Qwen3.5 family the node pins. The QwenLM GitHub README records Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B released on 12 August 2026 and Qwen3.8-27B on 14 August ([QwenLM/Qwen3.8](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3.8)), and a separate repository records **Qwen3.8-Flash-Next (26 August 2026): "a 125B-parameter main model, plus an additional 51B of N-gram embedding parameters, with 6B parameters activated per token"**, described by Qwen as "an early preview of the architecture used in Qwen4", with the n-gram table "offloadable to host memory via asynchronous prefetching" and llama.cpp support for text and vision ([QwenLM/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next)). The 2.4T model is out of envelope and text-only under a custom "Qwen3.8-Max License" ([sqmagazine](https://sqmagazine.co.uk/qwen3-8-open-weights-two-licenses/), excerpt). The 27B is a native multimodal dense model with 262K context that Qwen says "outperforms Qwen3.7-Plus overall", under Apache 2.0 per Qwen's own announcement ([Qwen on X](https://x.com/Alibaba_Qwen/status/2088280182356611304), excerpt); its BF16 safetensors are about 55.6 GB, so Q8_0 lands near 29 GB ([OrcaRouter](https://www.orcarouter.ai/blog/qwen-3-8-27b-huggingface), excerpt). Flash-Next's llama.cpp support merged on 27 August 2026 as the `qwen4exp` architecture, and the pull request is the best primary description of what the node would be taking on: a BF16 file of 337.6 GiB of which the per-layer embedding table is 97.7 GiB, Gated DeltaNet on three of every four layers, 512 experts with top-10 routing, Qwen Sparse Attention whose output "diverges on 3 percent of positions" at 8,192 tokens, quantised results that differ measurably from the reference, and an MTP head that was "still WIP" at merge ([PR #27742](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/27742)). Follow-ups halved the indexer memory on 3 October and let the model accept image embeddings for MTP on 13 October 2026, both after the node's pin ([PR list](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pulls?q=is%3Apr+Qwen3.8+OR+Qwen3.6+OR+Qwen3.5+OR+%22Muse+Glimmer%22+OR+Trinity+OR+Ling)). Between 3.5 and 3.8, Qwen3.6 shipped only a 35B-A3B (16 April) and a 27B dense (22 April 2026), and Qwen3.7 never shipped open weights at all ([QwenLM/Qwen3.8](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3.8); [Fireworks](https://www.fireworks.ai/models/fireworks/qwen3p7-plus), excerpt). **The last open ~120B-class Qwen MoE therefore remains Qwen3.5-122B-A10B unless Flash-Next is treated as its successor**, and the two usable Qwen 3.8 members are the 27B (as a dense precision, vision and coding engine) and Flash-Next (as a challenger to the default seat), both examined below.

The same check was run on every other line the Principal might expect to have moved. OpenAI's gpt-oss README still lists only the 120b and 20b checkpoints with no changelog, and the one blog claiming a June 2026 refresh contradicts the official specifications ([openai/gpt-oss](https://github.com/openai/gpt-oss); [frankx.ai](https://www.frankx.ai/blog/gpt-oss-analysis-2026), excerpt). NVIDIA released Nemotron 3 Ultra 550B-A55B (4 June 2026, too large at an estimated 330 GB even at 4-bit), Nemotron 3.5 Lightning 30B-A3B (11 August) and a Nemotron-3-Puzzle-75B-A9B with llama.cpp support from 3 September 2026, while Nemotron 4 is "in training" with no date ([PR #25444](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/25444); [BNN Bloomberg](https://beta.bnnbloomberg.ca/business/company-news/2026/08/11/nvidia-building-1-trillion-parameter-nemotron-4-to-rival-open-ai-models/), excerpt). Zhipu's GLM-5.3-Flash (320B-A18B, MIT, late August 2026) is the one GLM near the envelope, and its llama.cpp support merged on 30 September 2026 with MTP on 7 October, both after the pin ([PR #27773](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/27773)). Mistral Large 4 weights are promised "by the end of October 2026" at ~1.05T parameters ([kingy.ai](https://kingy.ai/blog/mistral-large-4-specs-benchmarks-pricing/), excerpt), Meta's only 2026 open model is the ~28B dense Muse Glimmer ([PR #26841](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/26841)), and the open-weight leaders on the current Artificial Analysis index, MiMo-V2.6-Pro (46), GLM-5.3 (45) and Kimi K3 (44), are all 744B or larger ([AA leaderboard](https://artificialanalysis.ai/leaderboards/models), excerpt). The frontier has moved to 0.5 to 3T-parameter mixtures that no 192 GB machine can hold at a precision the Principal would accept, which is why the recommendations that follow concentrate on precision, companions and the two Qwen 3.8 members rather than on a new flagship.

## The 164 GiB budget admits 300B mixtures only at 3-bit, and KV q4_0 is unsafe for the Apex engine

The node's envelope is fixed by design figures, not measurements: MemTotal of about 187 to 188 GiB once the crash-kernel reservation is removed, minus a 6 to 8 GiB GPU resident set and a fixed 16 GiB host reserve, gives **about 164 GiB for engines**, with at most two engines resident and one generating ([DOC L231](../docs/ATLAS_FRAMEWORK_REVIEW.md#L231); [DOC L252](../docs/ATLAS_FRAMEWORK_REVIEW.md#L252)). The Apex envelope is 155 GB of weights with every rung of its KV ladder projected at 160.3 GiB or less, leaving "about 3 to 5 GiB to spare", and the everyday pair of gpt-oss-120b plus the vision engine is projected at 157.7 GiB with caches ([DOC L231](../docs/ATLAS_FRAMEWORK_REVIEW.md#L231); [DOC L237](../docs/ATLAS_FRAMEWORK_REVIEW.md#L237)). The community has not yet qualified this class of machine: the most careful Strix Halo guide states "No 192GB-class profile is qualified. The 128GB values must not be reused on 192GB systems" and its setup script stops above about 136 GiB of visible RAM ([strix-halo-guide](https://github.com/hogeheer499-commits/strix-halo-guide/blob/main/STRIX_HALO_LOCAL_LLM_SETUP.md)), and the only 192 GB measurement found anywhere is a GLM-5.3-Flash GGUF "sized to run fully on the GPU of a 192 GB Framework Desktop" at about 143.5 GiB, decoding at **17.4 tok/s with 256 tok/s prefill** on Vulkan, whose card suggests a TTM setting that "gives the GPU 176 GiB" ([cafonez/GLM-5.3-Flash-Gorgon-GGUF](https://huggingface.co/cafonez/GLM-5.3-Flash-Gorgon-GGUF), excerpt). That 176 GiB cap minus compute buffers and server RSS is consistent with the node's own 164 GiB figure, so the budget below is treated as sound but unmeasured.

What "less quantisation" buys depends entirely on how a model was trained. On llama.cpp's own reference tables, **Q8_0 is indistinguishable from the 16-bit source (mean KL divergence 0.0014, 97.7% identical top tokens on Llama-3-8B), Q6_K is within noise (KLD 0.0055, 96.0%), and Q4_K_M costs a visible KLD of 0.028 and 8% of argmax tokens** on an 8B dense model ([llama.cpp perplexity README](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/tools/perplexity/README.md)). The penalty shrinks with scale: NVIDIA measures INT4-AWQ MMLU loss of 5.66% at 8B but 1.07% at 70B ([NVIDIA Model-Optimizer benchmark](https://github.com/NVIDIA/Model-Optimizer/blob/main/examples/benchmark.md)), and on a BF16-trained 355B mixture (GLM-4.6) the measured ladder runs from +0.9% perplexity at IQ4_K (211 GB) to +2.5% at IQ4_KS (196 GB) and +4.2 to 5.7% at the 151 to 170 GB three-bit rungs that would fit this node ([ik_llama.cpp #715](https://github.com/ikawrakow/ik_llama.cpp/discussions/715)). Reasoning tasks amplify what perplexity hides: a hosted-endpoint audit found gpt-oss-120b served through FP8 "Turbo" paths lost 5.5 MATH-500 and 9.0 AIME-25 points against BF16 endpoints "even where MMLU-style smoke tests show no gap" ([Token Arena, arXiv 2605.00300](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.00300), excerpt). For natively low-precision models the question largely disappears: gpt-oss ships its experts in MXFP4 so every GGUF label is 62.6 to 63.4 GB ([openclawdc](https://openclawdc.com/blog/mxfp4-vs-q4-k-m/), excerpt, consistent with the `MXFP4_MOE` rule in [llama-quant.cpp](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/src/llama-quant.cpp)), and **DeepSeek V4 Flash's Unsloth UD-Q8_K_XL (161.9 GB) matches the official weights bit-for-bit on all 1,328 tensors while UD-Q4_K_XL (155.1 GB) re-quantises only the non-expert 4% and measures KLD 0.0102 with 96.28% identical top tokens** ([Unsloth DeepSeek-V4](https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/deepseek-v4), excerpt). The only precision-versus-precision benchmark anchor found is Artificial Analysis running Nemotron 3 Ultra at BF16 (48.2) and NVFP4 (47.7), a half-point gap on a 550B model ([AA Nemotron 3 Ultra](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/nvidia-nemotron-3-ultra-released), excerpt). The practical rule for this roster is therefore: the gpt-oss pair are already lossless, DeepSeek V4 Flash becomes lossless for 7 GB more, Nemotron and Qwen3.5 at Q8_0 are at release precision, and any BF16-trained 300B-class newcomer (GLM-5.3-Flash, Hy3, Qwen3.5-397B) can only enter at a 3-bit rung whose measured cost on comparable models is 4 to 6% perplexity, which is the wrong direction for the Principal's fourth requirement.

The KV-cache evidence changes one design detail. Measured on llama.cpp, **q8_0 for both K and V is lossless at the task level** (at most 4 of 500 ARC answers change on any model, zero flips across 1,600 deep-context comparisons up to 41K tokens, KLD around 0.002 to 0.006), whereas **q4_0 on the K cache is the dangerous setting**: Qwen2.5-7B collapsed from 92.0% to 24.2% on ARC with q4_0 K alone, while q4_0 on V alone changed one answer ([llama.cpp discussion #23470](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/23470)). Since 1 April 2026 llama.cpp rotates Q, K and V with a Hadamard transform before caching, which roughly halves q4_0's divergence and restored gpt-oss-20b's AIME25 score at q4_0 from 2.0% to 21.7% (f16: 37.9%), but the rotation is explicitly "not applied to MLA models" ([PR #21038](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/21038)). DeepSeek V4 is an MLA-family model, and the node's Apex KV ladder is f16 then q8_0 then q4_0 with four proof lines ([EJ L141-L177](../scripts/day1/config/engines.json#L141)); the bottom rung therefore runs without the protection that makes q4_0 tolerable on the GQA engines, and a DeepSeek-V4-Flash test found even q8_0 KV "materially changing token choices" at short context ([breachprotocol](https://dev.to/breachprotocol/quantizing-v4-flashs-kv-cache-in-llamacpp-changes-which-tokens-it-picks-38np), excerpt). The research supports stopping the Apex ladder at q8_0 and spending memory, not cache bits, on the lossless weight file. For the hybrid engines (Nemotron, Qwen3.5) KV quantisation buys little because only their few attention layers hold a growing cache and the recurrent state stays f32 ([llama.cpp discussion #23470](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/23470); [arbiter.py](../scripts/day1/orchestrator/src/atlas/arbiter.py)).

### Measured decode on this silicon, and where the plan's projections are exposed

Every speed figure below comes from 128 GB Ryzen AI Max+ 395 systems with the same 40-CU gfx1151 GPU; the PRO 495's LPDDR5X-8533 raises bandwidth from 256 to about 273 GB/s, a 6.6% ceiling lift and nothing more ([VideoCardz](https://videocardz.com/newz/framework-desktop-with-ryzen-ai-max-pro-495-gets-6-6-higher-memory-bandwidth-and-192gb-ram), excerpt). On Vulkan/RADV, **gpt-oss-120b MXFP4 decodes at 53.8 to 55.6 tok/s with prompt processing of 727 to 1,407 tok/s depending on build and micro-batch**, falling to 40.6 tok/s at 32K context and 29.1 tok/s on a real 87,000-token request ([huppiflupp](https://github.com/huppiflupp/strix-halo-llm-speeds); [strix-halo-guide BENCHMARKS](https://github.com/hogeheer499-commits/strix-halo-guide/blob/main/BENCHMARKS.md)). **Qwen3.5-122B-A10B runs 22.1 tok/s at UD-Q4_K_XL (31.0 with MTP) and 19.1 tok/s at UD-Q6_K_XL on ROCm 7.2.3 (26.9 with `--spec-type draft-mtp`)** ([huppiflupp](https://github.com/huppiflupp/strix-halo-llm-speeds); [discussion #23659](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/23659)); the document's 15 to 17 tok/s band for Q8_0 is consistent with bandwidth scaling from those two points. Nemotron 3 Super measured 18.9 tok/s at UD-IQ4_XS, so the document's 12 to 14 tok/s at Q8_0 is plausible ([strix-halo-guide BENCHMARKS](https://github.com/hogeheer499-commits/strix-halo-guide/blob/main/BENCHMARKS.md)). Among the newcomers, **Qwen3.8-Flash-Next at UD-IQ4_XS decodes 26.9 tok/s bare and 41.3 tok/s with MTP on Vulkan, and 43.9 tok/s on the kyuz0 ROCm 10.0 image**, while Qwen3.8-27B dense Q8_0 manages 7.3 tok/s (12.3 at Q4_K_M) bare and 26 to 37 tok/s with MTP under agent load ([huppiflupp](https://github.com/huppiflupp/strix-halo-llm-speeds); [kyuz0 toolboxes](https://github.com/kyuz0/amd-strix-halo-toolboxes); [sypherin](https://github.com/sypherin/strix-halo-setup/blob/master/README.md)). GLM-5.3-Flash at UD-IQ1_S ran 17.6 tok/s (25.0 with MTP, `-fa off` required), MiMo-V2.6-Flash at 2 bits 27.8 tok/s, and Mistral Small 4 35.3 tok/s but only after a Vulkan patch; the stock build ran its expert down-projections on the CPU at 0.82 tok/s ([huppiflupp](https://github.com/huppiflupp/strix-halo-llm-speeds)).

The exposed projection is the Apex engine. The document bands DeepSeek V4 Flash at **25 to 32 tok/s** ([DOC L311](../docs/ATLAS_FRAMEWORK_REVIEW.md#L311)), but the only measurements on this GPU are at far lower bit-widths: **UD-IQ2_XXS (91 GB) reached 18.9 tok/s on a tuned fork and 11.9 tok/s on mainline b10488, and UD-IQ3_XXS (104 GB) 18.4 tok/s**, with DSpark drafts lifting the fork to 28.9 but slowing mainline to 7.7 ([huppiflupp](https://github.com/huppiflupp/strix-halo-llm-speeds)). A 155 GB file reads more bytes per token than either, so the UD-Q4_K_XL build is likely to land in the low-to-mid teens on mainline Vulkan, under the lower bound Phase 3 fails on ([P3 L33](../scripts/day1/phase3-models.sh#L33)). This is a research finding for V10 to settle, not a reason to drop the engine: quality is the Principal's first criterion and the Apex seat is reserved for Deep Think and TF_OMEGA work where 12 tok/s is tolerable. The DeepSeek V4 pull request also lists "load failures on SYCL/Vulkan/ROCm" among reported issues, so the first load on the node's Vulkan build is itself a test ([PR #24162](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/24162)).

Backend state supports the node's Vulkan choice. The HIP build of llama.cpp produced cross-request corruption and garbled output on gfx1151 integrated GPUs across ROCm 7.2.1 to 7.14.0 while Vulkan builds were unaffected, and the issue was auto-closed "not planned" on 9 October 2026 with AMD's fix still unmerged ([llama.cpp issue #25992](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/25992)); like-for-like runs give Vulkan the decode win by 10 to 25% and HIP the long-prompt prefill win by a similar margin ([strix-halo-guide BENCHMARKS](https://github.com/hogeheer499-commits/strix-halo-guide/blob/main/BENCHMARKS.md)). Three Vulkan-specific traps matter for candidates: the DeepSeek-style hyper-connection operator used by Kimi K3 and GLM-5.3-Flash is "CPU + CUDA only, so Metal/Vulkan will fall back per-node" ([PR #26185](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/26185); [PR #27773](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/27773)); Nemotron 3 Puzzle's Vulkan decode "collapses on 4-bit MoE quants, while Q8_0 works" ([PR #25444](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/25444)); and GLM-5.3-Flash requires `--kv-unified` for multiple sequences, which the node's eight-slot design deliberately does not pass ([PR #27773](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/27773); [DOC L277](../docs/ATLAS_FRAMEWORK_REVIEW.md#L277)). Native MTP speculative decoding is the one free speed-up: 1.4 to 1.6 times on the MoE engines and 2 to 3 times on dense 27B models, whereas separate draft models consistently slow this bandwidth-bound part (gpt-oss-120b with EAGLE3 fell from 51.7 to 27.7 tok/s) ([huppiflupp](https://github.com/huppiflupp/strix-halo-llm-speeds); [llama.cpp speculative docs](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/docs/speculative.md)).

One reconciliation is needed before any candidate is judged "supported". The node pins llama.cpp tag v0.4.1 at commit b29c606, recorded as "VERIFIED (2026-09-14)" ([L01 L27-L29](../scripts/day1/phase2/01-llama.sh#L27)), and the llama.cpp pull-request list shows the 0.4.1 version bump as PR #28900 on 14 September 2026, followed by 0.5.0 on 23 September and 0.6.0 on 5 October ([PR list](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pulls?q=is%3Apr+MiMo+OR+Kimi+OR+KDA)). The companion-model note instead inferred a mid-June build from the b9840 minimum tag and build cadence; the primary PR record outranks that inference, so this report treats the pin as **14 September 2026**. On that date the pin already contains Qwen3.5 vision (February), Gemma 4 vision and audio (April), Step-3.7-Flash (2 June), DeepSeek V4 (29 June), Nemotron 3 Nano Omni (28 July), Qwen3-TTS (4 August), Nemotron MTP (10 August), Qwen3.8-Flash-Next (27 August) and Nemotron Puzzle (3 September), but not MiMo-V2.6 (22 September), GLM-5.3-Flash (30 September), the Flash-Next indexer and image-MTP follow-ups (3 and 13 October), GLM5-Next MTP (7 October) or MiniCPM-V 4.7 (10 October) ([PR #27742](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/27742); [PR #27773](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/27773); [PR #29257](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/29257)). A follow-up must confirm the commit date directly; everything below assumes it.

**The Corporate default stays gpt-oss-120b because nothing in the envelope beats it on the combination the seat needs.** It is the fastest engine on this silicon by a factor of two over any MoE with ten or more active billions ([huppiflupp](https://github.com/huppiflupp/strix-halo-llm-speeds)), it is already at its native precision so the Principal's fourth requirement is satisfied by definition ([DOC L315](../docs/ATLAS_FRAMEWORK_REVIEW.md#L315)), its 63 GB is what makes a co-resident partner possible at all, and its card numbers (GPQA Diamond 80.1 at high effort, HealthBench 57.6, HealthBench Hard 30.0) remain respectable ([gpt-oss model card](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.10925), excerpt). The honest weakness is that the intelligence indexes have moved past it: on Artificial Analysis v4.3.2 it scores 12 against ~34 for Qwen3.8-27B and 39 for DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, and even on the older index where it scored 33.3, Gemma 4 31B (39.2) and Nemotron 3 Super (36.0) sat above it ([AA gpt-oss-120b](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/gpt-oss-120b), excerpt; [AA Nemotron 3 Ultra](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/nvidia-nemotron-3-ultra-released), excerpt; [respan](https://www.respan.ai/articles/qwen-3.8-models-comparison), excerpt). The one credible challenger for the seat is **Qwen3.8-Flash-Next**: a newer architecture, vision-capable, 26.9 to 43.9 tok/s measured, 262K context, and a vendor SWE-bench Pro of 62.5 against 61.7 for the 27B ([codersera](https://codersera.com/blog/qwen-3-8-flash-complete-guide-2026/), excerpt). Against it stand four unknowns: no independent score, a licence that the GitHub README does not state ([QwenLM/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next)), the 51B n-gram table whose memory placement decides whether a Q6 or Q8 build fits beside anything (the IQ4_XS build already spilled 27.1 GiB onto host RAM at full context on a 128 GB box), and the QSA sparse-attention divergence and quantisation drift recorded in its own pull request ([PR #27742](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/27742)). A head-to-head on the node, gpt-oss-120b against Flash-Next at the highest quant that fits, is the right next step; promoting Qwen3.5-122B-A10B Q8_0 to the Corporate default is the fallback that needs no new download but would halve everyday decode and forbid the vision pairing.

**The Estate default stays Nemotron 3 Super at Q8_0.** NVIDIA shipped nothing between 75B and 550B after it, the Super's Q8_0 is at or above its release precision, and the two independent data points found both favour it: 36.0 on the Artificial Analysis index of its launch month against 33.3 for gpt-oss-120b on the same version ([AA Nemotron 3 Super](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/nvidia-nemotron-3-super-the-new-leader-in-open-efficient-intelligence), excerpt), and a 0.4% refusal rate on 141 benign biology research prompts, where Kimi K2.6 refused 91 to 95% ([RefusalBench, arXiv 2605.21545](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21545), excerpt), which matters for an Estate hemisphere that handles medical and security material without the abliterated engine. Its MTP support merged inside the pin on 10 August 2026 ([PR list](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pulls?q=is%3Apr+%22MiMo-V2.6%22+OR+%22MiMo-V2.5%22+OR+mimo2+sort%3Acreated-asc)), so the 12 to 14 tok/s band may improve without memory cost. The alternative worth a look is **Nemotron-3-Puzzle-75B-A9B**, a newer NVIDIA Labs hybrid (40 Mamba-2, 8 attention and 40 MoE blocks) whose Q8_0 at roughly 80 GB would sit beside gpt-oss-120b and restore the Ren-plus-Arthur co-residency the document gave up at 186 GB ([PR #25444](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/25444); [DOC L241](../docs/ATLAS_FRAMEWORK_REVIEW.md#L241)); but its licence and release date were not retrievable, it has no benchmark anywhere in the notes, and on RADV gfx1151 only its Q8_0 decodes correctly. It is a watch-list item, not a recommendation.

**The Apex seat keeps DeepSeek V4 Flash 0731, and the upgrade is the file, not the model.** The lossless UD-Q8_K_XL is 6.8 GB larger than the pinned UD-Q4_K_XL and differs only in the 4% of tensors that are not experts, yet the pinned file's KLD of 0.0102 is the same order as Q5_K_M on a dense model, which is exactly the "measurable but small" band the Principal asked to leave behind ([Unsloth DeepSeek-V4](https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/deepseek-v4), excerpt; [llama.cpp perplexity README](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/tools/perplexity/README.md)). The document already records the Q8 file as a post-Day-1 experiment that "would leave about 2 GiB for the KV cache where Q4 leaves about 9" ([DOC L317](../docs/ATLAS_FRAMEWORK_REVIEW.md#L317)); that arithmetic reads the 161.9 GB file as 161.9 GiB, the Arbiter's deliberate over-estimate ([CFG L322-L331](../scripts/day1/orchestrator/src/atlas/config.py#L322)), while the decimal file is about 150.8 GiB, and the f16 cache at the 16K cap is itself an unverified projection ([ARB L143-L144](../scripts/day1/orchestrator/src/atlas/arbiter.py#L143)). Whether the lossless file fits with a q8_0 cache at 16K to 32K is therefore a measurement the `kv-estimates` to-do will produce, and the research says it is worth producing. Two further points: the KV ladder should end at q8_0 because the MLA architecture gets no Hadamard protection ([PR #21038](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/21038)), and the 25 to 32 tok/s projection is unsupported by any measurement on this GPU, as set out above. The alternatives considered all fail the precision rule: **Qwen3.5-397B-A17B** has the best independent evidence of any in-envelope model, leading Claude Opus 4.5 and GPT-5.2 on tau2-bench retail (84.4) and telecom (97.8) in the same Sierra run, but scoring 9.8 on banking knowledge ([tau2 manifest](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/sierra-research/tau2-bench/main/web/leaderboard/public/submissions/manifest.json)), and it fits only at UD-IQ3_XXS (140.3 GB), a three-bit rung on a BF16-trained mixture ([openclawdc](https://openclawdc.com/blog/ryzen-ai-max-pro-495-192gb-wait-or-buy/), excerpt); **GLM-5.3-Flash** fits only at UD-Q3_K_XL (147.5 GB), needs a llama.cpp newer than the pin, falls back to CPU for its hyper-connection operator on Vulkan, requires `--kv-unified` for multiple slots, and slowed to about 6 tok/s by 128K context in review ([PR #27773](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/27773)); Hy3 and MiMo-V2.6-Flash are at or over the edge. If the Principal ever wants a second Apex-class opinion, the 397B at three bits is the one to test, because its tau2 evidence is primary and its licence is Apache 2.0.

**The long-document seat stays Qwen3.5-122B-A10B Q8_0 and gains a second job.** It is the only engine in the roster whose checkpoint is natively multimodal; llama.cpp runs it with vision when the roughly 1 GB mmproj is added, through the same Qwen3-VL encoder path merged on 10 February 2026, with the caveat that "DeepStack was temporarily removed" ([PR #19468](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/19468)). Its self-reported MMMU 83.9 and OCRBench 92.1 sit above Qwen3-VL-235B's 80.6 and 87.5 and far above Qwen2.5-VL-72B's MMMU 70.2 ([Qwen/Qwen3.5-122B-A10B](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-122B-A10B), excerpt; [Qwen3-VL table](https://gist.github.com/karminski/aa56b5b73e2f3070a7530dfe11c2ddb3); [debuggercafe](https://debuggercafe.com/qwen2-5-vl/), excerpt), and it decodes five to seven times faster than the dense 72B. The ceiling on its long-document value is the node's own: without `--kv-unified` each slot is a hard 32K request limit, so the 262K native window is unused until that reserved to-do is revisited ([DOC L274-L275](../docs/ATLAS_FRAMEWORK_REVIEW.md#L274)). Qwen3.8-Flash-Next ran at a 262,144-token context on a 128 GB box ([huppiflupp](https://github.com/huppiflupp/strix-halo-llm-speeds)), so it is the natural successor here too if the bake-off favours it.

**A coder seat is new and the evidence for it is weaker than for any other row.** The 2026 coding boards are led by generalists that do not fit (GLM-5.2 at 84.2 SWE-bench Verified vendor, Kimi K3 at 88.3 Terminal-Bench 2.1), and the official SWE-bench Verified data with a fixed harness put open models 15 to 20 points below their vendor tables (Devstral 53.8 against a claimed 72.2; Qwen3-Coder-480B 55.4) ([swe-bench.github.io](https://github.com/SWE-bench/swe-bench.github.io); [GLM-5 README](https://github.com/zai-org/GLM-5); [Kimi-K3 README](https://github.com/MoonshotAI/Kimi-K3)). Within the envelope the choice is between a dense 27B that is newest and natively multimodal (Qwen3.8-27B: vendor SWE-bench Pro 61.7, Terminal-Bench 2.1 73.0, GPQA 89.2; Artificial Analysis ~34) and an 80B-A3B coding mixture with an official GGUF (Qwen3-Coder-Next: Aider polyglot 66.2 against 60.4 for the 480B, in its own report) ([kingy.ai](https://kingy.ai/blog/qwen3-8-27b-specs-benchmarks-local-hardware/), excerpt; [arXiv 2603.00729](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.00729), excerpt; [Qwen3-Coder README](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-Coder)). The 27B wins on precision (Q8_0 at 29 GB is trivially co-resident), on recency and on licence certainty (Apache 2.0 per Qwen), and its dense slowness is repaired by MTP, which measured 25.7 to 36.8 tok/s on Strix Halo ([sypherin](https://github.com/sypherin/strix-halo-setup/blob/master/README.md)). Devstral 2 at 123B dense would decode near 2 tok/s at Q8 and is excluded on speed, not quality.

## Companions: specialists lose everywhere except medicine, OCR and retrieval

The discipline survey found a consistent 2026 pattern: small domain fine-tunes lose to 100B-plus generalists on broad benchmarks, narrow extraction tasks still reward domain training, and the only specialists that beat frontier models are themselves frontier-scale post-trains by large labs. Fin-R1 (7B) scores 46.9 on FinReasoning against 68.0 for Qwen3-235B ([FinReasoning, arXiv 2603.19254](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.19254), excerpt); fine-tuned 8B security models "still trail frontier models like GPT-5 and Claude-4.5" ([KTH thesis](https://kth.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:2060365/FULLTEXT01.pdf), excerpt); a June 2026 Nature Medicine study found "Frontier LLMs outperformed clinical AI tools in all three evaluations" ([Nature Medicine](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04431-5), excerpt); and no open specialist of any size exists for marketing, AEC, logistics, estate security or game design. For the Principal's 36 domains this means finance, security, data science, communications and the engineering disciplines should keep their generalist owner engine plus retrieval and a code interpreter, which is the document's existing posture (C7: "hallucination is controlled by retrieval ... not by model choice") ([DOC L387](../docs/ATLAS_FRAMEWORK_REVIEW.md#L387)). Law is the one domain with a fresh specialist: **GLM Legal, released 12 October 2026, is the top open-weight model on Vals' LegalBench at about 86.2% against a closed cluster near 88.6%** ([Vals GLM Legal](https://www.vals.ai/models/zai_glm-legal), excerpt), but its parameter count, licence and GGUF status were unobtainable and it is almost certainly a GLM-5-class post-train (744B or 320B-Flash based), so it is a watch-list entry for Gideon's domain 6, not a candidate; nothing found is trained on Australian construction, strata or employment law.

**Medicine is the exception, and it argues for retiring Meditron-70B.** Meditron is a Llama-2-70B continued pre-train with an August 2023 knowledge cutoff and no instruction tuning ([epfLLM/meditron](https://github.com/epfLLM/meditron)); an independent 2026 test placed it at 52.9% on a neurology board exam, "the lowest among the 70B models evaluated", and its fully open 2026 successor Apertus-70B-MeditronFO averages 53.77 across benchmarks against 60.67 for MedGemma-27B ([micheledpierri](https://www.micheledpierri.com/2026/07/14/open-weight-medical-llms/), excerpt; [arXiv 2605.16215](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.16215), excerpt). The node spends 74 GB and a 3 tok/s dense decode on it. The specialist with real 2026 evidence is **Baichuan-M3-235B**: Apache 2.0, built on Qwen3, with a vendor-reported HealthBench of 65.1 and HealthBench-Hard of 44.4 against 42.0 for GPT-5.2-High and a 3.5% hallucination rate, served via SGLang or vLLM with an official GPTQ-INT4 and no GGUF mentioned ([Baichuan-M3 README](https://github.com/baichuan-inc/Baichuan-M3-235B); [arXiv 2602.06570](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06570), excerpt); gpt-oss-120b, Minerva's default, scores 57.6 and 30.0 on the same two measures ([gpt-oss model card](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.10925), excerpt). Three honest caveats apply: the 14-point Hard-subset gap is vendor-run and HealthBench scores move with the judge model ([InfiMed-ORBIT](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.15859), excerpt); the README is Chinese-first; and at roughly 130 to 144 GB the model fits only at Q4 and only alone, so the cross-check becomes an engine swap rather than a co-resident call, which is already how the document defines a "strong cross-check" ([DOC L581](../docs/ATLAS_FRAMEWORK_REVIEW.md#L581)). Its base is the `qwen3moe` architecture llama.cpp supports, so a community GGUF is plausible but unverified. If none exists, **MedGemma-27B** (Gemma 3 base, MedQA 86.2 "best for an open model at release, aside from DeepSeek-R1", about 28 GB at Q8_0, HAI-DEF licence that must be recorded like the gated pyannote and FLUX terms) is the cheap co-resident alternative, and the 4B multimodal MedGemma 1.5 covers chest X-ray, dermatology, ophthalmology and histopathology for Minerva's imaging work beside Rad-DINO ([Google-Health/medgemma](https://github.com/Google-Health/medgemma); [Nature Medicine MedGemma](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04626-w), excerpt).

**Vision is where the extra memory is most clearly mis-spent today.** The sole vision engine, Qwen2.5-VL-72B at Q8_0, was chosen for "maximum quality, slow speed accepted" ([DOC L998](../docs/ATLAS_FRAMEWORK_REVIEW.md#L998)); on the independent OmniDocBench v1.5 it scores 87.02 and its MMMU is 70.2, while the whole Qwen3.5 family released a year later is natively multimodal with self-reported MMMU of 82.3 (27B), 83.9 (122B) and 85.0 (397B) and OCRBench of 89.4 to 93.1 ([OmniDocBench v1.5](https://github.com/opendatalab/OmniDocBench/blob/v1_5/README.md); [Qwen/Qwen3.5-397B-A17B](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-397B-A17B), excerpt). Qwen3.8-27B inherits that line as "a native multimodal dense model" with a 262K window ([Qwen on X](https://x.com/Alibaba_Qwen/status/2088280182356611304), excerpt) and at about 29 GB Q8_0 it leaves the gpt-oss pairing with some 70 GB to spare, where the 72B consumed the whole pair budget (157.7 GiB projected). The recommended two-tier arrangement, the 27B co-resident for everyday screenshots and scans and the 122B with mmproj loaded alone for heavy documents, touches C25's "one engine" decision and is therefore the Principal's to re-open, not an automatic swap. **Gemma 4 31B is the strongest alternative for the light tier**, with the best independently retrievable vision numbers of any sub-40 GB model (MMMU-Pro 76.9, OmniDocBench edit distance 0.131, Arena text 1451) and reported Apache 2.0 licensing, but its Gemma Terms history (a Prohibited Use Policy that forbids circumventing safety filters in derivatives) needs the official card to confirm the licence change, and its dense 31B decodes at 9.7 to 10.5 tok/s at Q4 ([Gemma 4 model card](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/core/model_card_4), excerpt; [arXiv 2607.02770](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.02770v1), excerpt; [Gemma Prohibited Use Policy](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/prohibited_use_policy), excerpt; [discussion #23659](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/23659)). No vision score for Qwen3.8-27B was retrievable, so the choice between it and Gemma 4 31B must be made on the node with the Principal's own drawings and contracts; the engineering-drawing benchmarks published in 2026 (DrawingVQA, AECV-Bench, Enginuity) all report "substantial gap between model and expert performance" without per-model scores ([DrawingVQA](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.15418v1), excerpt).

**A sub-4 GB OCR model should sit permanently beside the text engines.** On OmniDocBench v1.6 the specialised 0.9 to 3B OCR models outscore every general VLM: **PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 96.34, MinerU2.5-Pro 95.75, GLM-OCR 95.22**, against 93.70 for the best general open VLM (Ovis2.6-30B-A3B), 92.91 for Gemini 3 Pro, 89.78 for Qwen3-VL-235B and 86.59 for GPT-5.2 ([OmniDocBench](https://github.com/opendatalab/OmniDocBench)). GLM-OCR is the licence-clean, llama.cpp-ready choice (code Apache 2.0, model MIT, "model: support GLM-OCR" merged 18 February 2026, well inside the pin), and llama.cpp documents the prompt structures OCR models need ([zai-org/GLM-OCR](https://github.com/zai-org/GLM-OCR); [llama.cpp multimodal docs](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/docs/multimodal.md)). PaddleOCR-VL has the top score but its repository path returned 404 and its licence could not be read. The OCR model transcribes to markdown with tables and formulas; it does not reason over a page image, so it complements the vision engine rather than replacing it, and none of these benchmarks covers handwriting or drawings. For the resident router, **Qwen3.5-4B stays**: it is the only 4B-class model with a 2026 vendor date and explicit llama.cpp text-and-vision support, Qwen 3.8 shipped no small model, and the alternatives (Gemma 4 E4B, Granite 4.1 8B) offer no measured routing advantage ([QwenLM/Qwen3.5](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3.5)). Retrieval is the quiet upgrade with the largest measured margin: **Qwen3-Embedding-8B scores 70.58 on MTEB multilingual against 59.56 for bge-m3, and Qwen3-Reranker-8B 72.94 on MMTEB-R against 58.36 for bge-reranker-v2-m3**, with the 4B variants (69.45 and 72.74) nearly as strong ([QwenLM/Qwen3-Embedding](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-Embedding)); the costs are a full re-index of Chroma and LightRAG, whose configuration is bound to bge-m3's dimension ([M04 L120-L123](../scripts/day1/phase2/04-memory.sh#L120)), and confirmation that the llama-server embedding and reranking endpoints accept these architectures, which the notes do not establish. A September 2026 comparison places Qwen3-Embedding fourth or fifth behind newer entrants that could not be identified ([presenc](https://presenc.ai/research/best-open-weight-embedding-models-2026), excerpt).

**Speech upgrades are ready and cheap.** Whisper large-v3 and turbo now trail the field on the Open ASR leaderboard: about 7.4% English short-form WER against 6.32% for Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3, 5.63% for Canary-Qwen 2.5B and 5.1 to 5.8% for Qwen3-ASR-1.7B ([ASR Leaderboard paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06961v4), excerpt). **Qwen3-ASR-1.7B is the candidate that runs on the node's existing Vulkan llama.cpp**: Apache 2.0, 52 languages, an official `ggml-org` GGUF in llama.cpp's supported list, and Qwen's own table beating Whisper-large-v3 on every English set it lists (Tedlium 4.50 vs 6.84, CommonVoice-en 7.39 vs 9.90, LibriSpeech-other 3.38 vs 3.97) ([QwenLM/Qwen3-ASR](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-ASR); [llama.cpp multimodal docs](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/docs/multimodal.md)). The alternative keeps faster-whisper and moves it off the CPU: CTranslate2 4.7.0 added ROCm HIP support on 2 February 2026 and a gfx1151 user reports large-v3 at 11.5x realtime on ROCm 7.2.2 with no source build, but nothing confirms the wheel against the node's ROCm 10.0 image ([CTranslate2 PR #1989](https://github.com/OpenNMT/CTranslate2/pull/1989); [nabe2030](https://github.com/nabe2030/faster-whisper-rocm-strix-halo)). Parakeet and Canary need NeMo, which recommends CUDA. For diarisation the node already carries the right answer as its fallback: **pyannote community-1 beats 3.1 by 1 to 4 DER points on eleven of twelve sets** (AMI-SDM 19.9 vs 22.7, DIHARD3 20.2 vs 21.4, AliMeeting 20.3 vs 24.5) under CC-BY-4.0, while DiariZen's larger gains are CC BY-NC, Sortformer is capped at four speakers and pyannoteAI precision-2/3 run on their servers, which the zero-cloud rule forbids ([pyannote/pyannote-audio](https://github.com/pyannote/pyannote-audio); [BUTSpeechFIT/DiariZen](https://github.com/BUTSpeechFIT/DiariZen)). For TTS, Kokoro keeps the routine tier (about 1064 Elo, sixth overall on the open-weights arena; on gfx1151 it runs about 20x realtime on ROCm after a one-off MIOpen warm-up fixed by `MIOPEN_FIND_MODE=2`) ([AA TTS arena](https://artificialanalysis.ai/text-to-speech/leaderboard/provider-voice/open-weights), excerpt; [Kokoro-FastAPI #454](https://github.com/remsky/Kokoro-FastAPI/issues/454)); the pinned Chatterbox 0.1.7 predates **Chatterbox Turbo (350M, one-step decoder), Nano (110M, CPU) and Multilingual V3 (23 languages), all MIT**, with community ROCm runs on RDNA3 cards but none on gfx1151 ([resemble-ai/chatterbox](https://github.com/resemble-ai/chatterbox); [chatterbox issue #445](https://github.com/resemble-ai/chatterbox/issues/445)); and **Qwen3-TTS 0.6B/1.7B** (Apache 2.0, three-second cloning, Seed-TTS WER 0.77 zh / 1.24 en) gained llama.cpp support on 4 August 2026, inside the pin, which makes the document's "test after core build" watch-list entry executable without the ROCm image ([QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS); [llama.cpp PR search](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pulls?q=is%3Apr+is%3Amerged+Qwen3-TTS+OR+parakeet+OR+granite-speech+OR+pockettts+in%3Atitle); [DOC L737](../docs/ATLAS_FRAMEWORK_REVIEW.md#L737)).

**Image generation: the licence pattern is unchanged, the practical candidates have moved.** FLUX.1-dev, FLUX.2-dev (32B, November 2025) and FLUX.2-klein-9B all carry the same non-commercial-model, commercial-outputs terms the Principal accepted ([LICENSE-FLUX1-dev](https://github.com/black-forest-labs/flux/blob/main/model_licenses/LICENSE-FLUX1-dev); [black-forest-labs/flux2](https://github.com/black-forest-labs/flux2)), and Qwen-Image-2.1 remains non-commercial per the node's watch-list ([DOC L850](../docs/ATLAS_FRAMEWORK_REVIEW.md#L850)). The permissive options are **Z-Image-Turbo (6B, Apache 2.0, "#1 open-source" on the Artificial Analysis text-to-image board as of 8 December 2025, within 16 GB)**, FLUX.2-klein-4B (Apache 2.0), Qwen-Image-2512 (20B, Apache 2.0, "strongest open-source image model" by Qwen's own arena) and HiDream-I1 (17B, MIT) ([Tongyi-MAI/Z-Image](https://github.com/Tongyi-MAI/Z-Image); [QwenLM/Qwen-Image](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen-Image); [HiDream-ai/HiDream-I1](https://github.com/HiDream-ai/HiDream-I1)). The only Strix Halo timings are for Qwen-Image: **74 to 79 s per image with the 4-step LoRA and 293 to 403 s at 20 steps BF16**, under a ComfyUI toolbox that requires `--disable-mmap` because "memory mapping above 64GB is currently very slow due to a ROCm issue" ([kyuz0 ComfyUI benchmarks](https://github.com/kyuz0/amd-strix-halo-comfyui-toolboxes/blob/main/docs/benchmark_results.json); [kyuz0 ComfyUI toolboxes](https://github.com/kyuz0/amd-strix-halo-comfyui-toolboxes)); FLUX, Z-Image and SD3.5 have no absolute gfx1151 timing anywhere, only "about two-thirds of an RTX 3090" ([ROCm issue #6611](https://github.com/ROCm/legacy-rocm-build/issues/6611)). Video is possible but not interactive (LTX-2 about 10 to 12 minutes per clip, Wan 2.2 A14B 30 minutes to hours), and a December 2025 report of constant ROCm hangs recommended stable-diffusion.cpp on Vulkan, which now supports FLUX.2, Z-Image, Qwen-Image 2.1, Wan and LTX ([leejet/stable-diffusion.cpp](https://github.com/leejet/stable-diffusion.cpp); [Medium](https://medium.com/@jmdevita/z-image-turbo-on-amd-ryzen-ai-max-395-local-ai-image-generation-with-vulkan-framework-desktop-b577b798b6ca), excerpt). Keeping FLUX.1-dev as the validated engine and adding Z-Image-Turbo as a permissive, fast tier costs about 12 GB of disk and no licence exposure; FLUX.2-dev would add a 24B Mistral text encoder and 64 GB of BF16 weights for a quality gain nobody has measured on this hardware.

## Abliterated options widen, but stay a Q4 story above 120B and cannot be made in-house

The abliterated engine remains available and its supply has grown; the quality evidence has sharpened, mostly against the larger builds. huihui-ai is the only producer with a near-complete GGUF line across every candidate family (gpt-oss-120b, Qwen3.5-122B and 397B, DeepSeek V4 Flash and 0731, GLM-5.1 through 5.3-Flash, Kimi K2.x, Mistral Small 4 and Medium 3.5, Gemma 4, Qwen3.8-27B), and Heretic-made community builds exist for gpt-oss-120b, Qwen3.5-397B, MiniMax M2.7, Gemma 4 and Qwen3.8-Flash-Next ([huihui Qwen3.5 collection](https://huggingface.co/collections/huihui-ai/qwen35-abliterated), excerpt; [Heretic README](https://github.com/p-e-w/heretic)). The node's current pick, the noctrex MXFP4_MOE re-quantisation of huihui's BF16 abliteration, is still the sensible default for Valerie: the only Heretic alternatives for gpt-oss-120b report **KL divergence of 0.53 (v2, 22/100 refusals) and 0.92 (v1)** against the original, an order of magnitude above the 0.03 to 0.16 the same tool reaches on 4B to 27B dense models, and an Ollama repackager notes 0.5 is "right at the threshold Heretic itself flags as 'may indicate significant damage'" ([kldzj/gpt-oss-120b-heretic-v2](https://huggingface.co/kldzj/gpt-oss-120b-heretic-v2), excerpt; [brianmatzelle/gpt-oss-heretic](https://ollama.com/brianmatzelle/gpt-oss-heretic), excerpt). No independent benchmark compares huihui's gpt-oss build against those or against the base model, and nobody has measured the cost of the BF16-to-MXFP4 round trip the noctrex file embodies; the document's own S1 note that "the 'no accuracy cost' reasoning applies only to the standard gpt-oss" stands ([DOC L1146](../docs/ATLAS_FRAMEWORK_REVIEW.md#L1146)). Where the two methods have been measured on the same model, they are close: on Qwen3.5-27B, Heretic 0.063 KLD and huihui 0.065 with MMLU down 0.3% for both, while reasoning loss tracks KLD almost monotonically on GSM8K and was as large as 18.81 points on one architecture ([abliterlitics](https://abliterlitics.dev/models/qwen3.5-27b/), excerpt; [sapwell](https://nathan.sapwell.net/posts/qwen3-vl-4b-heretic/), excerpt; [arXiv 2512.13655](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.13655), excerpt). That confirms C6: abliteration "slightly increases errors; it does not improve reasoning" ([DOC L372](../docs/ATLAS_FRAMEWORK_REVIEW.md#L372)).

The precision option the 192 GB machine newly affords is an abliterated **Qwen3.5-122B-A10B at Q6 or Q8**: huihui's own GGUF card points to a Q4_K file and calls the method "proof-of-concept", but other uploaders list abliterations of the same base at Q6_K 100.4 GB and Q8_0 121 to 130 GB ([huihui 122B](https://huggingface.co/huihui-ai/Huihui-Qwen3.5-122B-A10B-abliterated-GGUF), excerpt; [mradermacher 122B](https://huggingface.co/mradermacher/Qwen3.5-122B-A10B-abliterated-GGUF), excerpt; [wangzhang 122B](https://huggingface.co/wangzhang/Qwen3.5-122B-A10B-abliterated-GGUF), excerpt), which would give Arthur's explicit `[ARTHUR:UNCENSORED]` prefix an engine at release precision instead of a re-quantised MXFP4 file. Above that class the picture is Q1 to Q4 only: huihui's DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731 abliteration added a Q4_K on 3 August 2026 with the note that "the Q4 quant used a stronger ablation intensity than Q2", its Vision-Exp sibling ablated layers 11 to 28 with "all expert modules ... not ablated", and a Heretic issue records the unresolved claim that DeepSeek V4's manifold-constrained hyper-connections "break abliteration", closed only because Heretic switched its default method ([huihui V4-Flash-0731](https://huggingface.co/huihui-ai/Huihui-DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731-abliterated-GGUF), excerpt; [huihui on X](https://x.com/support_huihui/status/2097951984078995843), excerpt; [p-e-w/heretic#310](https://github.com/p-e-w/heretic/issues/310)). Nemotron 3 Super has no working abliteration at all: the one "uncensored" GGUF found "refuses in identical circumstances to base Nemotron model" per its own discussion tab, and Heretic states that "pure state-space models and certain other research architectures are not yet supported" ([timteh673 discussions](https://huggingface.co/timteh673/Nemotron-3-Super-120B-A12B-Uncensored-GGUF/discussions), excerpt; [Heretic README](https://github.com/p-e-w/heretic)). For the two Qwen 3.8 members there are already a huihui Qwen3.8-27B abliteration (layers 18 to 51 only) and a Heretic-2 Qwen3.8-Flash-Next GGUF benchmarked "on a Ryzen AI Max+ 395 with 128 GB of unified memory, using Vulkan on llama-server" ([huihui Qwen3.8-27B](https://huggingface.co/huihui-ai/Huihui-Qwen3.8-27B-abliterated), excerpt; [cygnal Flash-Next Heretic2](https://huggingface.co/cygnal/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-Heretic2-IQ4XS-NGQ4-GGUF), excerpt). Producing an abliteration on the node itself is not realistic for the 120B-plus class: Heretic's memory needs are "exactly the same as those for running the model with Transformers" in BF16, gpt-oss-120b needed 256 GB of system RAM merely to load, bitsandbytes 4-bit "still does not even support MoE", and the only ROCm attempt on gpt-oss failed in the MXFP4 Triton kernels ([p-e-w/heretic#89](https://github.com/p-e-w/heretic/issues/89); [p-e-w/heretic#8](https://github.com/p-e-w/heretic/issues/8); [p-e-w/heretic#453](https://github.com/p-e-w/heretic/issues/453); [p-e-w/heretic#274](https://github.com/p-e-w/heretic/issues/274)). Two governance points survive unchanged: every abliterated output still passes the approval gate (R13), and producers measure "success" as compliance with advbench-class prompts, so a working abliterated engine complies with that class by design ([DOC L1052](../docs/ATLAS_FRAMEWORK_REVIEW.md#L1052); [config.default.toml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/p-e-w/heretic/master/config.default.toml)).

## What a follow-up must close before any change is proposed

The research reached GitHub and the repository in full but almost nothing else, so the gaps are concrete and mostly checkable from a Hugging Face card or a single node run. On **support**, the pinned commit b29c606's date must be confirmed against the 14 September 2026 version bump, because the two notes disagree and the answer decides whether Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, Qwen3-TTS, Nemotron MTP and Nemotron Puzzle are inside the pin ([L01 L27-L29](../scripts/day1/phase2/01-llama.sh#L27); [PR list](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pulls?q=is%3Apr+MiMo+OR+Kimi+OR+KDA)); GLM-5.3-Flash, MiMo-V2.6 and MiniCPM-V 4.7 are definitely outside it. On **sizes and licences**, no GGUF file size was read from a repository for Qwen3.5-122B Q8_0, Nemotron 3 Super Q8_0, Qwen3-Coder-Next, Baichuan-M3, Gemma 4 31B Q8_0 or any Qwen3.8-Flash-Next rung above IQ4_XS, and the licences of Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, Nemotron Puzzle, Gemma 4 (Apache 2.0 only per secondary sources), Qwen3-Coder-Next and GLM Legal are unverified ([PR #27742](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/27742); [xigh/open-weight-models](https://github.com/xigh/open-weight-models)). On **quality**, no independent score exists on a current index for Qwen3.5-122B-A10B, Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, GLM-5.3-Flash, Step-3.7-Flash, Nemotron 3 Super or Baichuan-M3; Artificial Analysis index versions are explicitly not comparable (gpt-oss-120b 58 at launch, 12 on v4.3.2), Terminal-Bench 2.0 through 4.0 are mixed in the same tables, and every 2026 open-model coding number is vendor-run on a vendor scaffold ([AA gpt-oss-120b](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/gpt-oss-120b), excerpt; [digitalapplied TB guide](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/swe-bench-terminal-bench-benchmark-guide-2026), excerpt). On **the node itself**, Phase 3's V10 should record DeepSeek V4 Flash decode at both UD-Q4_K_XL and UD-Q8_K_XL with a q8_0 cache (the 25 to 32 tok/s band has no measurement behind it), the real KV footprints the `kv-estimates` to-do already promises, Qwen3.5-122B's document quality with the mmproj and without DeepStack, a side-by-side of Qwen3.8-27B and Gemma 4 31B on the Principal's own scans and drawings, GLM-OCR throughput, Qwen3-ASR word-error rate on the Principal's audio, and Chatterbox Turbo and the CTranslate2 ROCm wheel on the ROCm 10.0 image, none of which any external source has done on gfx1151 ([DOC L948](../docs/ATLAS_FRAMEWORK_REVIEW.md#L948); [huppiflupp](https://github.com/huppiflupp/strix-halo-llm-speeds)). A node-side verification protocol is already supported by the tooling: record BF16 (or the native Q8_K_XL) logits once with `llama-perplexity --kl-divergence-base` on Wikitext-2 plus a domain corpus, score each candidate file and KV type with `--kl-divergence`, and count changed answers on a small deterministic task set, as the llama.cpp maintainers did for their own KV decisions ([llama.cpp perplexity README](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/tools/perplexity/README.md); [discussion #23470](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/23470)). Finally, no community source has qualified a 192 GB GTT and TTM profile; the node's `amdgpu.gttsize=196608` line is extrapolated from 128 GB values and validated only by V3 ([DOC L135](../docs/ATLAS_FRAMEWORK_REVIEW.md#L135); [strix-halo-guide](https://github.com/hogeheer499-commits/strix-halo-guide/blob/main/STRIX_HALO_LOCAL_LLM_SETUP.md)).

## Conclusion

The move from 128 GB and Windows to 192 GB and Linux did not open the door to a new class of flagship; the 2026 open frontier is 0.5 to 3 trillion parameters and sits two to three bits of precision beyond this node, and the Principal's own rule, quality first and less quantisation, is exactly what keeps those models out. What the memory did buy is already in the plan at Q8_0 for Nemotron and Qwen3.5, and the research shows two places where the same principle still has slack: a bit-exact DeepSeek file that costs 7 GB, and a vision partner where 79 GB of dense 2025 weights at 3 tok/s can be replaced by 29 GB of natively multimodal 2026 weights that leave room for everything else. The pattern across every discipline is that scale beats specialisation except where a large lab has post-trained a frontier base for the domain (medicine, and possibly law), and that the biggest measured gains at the edges of the roster, OCR, retrieval, speech, come from models under 10 GB that nobody benchmarked against the 72B and 70B engines when the plan was drawn.

The two Qwen 3.8 members are the only genuinely new candidates for the LLM seats, and they enter in different ways: the 27B as a dense, Apache-licensed precision engine that opens a coder seat and a light vision tier without disturbing the budget, and Flash-Next as a challenger to the Corporate default whose architecture, licence and quantisation behaviour are all still being written down in llama.cpp pull requests. The deciding evidence for both, and for the Apex file change, has to come from the node, because the proxy denied the research every model card and leaderboard that could have supplied it; the follow-up list above is short enough to run inside Phase 3's own measurement gates, and until it runs, the current roster is the right one to build.
